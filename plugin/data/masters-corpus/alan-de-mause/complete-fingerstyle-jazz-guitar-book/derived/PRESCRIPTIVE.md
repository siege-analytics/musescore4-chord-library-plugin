# Prescriptive Lessons: Alan de Mause -- Complete Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Book

## maj7

Carter treats maj7 as the I and IV members of the diatonic seventh-chord set. Open-position voicing is prescribed as the practical default because close-position sevenths exceed most players' finger span. Voice-leading into the next chord should prioritize chromatic bass motion over root-position defaults.

- **tonic-diatonic-seventh**: Maj7 chords are I and IV of the diatonic set ('Two major sevenths I and IV'), voiced open with the third raised an octave. Bass note should continue chromatic movement rather than defaulting to root. (Ch. 9 pp. 117-120; Ch. 10 p. 120)
- **tonic-open-voicing**: Close-position seventh chords redirected to open voicing -- 'Unless you have long spidery fingers, you are probably more comfortable with these chords in either of the following standard alternate configurations.' (Ch. 9 p. 117)

## min7

Minor sevenths are the most numerous diatonic chord type (II, III, VI). Carter emphasizes independent voice motion in cycle-of-fifths practice and treats the II chord as the harmonic on-ramp in the II-V7-I progression.

- **diatonic-minor-seventh-ii-iii-vi**: Three minor sevenths in a major key. Voice with independent motion -- 'think of harmonic movement as several moving voices rather than a glob of dots on a chord box grid.' (Ch. 9 p. 117; Ch. 13 p. 156)
- **ii-chord-in-ii-v7-i**: The II-V7-I is 'the most common jazz progression unit.' The min7 II degree is the harmonic on-ramp before V7 -- 'putting a II of the upcoming new key in front of the new key V7.' Bass-first conception prescribed. (Ch. 14 p. 166; Ch. 11 p. 141)
- **harmonization-sixth-implies-extension**: Sixth-below harmonization may imply unwanted m7 extension. Optional adjustment: 'You can change the C to D if you wish to keep the sound to a simple Dm chord.' (Ch. 8 p. 104)

## min7b5

Carter identifies min7b5 as the VII chord of the major scale -- a diatonic upper-structure chord rather than a chromatic passing device.

- **half-diminished-vii-diatonic**: 'One half-diminished seventh VII.' For ascending chromatic root movement, Carter prescribes dim7 rather than min7b5 as the connector. (Ch. 9 p. 117; Ch. 10 p. 120)

## dom7

The dominant seventh receives the most extensive treatment -- resolution, turnarounds, introductions, modulation, ensemble voicing, walking bass, and comping register.

- **v7-dominant-resolution**: V7-I is 'the most basic harmonic movement of jazz.' Intro must end on V7. Voice-leading uses contrary motion with tritone pull. (Ch. 13 p. 156; Ch. 14 p. 160)
- **tag-initiation-substitute-for-tonic**: V7 replaces final I chord when initiating a tag -- 'required.' (Ch. 14 p. 164)
- **turnaround-gateway**: V7 closes every turnaround, which is 'practically another intro.' (Ch. 14 p. 164)
- **turnaround-singer-restraint** [PROSCRIPTIVE]: Do not elaborate turnaround when singer holds a note -- 'don't kick out the jams.' (Ch. 14 p. 164)
- **dominant-seventh-rootless-ensemble-voicing**: Rootless close voicing with alternating fourths and tritones -- bassist covers roots and fifths. Ensemble use only. (Ch. 10 p. 129)
- **dominant-seventh-cycle-voice-leading**: Analyze dom7-to-dom7 motion voice by voice, not as block changes. Each voice moves by half-step or small interval. (Ch. 13 p. 156)
- **instant-modulation-borrowed-v7**: Borrow V7 from destination key -- achievable in two beats minimum. One beat arrives too early. (Ch. 14 p. 165)
- **spread-texture-bass-alternation**: Root bass notes alternate up a fourth then down a fifth. Each new bass note is a tenth from melody above. (Ch. 13 p. 157)
- **altered-fifth-bass-derivation**: Major-scale shortcut does not work for #5 or b5 chords. Read alteration from chord name directly. (Ch. 5 p. 77)
- **walking-bass-chromatic-approach**: Chromatic approach notes define jazz walking bass. Bass-first conception mandatory -- 'Think bass-plus-chord, not chord-plus-bass.' (Ch. 10 p. 124; Ch. 11 p. 141)
- **intro-whole-tone-color**: Whole-tone scale color available for dom7 in intros -- 'a little sprinkle of whole-tone scale thrown in at the C+7.' (Ch. 14 p. 160)
- **back-cycling-modulation**: Back-cycle through cycle of fifths for systematic modulation when direct V7 borrowing is insufficient. (Ch. 14 p. 166)
- **tritone-accompaniment-sparse-texture**: Tritone pairs as primary harmonic accompaniment for sparse, ensemble-friendly arrangements -- '3 on 4' style. (Ch. 15 p. 179)
- **moving-tenths-independent-rhythm**: Tenths under dom7 must have independent rhythmic motion from the melody. (Ch. 7 pp. 98-99)
- **parallel-tenths-proscription** [PROSCRIPTIVE]: Do not use parallel tenths throughout -- melody would match bass rhythm. (Ch. 7 p. 98)
- **comping-register-high-strings-electric-bass**: Voice comping on top four or five strings with electric bass to avoid 'muddy tangle of sounds.' (Ch. 11 p. 135)

## aug7

Carter's altered-dominant bass rule applies: raise the fifth a half step from the chord name, not from the major scale.

- **altered-dominant-bass-fifth**: 'If the chord is C7#5, raise the fifth a half step.' Plain major-scale fifth derivation forbidden. (Ch. 5 p. 77)

## dim7

Diminished seventh serves as the chromatic connector between chords with ascending root motion -- 'the hamburger helper of ascending harmonic movement.'

- **ascending-chromatic-connector**: Standard connector for ascending chromatic root movement. (Ch. 10 p. 120)
- **half-step-approach-chord**: Chromatic approach from half step above or below is 'another accepted way of moving the harmony.' (Ch. 15 p. 167)

## maj (triads)

Major triads receive extensive treatment for bass voicing, harmonization techniques (thirds, sixths, tenths), arpeggiation rules, and arrangement texture.

- **triad-bass-third-supply**: When melody contains the third, bass plays root or fifth. When melody is other than a third, bass supplies the third to identify the chord. (Ch. 5 p. 83)
- **harmonization-thirds-below-free-stroke**: Free stroke mandatory for third-below harmonization because thirds fall on adjacent strings. Project top melody note by tugging it more forcefully. (Ch. 6 p. 91)
- **harmonization-sixths-key-note-on-top**: Key note must stay on top in sixth-below harmonization -- bottom position causes ear to perceive minor key. Non-negotiable. (Ch. 8 p. 108)
- **oom-pah-root-fifth-bass**: Root/fifth alternation is foundational bass pattern. Mixed-rhythm bass (quarter, half, whole) de-emphasizes the oom-pah. (Ch. 5 pp. 76-78)
- **moving-tenths-stride-device**: Moving tenths are the signature stride-inspired device -- 'Fats Waller, Art Tatum, Teddy Wilson made this the style.' Aim for at least half notes. (Ch. 10 p. 127; Ch. 7 p. 98)
- **tonic-ending-resolution**: Three standard endings: V7-I, IV-I, and II-V7-I. IV-I is soul/gospel. (Ch. 14 p. 163)
- **deceptive-cadence-bvi-substitution**: bVI chord insertion as deceptive cadence 'opens up several possibilities.' (Ch. 15 p. 167)
- **tonic-sparse-two-note-voicing**: Two inner-voice notes 'often all you need to make an arrangement sound full.' (Ch. 10 pp. 125-126)
- **walkdown-structural-device**: Descending bass line with common tone on top -- 'the device that keeps popping up.' (Ch. 15 p. 181)
- **arpeggiation-texture-melodic-ceiling**: Arpeggio must not reach higher than the melody. (Ch. 6 pp. 87-89)
- **arpeggiation-abandonment-leaping-melody** [PROSCRIPTIVE]: Abandon arpeggiation when melody 'leaps up and down wildly.' (Ch. 6 p. 90)
- **three-part-texture-bass-plus-thirds**: Thirds alone insufficient for full arrangement -- 'Put the bass back in.' (Ch. 6 p. 92)
- **thirds-only-no-sustained-arrangement-proscription** [PROSCRIPTIVE]: Do not rely on thirds harmonization for entire arrangement. (Ch. 6 p. 92)
- **non-chord-tone-thirds-ear-test**: Non-chord tones in thirds are 'interesting spice' -- 'It's your ear and your decision.' (Ch. 6 p. 92)
- **comping-density-holes-vs-busy**: 'Play more when the soloist is not playing, and play less when he is.' (Ch. 11 p. 135)
- **avoid-constant-bass-doubling-modern-style** [PROSCRIPTIVE]: Avoid constant doubled bass notes unless recreating vintage jazz. (Ch. 11 p. 141)

## min (triads)

Minor triads follow the same bass and harmonization rules as major, with the minor third as the identity-establishing tone.

- **minor-triad-third-supply**: Same third-supply rule as major -- when melody lacks minor third, bass supplies it. (Ch. 5 pp. 79-83)
- **harmonization-sixths-key-note-on-top**: Key-note-on-top rule applies equally. Diatonic sixths may imply extensions; adjustment permitted. (Ch. 8 pp. 104-108)
- **oom-pah-root-fifth-bass**: Root/fifth applies identically. Altered fifths read from chord symbol. (Ch. 5 pp. 76-77)
