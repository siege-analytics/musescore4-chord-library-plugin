# Prescriptive lessons — barry-harris × jazz-workshop

## dom7

Harris treats the dominant 7th chord as the entry point into a four-chord family organized by a single diminished triad built a major 3rd above the dominant root. Substitution within this family is structural, not ornamental: the bass retains the original dominant root while upper voices shift freely among the related dominants. Practice vocabulary is built from exactly three arpeggio positions (tonic, 5th, 7th) plus scale-degree-specific chromatic half-steps in descending lines.

- [ch.1 p.27] Harris assigns every dom7 chord to the major 6 diminished scale family. The locating rule is fixed: build the related diminished chord a major 3rd above the dominant root to identify which family the chord belongs to.
- [ch.4 p.83] The four dominant 7ths sharing a diminished triad are interchangeable at V7–I. D7–Gmaj7 illustrates: F7, Ab7, and B7 (all related to F# diminished) make valid, colorful substitutions for D7.
- [ch.4 p.83] When substituting related dominants, keep the original dominant root in the bass. F7, Ab7, and B7 are voiced in the upper register against D in the bass, preserving bass-line identity while varying harmonic color above.
- [ch.1 p.13] For the dominant 7th scale, Harris limits arpeggio study to exactly three positions: tonic, 5th degree, and 7th degree. No other positions are prioritized.
- [ch.1 p.17] The dominant 7th scale has its own scale-degree-specific rules for inserting chromatic half-steps in descending phrases. These half-steps are the defining feature of bebop vocabulary.
- [ch.5 p.93] In minor-key contexts, the related diminished chord selection is both key- and chord-specific: C7 in F minor takes E-diminished; G7 in C minor takes B-diminished.

## maj6

The major 6th chord is the foundational identity of Harris's first universal chording scale. Every major-function voicing in the book is drawn from this one scale, whose internal architecture is three interlocking diminished chords. Voice leading within the family follows a single directive: move each note to the next note of the scale.

- [ch.3 p.66] Harris assigns the major 6th chord as the primary identity of the major 6 diminished scale family — the source scale for all major-function voicings throughout the book.
- [ch.3 p.67] The C6 diminished scale contains three interlocking diminished chords: C and A belong to one diminished, E and G to a second, and B-diminished provides the remaining four notes.
- [ch.3 p.71] The voice-leading rule for all maj6-family motion: move each note to the next note of the major 6 diminished scale.

## min7

Harris dissolves the min7 chord as an independent category: any m7 chord is reconceived as an inversion of a major 6th chord, collapsing it into the major 6 diminished scale family. One specific min7 — the chord on the 5th degree of a dominant 7th scale — earns the special designation "Important Minor."

- [ch.3 p.69] Every minor 7th chord is an inversion of a major 6th chord. Stop thinking of m7 as its own category and instead identify the corresponding major 6th chord, unlocking all major-6-diminished voicings.
- [ch.3 p.69–71] Because the m7 is an inversion of a major 6th chord, all voicings are drawn from the major 6 diminished scale of the corresponding major 6th. Standard movable-chord voice-leading rules apply directly.
- [ch.2 p.46] Harris names the minor 7th chord on the 5th degree of a dominant 7th scale the "Important Minor" (5 of 5) — a reusable harmonic tool in progressions such as Rhythm Changes.

## min7b5

Harris collapses the half-diminished chord into his second universal family. Every m7b5 is an inversion of a minor 6th chord, making the entire minor 6 diminished scale available for half-diminished contexts.

- [ch.3 p.69] Every m7b5 (half-diminished) chord is an inversion of a minor 6th chord. Identify the corresponding minor 6th chord and draw all voicings from its minor 6 diminished scale.
- [ch.3 p.69–71] All m7b5 voicings are sourced from the minor 6 diminished scale of the corresponding minor 6th chord. The governing rule is identical: move each note to the next note of the minor 6 diminished scale.

## min6

The minor 6th chord anchors Harris's second universal chording scale and appears at two distinct structural levels: as the foundation of the minor 6 diminished scale, and as the cadential arrival point of the named major–minor–minor/6 voice-leading formula.

- [ch.3 p.66] The minor 6th chord is the foundational identity of the minor 6 diminished scale family — the second of two universal chording scales covering all harmony in the book.
- [ch.2 p.58] The minor 6 diminished scale is constructed by combining a minor 6 chord with its related diminished chord (built a major 3rd above the dominant note of the scale).
- [ch.4 p.79] Harris names a transportable three-chord voice-leading unit: major → minor → minor-with-6th-in-bass (e.g., Bb major → G minor → Gm/E). The min6 inversion is the cadential arrival point of this formula.

## dim7

The diminished 7th chord is the organizational hub of Harris's entire harmonic system. It locates four interchangeable dominant 7ths, constitutes the internal stepping-stone architecture of the diminished scales, and is key-specifically assigned over dominant chords in minor-key contexts.

- [ch.1 p.27 / ch.2 p.42] The related diminished chord is located by going up a major 3rd from the root of the dominant 7th chord. This single rule unlocks the four-chord interchangeable-dominant family in any key.
- [ch.4 p.83] Each diminished chord generates exactly four related dominant 7ths, all interchangeable in cadential contexts. F# diminished yields D7, F7, Ab7, and B7.
- [ch.3 p.67] Within the C6 diminished scale, three interlocking diminished chords form the scale's internal architecture and serve as symmetric stepping-stones for moving voicings up or down the scale.
- [ch.5 p.93] In minor keys, the related diminished chord for each dominant is key-specific: C7 in F minor uses E-diminished; G7 in C minor uses B-diminished.

## aug

Harris introduces the augmented arpeggio as a technical prerequisite in the vocalist's training sequence, defined structurally as a major arpeggio with the 5th raised a half-step.

- [ch.5 p.95] The augmented arpeggio is defined as a major arpeggio with the 5th degree raised a half-step (e.g., Ab augmented). It appears in the vocalist's warm-up series as a required technical skill.
