# Chapter 1 Summary

Barry Harris introduces the foundational principle that **technique must always serve musical context**, encapsulated in his dictum "practice your playing—not your practicing," which establishes the pedagogical frame for all subsequent exercises. The chapter teaches essential building blocks including the **'up and down' scale** (2-bar phrases from tonic to 7th and back), the **three important arpeggios on dominant 7th** (on tonic, 5th, and 7th degrees), and the signature **pivoting technique** (a multi-purpose tool that creates rhythmic strength through accentual shifts). Harris's characteristic pedagogical move is the **half-step practice model**, a generative system rooted in bebop language that applies scale-degree-specific rules across dominant 7th, major, and minor (melodic) scales to create endless improvisational possibilities. The chapter establishes critical terminology including major scale half-step rules, minor scale half-step rules (using ascending melodic minor with one exception for the b3rd), and the practical rule of thumb for finding related diminished chords (build a major 3rd above the dominant note). Throughout, Harris emphasizes re-creating the rhythm section mentally during solo practice.
