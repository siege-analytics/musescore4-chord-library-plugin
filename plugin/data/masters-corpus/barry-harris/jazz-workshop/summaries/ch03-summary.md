# Chapter 3: Movable Chords for Piano and Guitar

This chapter introduces the **scale for chording** concept, which assigns virtually all chords to one of two foundational scales—the major 6 diminished and minor 6 diminished—enabling systematic voicing movement across a progression. Harris addresses the **harmonic rigidity misconception** that chords are fixed vertical points, diagnosing why players repeat the same two or three voicings for each change, and offers movable chords as the antidote. The method rests on two axiomatic equivalences: every **m7** chord is an inversion of a major 6th, and every **m7b5** is an inversion of a minor 6th, collapsing seemingly different chord families into a unified harmonic vocabulary. The **C6 diminished scale construction** reveals internal architecture—eight notes comprised of three interlocking diminished chords—that becomes the organizational blueprint for voicing choices. The mechanical engine driving all movement is Harris's characteristic directive: **remember to move each note to the next note of the scale**, transforming abstract harmonic theory into a tactile, scale-based sequencing rule that piano and guitar players can execute immediately.
