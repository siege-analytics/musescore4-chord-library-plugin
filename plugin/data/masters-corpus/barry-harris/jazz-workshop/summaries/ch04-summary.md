# Chapter 4: The Rhythm Section

Chapter 4 teaches the harmonic substitution technique of **related dominant 7th chords**, where the V7 chord and its related dominants (derived from the same diminished triad) can substitute for each other when resolving to the I chord, enabling richer voicing options and interchangeability in cadential movements. Harris illustrates this through the concrete **D7–Gmaj7 substitution example**, where F#-diminished generates four related dominants (D7, F7, Ab7, B7) that can all be voiced against D in the bass—a signature Harris technique for maintaining bass continuity while shifting harmonic color. The chapter introduces the **major-minor-minor/6 voice-leading move** (e.g., Bb major–G minor–Gm/E), a named harmonic unit with a specific bass-line formula that becomes reusable across transpositions. Harris generalizes the *Stella by Starlight* progression into a transportable Roman-numeral formula (I major–III7–relative minor–♭VIdim–Imaj/5–minor/6), exemplifying his method of extracting song-specific observations into universal harmonic rules.
