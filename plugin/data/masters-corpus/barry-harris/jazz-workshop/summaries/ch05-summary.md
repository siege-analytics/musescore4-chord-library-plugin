# Chapter 5: Vocalizing

The chapter teaches vocalizing as a jazz improvisation practice grounded in two principles: the singer must develop **independent time and phrasing** separate from the rhythm section's foundation, and technical mastery of **scalar and rhythmical skills** is prerequisite to expressive freedom. Harris demonstrates specific harmonic applications through *Y.M.B. in S.* figures, establishing key-dependent rules for **E diminished** and **B diminished** chord choices that correspond to dominant-7 resolutions in minor keys. The chapter introduces load-bearing vocabulary including the **augmented arpeggio** (major arpeggio with raised 5th), the **whole tone scale** (six-note construction built entirely on whole-steps), and the foundational concept of the **vocalist's role** as both storyteller and rhythmic instrument. Harris's characteristic pedagogical move appears throughout: presenting technical definitions and harmonic rules as the liberation pathway—mastery of scales and chord structures frees the singer to explore nuance and depth.
