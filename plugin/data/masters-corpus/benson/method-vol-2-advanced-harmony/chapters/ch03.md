## Turnaround progression definition

> The turnaround progression is a II / V / I with an added chord: the VIth degree, which can be
>      minor or dominant 7th depending on the harmonic situation. We have two different turnaround
>      forms: - II / V / I / VI or I / VI / II / V. Basically we have major and minor turnaround
>      progressions, however, the minor progression has 2 different options for the VIth degree, which
>      are the VI7 or the bVI7.

**Significance (p. 63).** This is the foundational definition of the chapter's central concept, establishing both structural forms and the minor-specific variant options.

## Minor II/V/I melodic minor dual membership

> The minor II / V / I cadence is unique. It has two chords that can belong to the melodic minor
>      family. Here is a Cm cadence as an example: Dø / G7 / Cm7. If we consider Dø chord as being
>      locrian major 2nd we will have the VIth degree of F melodic minor, and if we consider the G7
>      chord as being altered, we will have a VIIth degree of Ab melodic minor. Therefore, we
>      already have many substitutions to apply even without changing the cadence order:
>      subdominant, dominant, root. However we can do the same substitutions we did for the major
>      II/V7/I, replacing the dominant chord with the subdominant melodic minor chord family.

**Significance (p. 47).** This passage defines the theoretical engine behind all the minor-cadence substitutions in the chapter, explaining why melodic minor chords from two different keys can serve as substitutes in a single minor II/V/I.

## All-dominant turnaround in Brazilian/blues music

> This is a very common sequence in Brazilian music and blues music, where all the chords of the turnaround
>      become dominant with different voicings. Each dominant chord is leading to the next chord untill it finally
>      leads to the tonic.

**Significance (p. 69).** This provides a genre-context rule explaining when and why all chords in a turnaround may be made dominant, connecting the substitution technique to real-world musical styles.
