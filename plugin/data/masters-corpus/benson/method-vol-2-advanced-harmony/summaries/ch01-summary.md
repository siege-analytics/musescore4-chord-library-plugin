# Chapter 1 — Harmony Knowledge and Rules

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch01.md`._

Chapter 1 establishes the foundational harmony framework through three harmonic regions—Root (tonic: Cmaj7, Em7, Am7), Subdominant (IV and IIm chords with major and minor variants), and Dominant (V7, VII°)—each with distinct functions (rest, distancing, and tension respectively) that generate cadences as the core organizing principle for chord progressions. The chapter introduces load-bearing terminology including harmonic regions, cadences, voicing tensions, secondary dominants, tritone substitution, and lydian dominant, alongside exhaustive voicing palettes for each chord quality and rules governing their deployment: same-region chords are interchangeable substitutes, altered tensions (b9, #9, #11, b13) characterize minor V7 chords, and the melody always determines available harmonic choices during comping, while solo improvisation permits greater freedom. Extended frameworks cover minor keys, the II/V/I cadence structure in both major and minor, and the mechanical rule for tritone substitution (swap root for its tritone while adjusting voicings to maintain sonic coherence). Throughout, the author stresses a guitar-specific approach: every concept is presented from both 5th-string and 6th-string root positions, ensuring comprehensive fretboard knowledge.
