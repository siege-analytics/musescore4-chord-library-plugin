# Chapter 2 — II / V / I Chord Substitution Possibilities

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 teaches substitution techniques within the II/V/I cadence framework by establishing foundational symmetry principles—particularly that diminished chords repeat every minor third, enabling four equivalent options—and introducing two functional categories: Fake Diminished (inverted dom7(b9)) and Hybrid Diminished chords that substitute for V7. The chapter defines five cadence types (Perfect Authentic, Imperfect Authentic, Plagal, Deceptive, and Half-Cadence) governed by the rule that chord tensions do not alter cadential function, then expands harmonic possibility through Modal Interchange, which borrows chords from parallel modes (any modes sharing the same tonic) to create re-harmonization colors while respecting three harmonic regions: Root (I, III, VI), Dominant (V, VII), and Subdominant (II, IV). The master's characteristic move is the systematic translation of functional voice-leading logic—particularly the leading-tone relationship in VII7 substitution and the bass pedal point technique—into practical substitution rulesets by scale degree, with the discipline to recognize edge cases (such as prohibiting the V degree of melodic minor when it shares the root of the tonic). Key load-bearing terms include: diminished symmetry, Fake/Hybrid Diminished categories, tonal vs. modal vs. modal-tonal harmony, modal interchange, parallel modes, harmonic regions, and tritone substitution.
