# Chapter 3 — Turnaround Progressions

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch03.md`._

Chapter 3 teaches turnaround progressions, a II/V/I cadence with an added VI degree that creates two structural forms: II/V/I/VI or I/VI/II/V, with major and minor variants distinguished by whether the VI chord is minor, dominant 7th, or the flattened bVI7. The chapter's theoretical engine centers on melodic minor dual membership—the observation that in minor II/V/I progressions like D∅/G7/Cm7, the D∅ functions as the VIth degree of F melodic minor and G7 as the VIIth degree of Ab melodic minor, enabling rich substitution possibilities while preserving the cadential function. A characteristic move visible throughout is the all-dominant turnaround, common in Brazilian and blues music, where every chord in the progression becomes dominant with different voicings, each leading to the next until resolving to the tonic. The load-bearing terminology introduced includes the turnaround progression itself, the concept of melodic minor dual membership, and the distinction between major and minor turnaround forms.
