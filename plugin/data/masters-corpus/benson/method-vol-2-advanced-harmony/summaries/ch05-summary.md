# Chapter 5 — Open String Chords

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch05.md`._

Chapter 5 teaches open string chords—foundational shapes used by beginners that become sophisticated tools for advanced players—by establishing two categorical types: those locked to specific keys and voicings, and those transposable across frets while retaining the same shape. The core technique involves choosing a chord shape with at least one open string, then systematically applying it across different frets (most commonly the 1st and 2nd strings) to discover viable voicings, while understanding that chord quality can shift dramatically when moved (major to minor). The chapter introduces the concept of pedal point (a sustained bass or upper note, or inverted pedal between voices), a baroque-era tension-building device adapted for guitar using open strings on the 6th, 5th, or Drop D tunings, and organizes pedal-point chords into three string-based groups for practical application in short harmonic sequences. Key techniques include omitting notes for hand-size accommodation and recognizing that doubled notes within these voicings can create desirable effects in arpeggios. The foundational vocabulary of common open string chords must be memorized before advancing, and the method prescribes a two-step process of choosing shapes and testing them across the neck to internalize which voicing options sound best.
