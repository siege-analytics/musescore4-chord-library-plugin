# Chapter 6 — Quartal Harmony

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch06.md`._

Chapter 6 teaches quartal harmony, a chord-voicing system built on stacked perfect fourths (and augmented/diminished fourths) that produces harmonically ambiguous, 'hybrid' chords distinct from tertian (major/minor) harmony. The chapter establishes that quartal chords derive structural kinship with pentatonic scales and exhibit modal ambiguity—a single quartal voicing can function across different modes depending on the bass note, enabling the core technique of Modal Quartal Chord Voicings where bass note selection determines modal identity. Key terminology introduced includes quartal harmony itself, modal ambiguity, pentatonic quartal voicings (built from major and relative minor pentatonics), and the two main architectural concepts: melodic pedal point (fixed top voice with shifting lower voices) and polymodal quartal voicings (single harmonic structures adaptable across modes). The chapter emphasizes practical guitar application through register rules (favoring 3rd–5th string voicings over 6th-string roots), fretboard visualization as the learning goal rather than immediate performance use, and the technique's primary power in static-harmony compositions like 'So What' and 'A Love Supreme'—with historical grounding in McCoy Tyner, Bill Evans, and Miles Davis's quartal explorations. The method concludes with a practice protocol: study one key thoroughly before transposing, and selectively memorize voicing colors rather than exhaustive cataloguing.
