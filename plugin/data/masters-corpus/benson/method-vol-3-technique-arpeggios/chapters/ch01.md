## both hands must work together

> For many years, guitar players all over the world have been trying to understand the George
>     Benson right hand picking technique. However, different from what people may think and
>     unfotunately have been teaching incorrectly, both hands must work together at all times,
>     otherwise nothing is going to happen.

**Significance (p. 1).** Foundational premise: corrects the widespread misconception that the technique reduces to right-hand position alone.

## angled pick — no fixed rule

> Of course the angled pick is one of the most
>     outstanding features of this technique, but let's understand that although it is important, there is
>     no rule about how much the pick should be angled.

**Significance (p. 1).** Defines the role of the angled pick while refusing a fixed angle, preventing posture fixation.

## left-right hand connection is the secret

> The most important thing is to remember that there is no George Benson right hand technique
>     without the left hand technique (fingering). The secret is the connection between both hands.
>     And yes!.. we are talking about fingering and picking choices.

**Significance (p. 1).** Core thesis: coordinated fingering and picking choices, not right-hand mechanics alone, constitute the technique.

## four picking types taxonomy

> Let’s start splitting the picking exercises into four types:
>
>     1) Sweep Picking
>
>     2) “Bee” Picking
>
>     3) Rest Stroke Picking
>
>     4) Jumping Strings

**Significance (p. 1).** Structural taxonomy that organizes all of Chapter 1's technique exercises.

## correct fingering / digital memory

> 1) Correct Fingering (digital memory)
> Play the exact suggested >ingering for each melodic line or example. They were chosen
> speci>ically to better help you master this technique. Study the left hand >irst, and then
> after you are comfortable with the left hand >ingerings, apply the right hand.

**Significance (p. 2).** Establishes the method's left-hand-first learning sequence and introduces the digital-memory concept.

## three-finger playing preference

> Sometimes, the type of >ingering George Benson uses can look dif>icult because he does not
> use the pinky or 4th >inger very often, playing mostly with three >ingers. But as time goes
> by, it will become easier and we will be able to play much faster and more articulate than
> we had ever been able to play before.

**Significance (p. 2).** Hallmark of Benson's left-hand approach — deliberate avoidance of the fourth finger — shapes every fingering chart that follows.

## consistent fingering rule

> Do not forget that our >ingers have digital memory, which means that they will learn what
> we teach them. If our >ingerings are inconsistent, our >ingers will not learn anything and
> they will always be lost. Now, what type of connection between both hands should we
> expect if one of them is totally lost? Easy answer, none !!!

**Significance (p. 2).** Decisive rule against inconsistent fingering with mechanical reasoning — broken hand synchronization.

## drumstick fingers left-hand exercise

> 2) Drumstick Fingers
> Let’s make our left hand work hard by hitting every note as if the >ingers were drumsticks.

**Significance (p. 2).** Prescribes left-hand isolation drill with rhythmic articulation — a characteristic Benson preparatory exercise.

## staccato left-hand muting technique

> 3) Staccato
> In order to play more rhythmic and precise, let's use a technique called staccato, which
> means to mute all the notes with the left hand. In other words, we hit every note but we do
> not depress the string all the way. Although it may seem very dif>icult to control in the
> beginning, do not worry, the >irst step is to exaggerate the movement, thus teaching our left
> hand >ingers to work together with the right hand picking.

**Significance (p. 2).** Defines staccato as left-hand muting via partial depression; prescribes exaggeration as the first learning step.

## notes per string affects picking direction

> One of the most important things to correct the left hand 3ingering is the number of notes
>     we play per string. The reason why it is so important is very simple: it can completely
>     change the right hand picking, destroying the connection between both hands.

**Significance (p. 3).** Governing principle: string-crossing points determined by note count per string dictate picking strokes.

## string change requires up stroke rule

> A very important device concerning the "Bee" Picking Technique and the "Rest Stroke"
>     Picking Technique, is that depending on the melodic line, the "down and up strokes"
>     should be replaced by "up and down strokes", because everytime we want to change from
>     one string to another, we should play an up stroke.

**Significance (p. 3).** Central picking rule: string changes are preceded by up strokes, organizing all Bee/Rest Stroke picking direction decisions.

## odd notes high-to-low: start up stroke

> 1) Odd notes per string starting from the high and going to the low:
>     In this case we should start with an up stroke independent from the rhythm used.
>     However, be careful because some melodies might not work when rhythmically displaced.

**Significance (p. 3).** Concrete entry-stroke rule for one of the three note-per-string scenarios.

**Significance (p. 4).** Introduces 'double sweep picking' as a named technique and gives its triggering condition.

## hammer-on and pull-off used with care

> Very Important:
>     “Pull off” and "Hammer on” should be used with care, otherwise the articulation of our
>     melodic lines can sound wrong or odd. Therefore, in the begining we should give priority
>     to pick every note we play. However, later on we will understand their importance,
>     especially when playing medium tempos.

**Significance (p. 5).** Prescriptive rule — pick every note initially, reserve legato techniques for later — shapes early-chapter practice.
