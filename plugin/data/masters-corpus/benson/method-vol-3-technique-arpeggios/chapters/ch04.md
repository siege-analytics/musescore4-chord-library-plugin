## Blues scale definition

> The blues scale is a minor pentatonic scale with an added note. This note is called a "blue"
> note. Normally we use this scale from the key we are in rather than over an individual
> chord. Because it can be used over all the blues chords at once, this simpliBies playing over
> all the chord changes. Be careful not to rely on this technique. It will make you sound like a
> "lazy" improviser. Your lines will lack variation and will sound stagnant.

**Significance (p. 140).** Definition + use rule + caveat.

## Major vs. minor blues scale relationship

> Because of the connection between the blues scale and the minor pentatonic scale we can
> consider that there is a major blues scale, that actually will be the same scale, but
> starting from the 3rd degree of the original blues scale (now minor blues scale).

**Significance (p. 140).** Major blues derives from 3rd degree of minor blues.

## Combining blues scales for bluesy sound

> However, what creates a bluesy sound is the combination of the minor blues scale with the
> major blues scale in the same key. In other words, in the key of "C" for example, we would
> have"C" minor blues scale working together with the "C" major blues scale.
> Note: Blues scales played in the same key work only for major tonalities and not for minor,
> where we use a “C” minor blues scale working together with “Eb” major blues scale.

**Significance (p. 140).** Combination rule; minor-tonality exception.

## Minor blues scale usage rule

> Minor Blues Scale
>   The minor blues scale should be used over the major or minor tonality, summarizing all the
>   chords from the major blues form or minor blues form. However, when mixed with the
>   major blues scalein the same key, we can use it over any dominant 7th chord, bringing a
>   blues vibe to it. (Make sure to play the major 3rd quickly in the phrase, otherwise the
>   melody can sound wrong).

**Significance (p. 141).** Minor blues over dom7 + M3 caveat.

## Major blues scale usage rule

> Major Blues Scale
>   The major blues scale can be played over any individual major chord, including the major
>   7th and dominant 7th chords. However, this scale can sound not bluesy, so the trick is to
>   mix it with the minor blues scale in the same key, reaching a bluesy sound and vibe.

**Significance (p. 141).** Major blues chord scope + mixing rule.

## Major blues scale mixing rule

> When we mix the major blues scale with the minor blues scale, we cannot summarize all
>   the chords into one scale anymore, we must follow the harmony. In other words, the
>   combination of both blues scales will work for each dominant 7th chord individually.
>   Note: This will not work for minor blues form.

**Significance (p. 151).** Mixed scales follow harmony chord-by-chord.
