## Extended blues licks work over any chord

> Next, we have many examples of extended blues licks in the key of “C”. It is important to
> understand that in theory, we could use all of them over any major or minor chord, because
> we are talking about the blues.

**Significance (p. 210).** Establishes the universal applicability of extended blues licks across major and minor chords.

## Pure minor pentatonic over minor family

> However, when the melodic line is built from the minor pentatonic scale without any
> mixture of the major pentatonic scale, our preference is to use the lines over minor chords
> or chord sequences belonging to the "minor chord family" (natural minor harmonic Eield).

**Significance (p. 210).** Preference rule: unmixed minor pentatonic lines belong over the minor chord family.

## Minor pentatonic over major chords

> This is not a rule. Melodic lines using only the minor pentatonic scale will often work
> perfectly over major chords such as C6, Cmaj7 and of course C7.

**Significance (p. 210).** Counterpoint: minor pentatonic works over C6/Cmaj7/C7, so the previous preference isn't absolute.

## Lick chord-context labels

> In the begining of each melodic line we have given some chord possibilities that these lines
> will work over. Remember that blues lines work differently than other types of lines, as
> they can be played in many different harmonic situations.

**Significance (p. 210).** Reading aid: each line's chord-labels are suggestions because blues lines float across many harmonic contexts.

## Singing licks to internalize them

> The secret to mastering when and where to use these melodic lines is quite simple: learn to
> sing every melodic line (lick) that you know. Internalize the sound. If you do this, as time
> goes by things will become easier and you will know exactly when and where to use them.

**Significance (p. 210).** Method's core rule for lick mastery: sing first, internalize the sound, application follows.

## Fingering & picking must be followed exactly

> of the examples.

**Significance (p. 210).** Reinforces the method's core rule that notated fingering and picking are not arbitrary.
