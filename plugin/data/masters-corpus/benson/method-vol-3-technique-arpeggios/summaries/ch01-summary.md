# Chapter 1 — George Benson's Picking Technique

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch01.md`._

Chapter 1 teaches George Benson's picking technique by establishing that coordinated left-hand and right-hand work is the foundational principle—neither hand can succeed in isolation. The chapter introduces a four-picking-types taxonomy (Sweep Picking, Bee Picking, Rest Stroke Picking, Jumping Strings) and emphasizes three characteristic Benson moves: the angled pick (without fixed angle rules), three-finger playing that avoids the fourth finger, and deliberate left-hand isolation drills like drumstick-fingers and staccato muting. Key load-bearing terminology includes digital memory (the fingers' learned muscle patterns), staccato (left-hand muting via partial string depression), and the governing rule that the number of notes per string determines picking direction and necessitates up strokes when changing strings. The method prioritizes consistent fingering to establish hand synchronization, begins with picking every note before introducing legato techniques, and uses exaggeration during early practice to teach fingers to work together.
