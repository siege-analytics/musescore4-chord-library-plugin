# Chapter 2 — Simple Chord Arpeggios

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 teaches chord arpeggio technique—the melodic playing of individual notes from a chord in sequence—with emphasis on simple arpeggios played from root position using the foundational picking mechanics of sweep, rest stroke, and inverted hammer-on (which replaces upstrokes and pulls off similar to a standard pull-off). The chapter systematically organizes all triad and tetrad fingering options into two structural groups based on whether the root note sits on the 5th or 6th string, with three distinct shapes per group, and introduces the transposition principle that the first finger can replace open strings when shifting keys. A central technique-coordination principle runs throughout: notated fingering and picking patterns are deliberate tools for hand coordination rather than arbitrary choices. The guide-root concept frames how inverted arpeggios can be visualized flexibly depending on which string anchors the harmonic foundation, enabling transposition and adaptation across the fretboard.
