# Chapter 5 — Improving your Blues Ideas

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch05.md`._

Chapter 5 teaches blues improvisation through pre-set melodic ideas called licks, introduced via a language-learning analogy and practiced over the boogaloo rhythm. The chapter establishes that licks are contextual—fingering and picking choices depend on surrounding fretboard motion, and blues licks transcend strict harmonic rules, working across major, minor, and dominant chord contexts despite theoretical inconsistencies. Key load-bearing concepts include the 'minor blues substitution tricks' required because minor blues mixes minor and dominant chords, the principle that pure minor pentatonic lines belong over the minor chord family (though not exclusively), and the framework of chord-context labels that suggest harmonic options rather than dictate them. The master's characteristic moves center on pedagogical pragmatism: memorize substitutions first without understanding their theory, sing every lick to internalize its sound for authentic application, and follow notated fingering and picking exactly because hand coordination is inseparable from the lick itself. The chapter prioritizes internalization—sound and muscle memory—as the gateway to knowing when and where licks fit, placing ear and body before theory.
