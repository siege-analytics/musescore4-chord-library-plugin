# Chapter 6 — Natural Major Scale Application

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch06.md`._

Chapter 6 teaches key improvisation—using the Ionian Mode (natural major) and Aeolian Mode (natural minor) to improvise over any chord or sequence within their harmonic fields—with careful attention to avoid notes (the perfect 4th in Ionian and flat 6th in Aeolian) that can clash with melody. The chapter introduces a signature five-zone vertical system for organizing the fretboard, using chord shapes (Cmaj7 and Am7) as anchors to connect zones, based on the principle that "we visualize vertically but play horizontally." Pattern exercises are presented as first-step learning tools only, with explicit caution against relying on them mechanically, since both scales serve as the mother scale and relative scale that summarize all seven modes of the harmonic field. The pedagogy emphasizes horizontal melodic ideas over vertical ones, and acknowledges that avoid-note rules have contextual exceptions depending on the musical situation.
