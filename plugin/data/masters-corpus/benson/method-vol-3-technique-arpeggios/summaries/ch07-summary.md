# Chapter 7 — The Secret of the Two Chords

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch07.md`._

Chapter 7 introduces the "Major 7th and Minor 7th Scale Arpeggios," a signature technique for navigating the avoid note (perfect 4th) that the natural major scale creates over the I and VIm chords (Cmaj7 and Am7); the strategy concentrates on descending arpeggios with added notes transcribed from George Benson solos and organized zone-by-zone across the fretboard. The chapter defines **superimposition**—the harmonic and melodic joining of two or more chords—and explains why guitar requires arpeggiating stacked chords rather than playing them simultaneously like piano. Core load-bearing concepts include **rhythmic displacement** (offsetting intervallic patterns with rhythmic motifs to avoid mechanical predictability) and **zone definition** (ascending arpeggios establish fretboard zones while descending ones span two zones). The pedagogical spine reframes improvisation as language learning: listen to and sing the solos of great artists, then transcend raw transcription by extracting the harmonic concepts beneath individual licks to generate countless new lines. The chapter is integral to the three-volume set, requiring parallel study of volumes 1–2 for rhythmic vocabulary essential to tasteful variation.
