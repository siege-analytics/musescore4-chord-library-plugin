# Chapter 1 — Target Tones & Approach Tones

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch01.md`._

Chapter 1 establishes the foundational concepts of target tones and approach tones, defining target tones as the main notes of a melody—either chord tones or scale tension notes—while explicitly prohibiting avoid notes except when they become part of the chord itself. The chapter introduces three distinct categories of approach tones (Scale Tone, Chromatic, and Enclosure) that organize all subsequent instruction, with Scale Tone Approach broken into four sub-types (simple, double, triple, quadruple) distinguished by ascending or descending direction, and Chromatic Approach further divided into four sub-types (Simple, Double, Triple, Mixed) that vary in possibility depending on chord type and interval distance. Throughout, the master emphasizes careful, tasteful application—particularly warning that chromatic approaches, while historically pervasive, walk a thin line between musicality and non-musical excess. The chapter's characteristic move is taxonomic clarity: each concept receives a precise definition, a complete enumeration of sub-types, and directional or mechanical specification, with the pedagogical note that time signatures in exercises serve visualization rather than rhythmic prescription, directing students to extract melodic ideas and apply them where they sound best.
