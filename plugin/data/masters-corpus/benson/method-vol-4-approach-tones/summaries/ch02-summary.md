# Chapter 2 — Dorian & Mixolydian Single Line Ideas

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 teaches melodic improvisation over II–V cadences by treating Dorian and Mixolydian as a unified vocabulary, a foundational approach shaped by bebop masters like Charlie Parker and Dizzy Gillespie. The core technique is building single lines by superimposing arpeggios and chord shapes (tetrads and triads from the parent major scale) over minor and dominant 7th chords, with the critical caveat that the 4th (avoid note C) must be avoided over standalone G7 but replaced with emphasis on the 3rd (B). A signature move is adding the major 7th to the Dorian scale to function as a leading tone, creating the illusion of dominant resolution; this enables recycling major-7th arpeggio lines from the I and IV chords to generate Dorian vocabulary. The method introduces five fretboard zones and an eight-option taxonomy of arpeggio-connection patterns (Options A–H, combining ascending/descending arpeggios with scale-tone linking), teaching horizontal fretboard thinking through zone connections rather than vertical scale shapes alone. The chapter emphasizes that scales serve only as fretboard guides, not melodies — authentic improvisation emerges from mixing scales, superimposed arpeggios, and chromatic tones, reinforced by two-mode practice (slow visualization followed by musical integration) and rhythmic displacement to disguise mechanical patterns.
