## D7(#11) dual function warning

> D7(#11) is the IV degre from the "A" melodic minor scale
>     chord family. However, this chord also can be considered as a dominant diminished chord
>     voicing possibility. Maybe you are asking yourself: So what? As a matter of a fact, we must
>     know that in some cases we cannot use certain types of scales and chords, otherwise we
>     might have a terrible result.

**Significance (p. 28).** This is a critical rule-of-avoidance: it warns the player that a chord's dual identity (melodic minor IV vs. dominant diminished voicing) constrains which scales and chords can safely be used over it.

## Melodic minor tensions: not all required

> * The chords { D7(9,#11, 13), E7(9, b13) } do not have to contain all the tensions presented.
>     However, we have to know all of them in order to do not make a mistake when harmonizing
>     or improvising.

**Significance (p. 28).** This rule clarifies the difference between voicing practice and theoretical knowledge: not every tension need be voiced, but all must be known to avoid harmonization and improvisation errors.
