# Prescriptive lessons -- benson x method-vol-6-giant-lines

## dom7

Benson's dom7 teaching is built around a single harmonic laboratory -- the D7 chord family -- and is organized by a strict inside/outside discipline with two named substitution techniques (First and Second GB Chord Transitions) and one non-negotiable resolution rule. Outside playing is always a momentary superimposition, never a sustained region: the A7 flash, the B7-to-Em device, and the embedded mini V7-I cadence are all timed moves, not harmonic escapes. The Flux and Reflux concept adds a third axis -- tension built and released within the line itself over a static D7(9) vamp -- making tension management a trainable skill distinct from simple inside/outside alternation.

- [ch.9 p.26] First GB Chord Transition: superimpose small A7 ideas within a single-note line over D7 to create an outside color effect. The substitution is a flash move embedded in the line, not a comped chord change. Cross-ref: outside-duration-ceiling-dom7.
- [ch.9 p.26] Outside duration limit (proscriptive): do not extend the A7 superimposition past the point where outside color reads as color -- exceeding the threshold produces mere dissonance. The outside move is timed, not open-ended. Cross-ref: outside-line-superimposition-dom7.
- [ch.9 p.26] Second GB Chord Transition: embed a mini D7-to-G major V7-I cadence within the single-note line as a second outside device, distinct from A7 superimposition. The relative minor of G (Em) then unlocks additional dominant scale options for the continuing line.
- [ch.9 p.26] Ending Note Rule (universal): every line over D7 -- inside or outside, regardless of how far it travels harmonically -- must conclude on a chord tone of D7. Harmonic coherence is non-negotiable at the line's end even when the body of the line has gone well outside.
- [ch.9 p.31] Mixolydian-family B7 device: because D Mixolydian derives from the Em chord family, B7 ideas resolving to Em are prescribed as a "very beautiful" outside color over D7. Justification is modal-family coherence, not mere tritone substitution logic.
- [ch.13 p.77] Major-7th superimposition for dominant-to-minor resolution: over A7 resolving to Dm7, use major-7th superimposed ideas to strengthen the pull toward the minor tonic. This device is specific to dominant-to-minor cadences.
- [ch.13 p.77] Major-7th superimposition over dominant-to-major resolution (proscriptive): do not apply the same major-7th superimposed ideas over C7 when it resolves to Fmaj7 -- the result may sound too tense or simply wrong in that cadential context. The technique is contraindicated for dominant-to-major resolution.
- [ch.5 and 7 pp.15-37] Flux and Reflux over static D7(9): construct Giant Lines that generate and release harmonic tension within the line over a sustained D7(9) vamp. Chapters 5 and 7 develop this progressively, establishing tension-and-release as a discrete, trainable melodic skill.

## min7

Benson treats min7 in two sequential modes -- static (one-chord vamp) first, then active (changing chords) -- and requires the student to master both inside and outside vocabulary in the static context before moving on. The transition to active contexts is grounded in a concrete fretboard method: guide chord visualization maps the single-note melody onto chord shapes the hand already knows, preventing the active-line concept from floating free of physical execution.

- [ch.11 pp.60-90] Static Dm7 inside and outside Giant Lines: Benson organizes the full melodic vocabulary for Dm7 into explicit inside and outside categories, presenting worked examples of each. The student is expected to practice both categories systematically over the sustained Dm7 vamp before advancing to changing-chord contexts.
- [ch.13 p.77] Active Dm7 guide chord visualization: for Dm7 in changing-chord contexts, map single-note melodic fingerings directly onto guide chord shapes as the concrete fretboard execution method for Active Giant Lines. The student visualizes the underlying chord shape under the fingers while playing the single-note melody, grounding abstract harmonic choices in known hand positions.
