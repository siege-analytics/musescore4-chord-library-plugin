## Overall Arc and Pedagogical Structure

This book by George Benson (as pedagogue) is organized around a single master concept — the **Giant Line** — which it develops progressively across harmonic situations, chord qualities, and melodic contexts. Chapter 1 establishes the foundational premise: chord knowledge is the necessary gateway to harmonic mastery, and melodic fluency follows from understanding harmony rather than preceding it. This chord-to-harmony-to-line sequence is the book's governing pedagogical logic. Subsequent chapters (2–13) apply that logic almost entirely through notated exercises and musical examples rather than discursive prose, meaning the book is primarily a performance-based method: the student learns by playing through systematically organized musical situations rather than by reading explanations.

## The Core Terminology: Giant Lines, Situations, and Flux & Reflux

The book's proprietary vocabulary structures its taxonomy of melodic content. "Giant Lines" are the central object of study — extended single-note melodic constructions grounded in chord knowledge. These lines are organized by two primary categories: **Static Giant Lines** and **Active Giant Lines**. Static Giant Lines appear across multiple "Situations" (Chapters 2, 4, and 8 cover the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd Situations respectively), suggesting a hierarchy of harmonic contexts — likely differing by chord voicing, register, or harmonic complexity — that the student works through in order. Chapters 5 and 7 introduce the concept of **Flux and Reflux**, a term describing melodic motion that creates and releases harmonic tension over a sustained D7(9) chord, deepening the treatment of the Static domain before the book moves on. Chapter 12 labels the Active Giant Lines concept and provides its explanation, with Chapter 13 then applying it to Dm7 harmony, suggesting that Active lines involve motion across changing harmony — in particular, dominant-to-tonic cadential motion.

## The D7 Harmonic Laboratory

The book uses the D7 chord family as its primary harmonic laboratory. Chapters 3, 5, 7, 8, and 9 all center on D7 in its various forms — D7(9) and D7(#9) — making it the foundational chord situation through which the method's core techniques are demonstrated. Chapter 9 is the book's most theoretically explicit chapter: it teaches how to generate **outside effects** over D7 using substitution chords, specifically A7 and B7, and their associated scales. The mechanism given is that B7 ideas derive from the Em chord family, justified through **D Mixolydian mode derivation** — the Mixolydian mode is explained in terms of the chord family it belongs to, providing a theoretical anchor for the substitution. Chapters 10 and 11 then extend the Giant Lines framework into Minor 7th and Major 7th chord territory, with Chapter 11 explicitly offering both "inside" and "outside" ideas for Dm7, indicating the inside/outside distinction is a structural organizing principle throughout.

## Characteristic Moves and the GB Chord Transition Techniques

Benson's characteristic harmonic moves are named and systematized. Chapter 6 introduces the **First and Second GB Chord Transition Ideas** as discrete, labeled techniques — the master's own chord-transition approach codified into teachable units. Chapter 9 reactivates these same techniques in the context of D7(#9) outside playing, establishing that the GB Chord Transitions are not situational tricks but a recurring toolkit. Chapter 13 introduces **guide chord visualization** as the practical fretboard method underlying Active Giant Lines: single-note melodic fingerings are mapped onto guide chord shapes, grounding abstract melodic choices in concrete hand positions the player already knows.

## Rules and Prescriptions

The book establishes at least two explicit prescriptive rules. The first, from Chapter 9, is the **Ending Note Rule**: all outside-effect lines — regardless of how far outside they travel harmonically — must resolve to a D7 chord tone. This rule encodes Benson's threshold discipline: outside color is permitted, even encouraged, but harmonic coherence must be restored at the line's conclusion. The second rule, also from Chapter 9, is an implicit durational constraint against staying on a substitution chord too long; the book warns that exceeding the threshold where outside color is effective tips the line into mere dissonance. Together these two prescriptions define the inside/outside aesthetic as a matter of precise timing and resolution rather than harmonic freedom alone.

## Method Summary

The book's method can be stated as follows: (1) Ground all melodic thinking in chord knowledge. (2) Learn Giant Lines first in static harmonic situations (one chord sustained) before moving to active harmonic situations (changing chords). (3) Within static situations, develop Flux and Reflux — the ability to create and release tension melodically. (4) Extend lines outside the chord using named substitution techniques (the GB Chord Transitions, dominant substitutions via A7 and B7) justified by Mixolydian mode and chord-family theory. (5) Apply the Ending Note Rule to all outside playing. (6) Translate all melodic concepts to the fretboard through guide chord visualization. Chapter 13 applies this full chain to Dm7 harmony in a cadential context, making it the culminating applied chapter, while the progression from D7(9) through D7(#9) through Minor 7th and Major 7th situations represents the book's systematic expansion of the harmonic universe the method covers.
