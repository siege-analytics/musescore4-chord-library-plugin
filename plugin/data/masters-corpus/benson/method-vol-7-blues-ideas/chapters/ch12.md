## Cm7 replacing A half-diminished

> Above we have the cadence Cm7, F7 replacing Aø. In this case we should consider it as being a single Cm7 Dorian or Cm7 hybrid
>      chord. But let's understand step by step where Cm7 came from. First of all we have to remember that Aø is an inversion chord from
>      Cm6, which is the same chord as F7(9) chord. Apart from that, Cm7 Dorian contains the same notes as Aø Locrian because both are
>      derived from Bb key.

**Significance (p. 100).** Defines the harmonic equivalence justifying Cm7/F7 substitution for A half-diminished.

## C#m7/F#7 replacing C7 — altered tensions

> In this case we have the cadence C#m7, F#7 replacing C7. We can do that because it contains the altered tensions from F7 (#9, b9, #11,
> b13). Melodically speaking we can use C#m7 Dorian Scale ideas which will give a different flavor to C7 because of the note "B"(minor
> 7th from "C#m" and major 7th from "C").

**Significance (p. 110).** Theoretical justification and characteristic color note for C#m7 Dorian over C7.

## F#m7/B7 replacing C7 — scale and sound description

> Above we have a cadence C#m7, F#7 replacing C7 leading to "F" . In this case we should consider this cadence as a single F#m7
> Dorian, which can give a special flavour to C7 because of the note "B" (perfect 4th of "F#m7" and major 7th of "C"). As a matter of a
> fact, the melodic ideas from F#m7 Dorian or F#m7 hybrid when played over C7, will sound a combination between C7 Dominant
> Diminished with C7alt.

**Significance (p. 115).** Single-scale simplification of two-chord cadence; resulting sound (Dom Dim + C7alt).

## Abm7/Db7 replacing C7 — characteristic color notes

> Above we have the cadence Abm7,Db7 replacing C7 leading to "F". In this case we should consider this cadence as a single Abm7
> Dorian, which can give a special flavor to C7 because of two specific notes: "Cb (B)" and "F" (minor 3rd & major 6th from "Abm7",
> and major 7th of & perfect 4th from "C"). Apart from that, Abm7 is part of a minor 7th consecutive "chord chain": Gm7, Abm7, Am7,
> which sounds very interesting.

**Significance (p. 120).** Names the color notes by interval function and introduces the minor-7th chord chain.

## Turnaround — breathing between stories

> Notice that in this book we will not focus on the melodic possibilities for the sort turnaround
>      cadence in the last two bars of the blues form. Although we have some great ideas of it in some
>      examples, we must remember that usually we should breathe in this part in order to give space

**Significance (p. 98).** Performance rule: rest in the turnaround to phrase-separate choruses.
