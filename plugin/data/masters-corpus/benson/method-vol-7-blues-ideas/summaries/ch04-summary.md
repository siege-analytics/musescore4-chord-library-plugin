# Chapter 4 — Giant Lines Creating Flux & Reflux over D7(9) Chord

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch04.md`._

Chapter 4 teaches the fundamentals of improvisation over the D7(9) chord at three tempo categories—fast for technique fluency, medium for inverted/reverse hammer technique and breathing between phrases, and slow for mastering Rhythmic Positioning, the method's term for combining time feels and rhythmic cells. The master establishes that jazz pulse is anchored on beats 1 and 3 (not 2 and 4), introduces "the dance of the elephant" as a physical body-movement technique for internalizing pulse, and emphasizes that scales must be visualized differently depending on harmonic context despite enharmonic equivalence. Core practice methods include the pre-set chorus combination notation (1,1,1 meaning full chorus 1; 1,2,3 meaning mixed sections), studying each blues section separately rather than the whole form at once, and understanding that memorized lines are vehicles for internalizing harmonic concepts, not ends in themselves. The chapter introduces load-bearing terminology including Rhythmic Positioning, inverted/reverse hammer, Slow Blues Vibe, Free Slow Blues Vibe, scale visualization, and establishes that fast playing is a consequence of mastery, not a goal, with concrete guidance to begin new concept practice at 110 bpm on slow-medium tempos before scaling to faster and slower speeds.
