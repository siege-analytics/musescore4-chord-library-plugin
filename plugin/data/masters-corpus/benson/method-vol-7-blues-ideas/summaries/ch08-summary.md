# Chapter 8 — D7(#9) Giant Lines

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch08.md`._

Chapter 8 teaches advanced chord substitution techniques over D7(#9) that reharmonize dominant 7th chords through tritone substitution, subdominant region theory, and diminished chord mechanics. The master demonstrates characteristic moves including the IV-degree tritone substitution (C7 = F#7/Gb7), the use of Subdominant Minor region chords like Gbmaj7 and Emaj7 to replace Bb7, and the application of diminished chords as V7 substitutes by exploiting their leading-tone relationships (Fº = E7 creating leading tones to F7). Key load-bearing terminology includes Tritone Substitution, Subdominant Minor Region, Minor Subdominant Region chord, and the theoretical framework linking these substitutions to relative minor tonalities. The chapter's densest insight connects minor-tonality substitution rules to major tonalities, enabling substitutions like Bm7 (Minor Subdominant) replacing Bb7 (Major Subdominant) and deriving Emaj7 from the relative major (Ab) of the parallel minor (Fm) of the home key (F).
