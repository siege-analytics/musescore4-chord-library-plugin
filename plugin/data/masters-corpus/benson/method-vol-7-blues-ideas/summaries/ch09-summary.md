# Chapter 9 — The World of Minor 7th & Major 7th Giant Lines

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch09.md`._

Chapter 9 teaches how to superimpose minor 7th and major 7th chords over cadences in the worlds of Minor 7th and Major 7th Giant Lines. The central rule is that superimposed IIm7-V7-I cadences typically lose the V degree and retain only the IIm7 and Imaj7, with relative chords always available as additional color sources. The master's characteristic move involves using relative chords to create multiple 'colors' of superimposition—for example, in C major, options include Dm7, Fmaj7 (relative of Dm7), Cmaj7, and Am7 (relative of Cmaj7)—each paired with appropriate modal ideas (Dorian over IIm7, Lydian over the relative IV). Load-bearing terminology includes superimposed cadences, relative chords, Dorian and Lydian modal applications, hybrid ideas, and the Tritone Substitution exception, which permits Lydian Dominant or Dominant Diminished colors when the V degree of the superimposed cadence is itself a tritone substitution of a real dominant. The aesthetic preference is that hybrid ideas tend to sound better than strict modal applications.
