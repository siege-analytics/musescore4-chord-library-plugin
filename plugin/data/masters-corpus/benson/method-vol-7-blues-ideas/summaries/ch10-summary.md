# Chapter 10 — Dm7 Static Giant Lines (inside & outside ideas)

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch10.md`._

Chapter 10 teaches static substitutions over the F7 and Bb7 dominant chords of the blues form (inside and outside ideas) using Dm7-related structures, focusing on how non-diatonic chords can replace or reharmonize V7 degrees through chain substitutions, diminished equivalents, and superimposed cadences. The master's moves are built on the leading-tone principle—understanding how individual notes pull toward resolution—and the systematic recognition that a chord substituted into a dominant context inherits scale colors from its parent: Dorian for minor-chord replacements (Bm7, G#m7, Ebm7), Lydian for non-diatonic major substitutions (Bmaj7), and Dominant Diminished or Altered hybrids when notes coincide with altered tensions. Load-bearing terms include Tritone Substitution (Ab7 replacing D7), chain of consecutive minor 7ths (e.g., Bbm7, Am7, G#m7), the V-degree rule under superimposition (which strips tension-bearing capacity from the V in a superimposed IIm7-V7-I), and the use of relative chords (e.g., Gbmaj7 relative to Ebm7) to access additional flavor. The chapter emphasizes that substitution success depends on treating each replacement chord within its native scale identity while leveraging shared leading tones and unique upper-structure notes to bridge to the target harmonic region, including the elegant whole-step leading-note exception in the B°-as-C7 derivation.
