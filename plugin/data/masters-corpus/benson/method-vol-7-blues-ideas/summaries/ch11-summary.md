# Chapter 11 — Active Giant Lines (Explanation)

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch11.md`._

Chapter 11 introduces 'Active Giant Lines' as an explanation chapter. It teaches how to substitute dominant chords using the minor subdominant region: instead of playing a Major Subdominant chord like Bb7, you can derive a substitution from its Minor Subdominant Region equivalent (Bbm), selecting a related chord such as Gbmaj7 that carries a relative minor chord (Ebm7) which then precedes the original dominant via Ab7. The characteristic move demonstrated is the Ebm7/Ab7 cadential substitution replacing Bb7, which the author analyzes as a single Ebm7 Dorian or hybrid chord. Load-bearing terminology introduced includes Minor Subdominant Region, relative chord relationships, and the concept of deriving substitutions through parallel minor harmonic areas rather than direct modal replacement — this becomes the foundation for the 'active' (substitution-rich) blues lines elaborated in the following chapter.
