## Outline No.2 passing tones

> Passing tones: Since outline no.2 is an arpeggiated outline, it lends itself to diatonic passing tones between the chord tones.

**Significance (p. 54).** Structural identity of Outline No.2.

## Punch line rule

> The tone between the 5th and 7th of the ii chord is the target note of the V7 chord. This tone is usually saved for the V7 chord. It is the punch line, the denouement of the story, that is not given away by using it ahead of time as a passing tone.

**Significance (p. 54).** Central harmonic discipline.

## Minor ii-V applicability

> All the outlines work as well for ii@ - V7 in minor as they do in major. As shown before, they work for any progression with root movement downward in fifths.

**Significance (p. 52).** Generalizes beyond major-key ii-V.

## Adding notes

## Standard direction of Outline No.2

## Change of direction

> There are several occurrences of the "Round Midnight" outline with the direction changed. Instead of ascending the arpeggio of the ii chord, the arpeggio is turned upside down and descends in some way.

**Significance (p. 61).** Inverted variant.

## Breaking target-note rule

> The third example breaks the "rule" of playing the target note early: The F sharp appears as a passing tone before the D7 chord, but the negative effect is diminished by the extension of the line to the ninth of the ii chord.

**Significance (p. 58).** Qualifies the punch line rule.

## Multi-outline combinations

> Parker begins with outline no.2 and ends with outline no.1. There are many occurrences of this line in different guises throughout Parker solos.

**Significance (p. 59).** Common multi-outline combo.

## Fragments preserve clarity

> The root of the ii chord is left off. Harrell plays another note which is heard probably heard as the leading tone to the F. Evans and Rollins leave of the first note and begin on the third, These omissions take none of the clarity away from the lines. They all emphasize the target note (third of ii) even more, and the root of the chord is covered by the bass.

**Significance (p. 63).** Fragmentation without clarity loss.
