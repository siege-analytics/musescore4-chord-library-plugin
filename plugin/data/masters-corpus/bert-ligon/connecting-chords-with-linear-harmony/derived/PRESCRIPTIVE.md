# Prescriptive lessons — bert-ligon × connecting-chords-with-linear-harmony

## min7

Ligon treats the ii min7 as the harmonic initiator of the ii–V–I, with its third as the single most specific melodic target and its seventh as a directed pointer that must resolve down to the third of the V7. All three outlines are organized around this two-note kernel: the third establishes harmonic identity on beat one; the seventh, placed later and on a metrically significant beat, generates the forward pull that makes the progression audible in time. Fragments that omit other chord tones remain fully valid because the bass covers the root and the seventh-to-third resolution supplies sufficient harmonic information on its own.

- [ch.2 p.5] The third of the ii min7 is the single most harmonically specific melodic target; the root is omitted because the bass covers it.
- [ch.2 p.5–6] The seventh of the ii chord is the restless pointer that resolves downward to the third of V7.
- [ch.2 p.6; ch.4 p.18] Outline No. 1 over ii min7: third on beat one, stepwise descent through the chord, seventh delivered on beat four as the pointer into V7.
- [ch.2 p.8; ch.5 p.60, 63] Outline No. 2 over ii min7: ascending arpeggio 1–3–5–7; root-omitted fragments are fully valid.
- [ch.2 p.9; ch.6 p.66] Outline No. 3 over ii min7: descending arpeggio 5–3–1, then seventh below as pointer tone, typically on the upbeat.
- [ch.5 p.54] Punch-line rule: the sixth scale degree must be reserved for the V7 chord; early arrival collapses the harmonic tension.
- [ch.5 p.58] Punch-line exception: rule may be broken when the line extends to the ninth of the ii chord.
- [ch.7 p.76–77] Outline fragments retain harmonic coherence as long as target tones are placed and resolved correctly.
- [ch.8 p.78] Over a modal ii min7 context, superimpose bebop outlines via leading-tone approach with sawtooth contour.

## dom7

Ligon organizes the entire dominant seventh treatment around a single principle: the third of V7 is the primary harmonic arrival point for the entire ii–V–I, and every embellishment device exists to intensify the sense of arrival at that third on a metrically strong beat. All twelve chromatic pitches are available over the V7, but only when target tones land on strong beats. Root motion descending by fifths — not chord quality — is the controlling criterion for outline selection.

- [ch.2 p.5–6] The third of V7 is the primary arrival note; all three outlines converge on it.
- [ch.3 p.16] Parallel-minor borrowing: flat-9, sharp-9, flat-13 over G7 in C major.
- [ch.3 p.15; ch.4 p.39; ch.6 p.68] C.E.S.H. superimposes a descending chromatic line from the root of ii to the third of V7.
- [ch.6 p.66] Outline No. 3 signature move: leap from the third to the flat-nine creates compound melody.
- [ch.4 p.43; ch.6 p.74] Chromatic non-chord tones licensed only when target tones land on strong beats.
- [ch.4 p.32] Ascending scales must skip an upcoming target note.
- [ch.3 p.14] Octave displacement: third-to-ninth seventh leap after the guide tone, downbeat-to-upbeat, never across barline.
- [ch.5 p.52; ch.3 p.11] Encirclement: diatonic UNT + chromatic LNT approach to the target.
- [ch.4 p.29] Universal applicability: any descending-fifths root motion supports any outline regardless of chord quality.
- [ch.8 p.78] In modal blues, C.E.S.H. Outline No. 3 functions as contrast device against motivic playing.
- [ch.16 p.145] Stop-and-go rhythmic delay: withhold target arrival; 3–5–7–9 arpeggio pattern.
- [ch.16 p.145; ch.3 p.11] Sawtooth: leap back to chord tone + chromatic leading tone before target.
- [ch.16 p.145] Neighbor-tone surrounding: approach target from above (diatonic) and below (chromatic).

## min7b5

Ligon makes the extension of the outline system to the half-diminished chord explicit and unqualified: all three outlines apply to the minor ii–V context with the same target-tone logic as in major.

- [ch.5 p.52] All three outlines work for half-diminished/V7 in minor as in major; third of half-dim is the opening melodic goal.

## dom7b9

Ligon's parallel-minor borrowing rule carries full force in the minor ii–V–i context, and is more idiomatic there because the harmonic environment already lives in a minor key.

- [ch.3 p.16] Flat-9, sharp-9, flat-13 over V7 in minor ii–V–i; especially idiomatic in this context.

## sus4

Ligon collapses the sus4 chord into the ii–V7 composite rather than treating it as a separate chord quality.

- [ch.6 p.65] Dominant sus4 voicings are interchangeable with ii–V7 voicings for outline purposes; all three outlines apply unmodified.
