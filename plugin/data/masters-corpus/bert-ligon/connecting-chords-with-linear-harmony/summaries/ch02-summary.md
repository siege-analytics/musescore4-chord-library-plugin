# Chapter 2 — Linear Harmony

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Linear harmony unifies harmony and melody by treating chords not as vertical stacks but as horizontal lines unfolding over time, with notes chosen in relation to the bass line rather than root-position voicings. The chapter introduces a framework where eliminating the root opens eleven note choices, with the third providing harmonic specificity and the seventh acting as a restless pointer that resolves downward to the next chord's third—the engine driving melodic motion. Three foundational outlines represent distinct motion types: a descending stepwise line, an ascending arpeggio, and a descending arpeggio, each constructed by placing target tones (thirds and sevenths) on metrically significant beats and filling the remaining space with scalar or arpeggiated movement. These outlines function as enabling skeletons that inspire rather than stifle creativity, allowing the improviser to create smooth voice-leading that connects chords through good counterpoint, with the axiom that thirds appear earlier in melodic lines (more consonant) before the more dissonant sevenths. The load-bearing terms—linear harmony, voice leading, the third as most specific tone, and the seventh as the pointer—form the conceptual toolkit for melodic improvisation grounded in harmonic clarity.
