# Chapter 3 — Embellishing Devices

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch03.md`._

Chapter 3 teaches the foundational voice-leading principle that chromatically altered tones continue in their direction of alteration—flatted notes descend, sharped notes ascend—establishing the asymmetric convention of using diatonic upper neighbor tones (UNT) and chromatic lower neighbor tones (LNT). The chapter introduces octave displacement as a rhythmic and melodic device, with leaps occurring most commonly after the guide-tone and moving from downbeat to upbeat, never crossing the barline; the signature move is the 3rd-to-9th displacement, where a simple descending second becomes dramatic by replacing it with a seventh leap. Two load-bearing concepts emerge: C.E.S.H. (Chromatic Elaboration of Static Harmony), exemplified by the descending chromatic line in ii-V progressions, and the use of borrowed tones from the parallel minor key over dominant chords, particularly the flat 9, sharp 9, and flat 13, which carry a greater downward pull than their diatonic equivalents. Together, these devices provide the harmonic and melodic vocabulary for sophisticated embellishment within the jazz idiom.
