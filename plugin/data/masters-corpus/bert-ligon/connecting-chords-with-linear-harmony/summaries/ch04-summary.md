# Chapter 4 — Outline No.1

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch04.md`._

Chapter 4 introduces Outline No.1, a foundational melodic pattern that moves from the 3rd of the ii chord down to the 3rd of the V7 chord and resolves to the tonic, establishing a core mechanic applicable to any chord progression with descending fifth root movement regardless of chord quality. The chapter demonstrates how simple outlines become musically interesting through techniques including iteration (repetition for rhythmic motion), octave displacement for variety, and chromatic elaboration of static harmony (C.E.S.H.), where chromatic tones function linearly to create tension and resolve to target notes rather than serving as vertical harmonic mistakes. Key concepts include borrowing from the parallel minor (such as the flat-nine of a dominant chord), the principle that all twelve pitches can be used with harmonic clarity when target notes land rhythmically and non-harmonic notes resolve predictably, and the subtle rule of skipping ascending scales to avoid upcoming target notes. The chapter also reveals that reduction—stripping away embellishments—can be as artistically revealing as elaboration, and introduces compound melody as an emergent property of the outline's lower tones, establishing the master's characteristic approach of viewing chromaticism through linear context rather than strict vertical harmony.
