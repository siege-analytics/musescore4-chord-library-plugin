# Chapter 7 — Outline Fragments

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch07.md`._

Chapter 7 teaches outline fragments—skeletal harmonic structures that maintain clarity even when some essential chord tones are omitted—as a compositional technique for creating melodic interest within harmonic frameworks. The chapter illustrates this through analysis of jazz masters: Parker demonstrates how a minimal harmonic kernel (the seventh of ii resolving to the third of V7) can anchor a line despite ornamental turns and rhythmic displacement; Morgan shows how omitting the third of ii while anticipating and arpeggioating the V7 chord still preserves harmonic direction; and Harrell exemplifies how rhythmic placement of target notes—particularly through accented upper neighbor tones and strategic downbeat placement—reinforces harmonic function. The load-bearing concept is that outline fragments derive their coherence not from complete chord voicings but from the strategic placement and resolution of key harmonic targets within a broader outline structure.
