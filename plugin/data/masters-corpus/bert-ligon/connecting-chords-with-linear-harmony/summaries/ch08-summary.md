# Chapter 8 — Outlines in Modal Tunes

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch08.md`._

Chapter 8 teaches how jazz improvisers blend bebop vocabulary—particularly outline shapes and leading tones—into modal improvisation, as exemplified by Cannonball Adderley's strategic use of the C.E.S.H. version of outline no.3 on modal blues. The chapter demonstrates specific voice-leading mechanics: approaching chord tones by their leading tones, using encircling motion (as with F# and A around the root), and implying non-modal harmony (such as C7 through E natural) while maintaining modal context. Leading tones create a characteristic sawtooth contour that brings clarity to the outline shapes, particularly when they fall on downbeats, adding rhythmic and melodic definition to modal lines. The load-bearing concept is the strategic layering of bebop outlines—structured chord-tone vocabularies—over modal harmonic foundations, allowing improvisers to contrast motivic, blues-inflected playing with more architecturally precise harmonic statements.
