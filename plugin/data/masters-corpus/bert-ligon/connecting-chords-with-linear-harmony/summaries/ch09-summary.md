# Chapter 9 — Get It in Your Playing

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch09.md`._

To internalize jazz vocabulary and outlines, you must practice them with proper articulations, swing feel, and accent before attempting elaboration—beginning slowly with quarter notes to lock them in before accelerating. The chapter emphasizes that sloppy practice produces sloppy playing, making deliberate, quality repetition non-negotiable for mastery. Notation alone cannot convey the critical elements that make lines swing—accents, placement, inflections, articulations, and timbre—which must be learned through listening to and imitating great jazz artists rather than relying on written symbols. The chapter introduces a concrete harmonic map of common modulation targets, noting that standard tunes typically modulate by one accidental up or down, with the most frequent targets from C major being the diatonic triads (A minor, F, D minor, G, E minor, and C minor itself). This chapter establishes the foundational principle that you cannot elaborate on anything until you know it thoroughly, positioning focused, attentive practice as the essential prerequisite for artistic development.
