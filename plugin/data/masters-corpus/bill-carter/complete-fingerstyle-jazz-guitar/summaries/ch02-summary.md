# Chapter 2 — Beginning Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 2: Learning the blues: fretted notes, rests

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 introduces the fundamental right-hand and left-hand techniques for fingerstyle jazz guitar, establishing that the thumb (p) plays lower strings with free stroke while fingers (i, m, a) play upper strings with rest stroke, with stem direction in notation signaling which technique to use. The chapter defines the canonical left-hand position--thumb under the neck, fingers arched--and connects it to musical control over duration, tone, and expression. Carter's characteristic move is framing technical choices (fingering strategy, damping method) as deliberate options rather than rigid rules, giving the student agency while maintaining structural clarity. The chapter introduces three load-bearing concepts: the E minor pentatonic blues scale (the harmonic foundation for all blues work that follows), damping (the technique of stopping fretted notes by releasing finger pressure and stopping open strings by touching them lightly with a free finger, executed in alternation across all right-hand fingers), and two-part notation (the principle that melody and accompaniment voices must account for all beats in the measure, distinguished by stem direction and tied notes). The twelve-bar blues form is presented as the structural container for applying these techniques.
