# Chapter 3 — Beginning Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 3: Rhythming around

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch03.md`._

Chapter 3 teaches the foundational rhythmic and fingerstyle techniques for jazz guitar through blues-based exercises, introducing the principle of simultaneous plucking and damping to create self-accompanied solos where bass notes control both harmony and rhythm. The chapter establishes the alternating bass as the rhythmic engine of fingerstyle jazz, where the thumb maintains a steady quarter-note pulse on the lower strings while the fingers handle melody and chord tones on the upper strings. Carter introduces the shuffle feel as the default jazz-blues rhythmic framework and demonstrates how to voice chords so that each note falls naturally under the assigned right-hand finger. The exercises progress from simple two-voice textures to three-voice arrangements where bass, chords, and melody operate as independent but coordinated layers. A key pedagogical move is Carter's insistence that students internalize the bass pattern until it becomes automatic, freeing conscious attention for melodic improvisation above it.
