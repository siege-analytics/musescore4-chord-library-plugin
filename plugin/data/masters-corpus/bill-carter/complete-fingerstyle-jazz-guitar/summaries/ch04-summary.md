# Chapter 4 — Beginning Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 4: The flow of jazz: eighth notes

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch04.md`._

Chapter 4 teaches the notational and technical foundations for fingerstyle jazz guitar, emphasizing rhythmic clarity and two-voice independence. The chapter introduces the concept of writing out rhythmic values for both the melody voice and the bass voice so that each beat in the measure is accounted for in both parts simultaneously. Carter demonstrates how tied notes and rests work within this two-part framework, showing that when a note in one voice sustains across a beat, the other voice must still articulate its rhythm explicitly. The chapter builds on the E minor pentatonic blues scale from Chapter 2 by applying it within increasingly complex rhythmic frameworks, teaching students to maintain independent voice leading between the thumb's bass line and the fingers' melody. Carter's characteristic pedagogical approach surfaces here in his insistence that students write out and count every beat in both voices before attempting to play, establishing the analytical habit that supports all subsequent arranging work.
