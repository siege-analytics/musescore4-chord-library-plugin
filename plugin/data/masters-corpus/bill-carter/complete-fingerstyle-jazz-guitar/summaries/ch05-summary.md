# Chapter 5 — Creating Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Solos, Part 1: Oom-pah power

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch05.md`._

This chapter teaches the foundational skills for arranging jazz standards into fingerstyle guitar solos, beginning with tune selection criteria and progressing through the complete arrangement workflow. Carter establishes that the best tunes for fingerstyle arrangement have strong, singable melodies that can stand on their own without elaborate harmonic support, and he warns against tunes whose interest lies primarily in their chord changes rather than their melodic content. The chapter introduces the concept of reducing a full band arrangement to its essential elements for solo guitar, identifying which notes of a chord voicing are dispensable and which are load-bearing for the harmonic function. Carter demonstrates his approach to voice leading within these reduced voicings, showing how to connect chords smoothly while maintaining the melody as the top voice. The arrangement process is presented as a series of practical decisions rather than theoretical abstractions, with Carter consistently framing each choice as a tradeoff between harmonic completeness and physical playability on the instrument.
