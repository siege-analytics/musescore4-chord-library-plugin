# Chapter 6 — Creating Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Solos, Part 2: Harmonizing with thirds

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch06.md`._

Chapter 6 teaches transposition techniques and fingerstyle accompaniment fundamentals for jazz guitar self-arrangements. Carter presents transposition as an essential skill for adapting tunes to keys that exploit the guitar's open strings and natural resonances, demonstrating how the same melody can be repositioned across the fretboard to find voicings that are both playable and harmonically rich. The chapter introduces accompaniment patterns that function as the rhythmic and harmonic foundation beneath a melody line, ranging from simple root-fifth bass movements to more elaborate walking patterns. Carter emphasizes that the accompaniment must serve the melody rather than compete with it, teaching students to calibrate the density and rhythmic activity of their bass lines and chord insertions based on what the melody demands at each moment. The practical exercises progress from block-chord accompaniment through arpeggiated patterns to mixed textures that combine elements of both approaches within a single arrangement.
