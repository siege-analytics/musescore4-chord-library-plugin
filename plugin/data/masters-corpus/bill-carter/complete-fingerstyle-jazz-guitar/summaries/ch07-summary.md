# Chapter 7 — Creating Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Solos, Part 3

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch07.md`._

Chapter 7 teaches three interlocking concepts for fingerstyle jazz arranging: first, variations on the root/fifth bass pattern that create harmonic motion and rhythmic interest beneath a melody; second, the use of chord substitutions to enrich standard progressions while maintaining their functional direction; and third, techniques for connecting chords through chromatic and diatonic passing tones in the bass voice. Carter demonstrates how a simple root-fifth alternation can be transformed through approach tones, chromatic connectors, and scale-wise bass runs that imply walking bass movement without requiring constant quarter-note motion. The chord substitution material focuses on tritone substitutions and related ii-V replacements, presented not as theoretical abstractions but as practical voicing swaps the student can apply immediately to standard tunes. Carter's pedagogical approach is characteristically pragmatic, showing each substitution as a physical hand shape on the fretboard rather than a harmonic analysis exercise, and emphasizing that the ear is the final arbiter of whether a substitution works in context.
