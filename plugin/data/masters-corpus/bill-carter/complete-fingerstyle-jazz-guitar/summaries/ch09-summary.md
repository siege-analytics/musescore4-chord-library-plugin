# Chapter 9 — Creating Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Solos, Part 5

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch09.md`._

Creating Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Solos, Part 5 teaches two primary harmonization techniques: thirds (a note a third below the melody) and tenths (an octave plus a third below), evaluated against chord symbols to determine whether they are chord tones or colorful non-chord tones. Carter establishes that the overall harmonic effect matters more than note-by-note correctness -- non-chord tones add "interesting spice" -- and offers corrective moves like dropping from a third to a fourth interval to land on a chord tone when needed. The chapter prescribes a complete workflow: harmonize in thirds, consult chord symbols and the TABLE OF THIRDS, adjust by ear, then anchor the result with a simple root or root-fifth bass line to create a three-voice arrangement. Tenths function differently: they serve as independent bass notes with their own rhythm rather than moving in lockstep with the melody, and should not dominate an entire arrangement. Throughout, Carter emphasizes practice fluency through daily scales in thirds with rhythmic variations, establishing thirds and tenths as load-bearing vocabulary for fingerstyle voicing and arrangement.
