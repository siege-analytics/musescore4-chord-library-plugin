# Chapter 11 — Creating Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar Solos, Part 7: Harmonizing with sixths

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch11.md`._

Chapter 11 teaches the convention and practice of harmonizing melodies in sixths, establishing that the key note must sit on top with the harmony note a sixth below to preserve the major-key sound -- placing the key note at the bottom would be perceived as minor. The chapter applies the same major/minor adjustment discipline to sixths as was used with thirds, directing students to take familiar tunes and harmonize them diatonically. Carter's characteristic restraint appears in his taste principle: a complete arrangement should use arranging devices economically and well rather than layering every device at once, as exemplified in a call-and-answer structured tune with slow-moving bass, eighth-note melody lines, and swing feel. The load-bearing terminology introduced includes diatonic sixth harmonization, the major/minor sixth distinction, and call-and-answer as a compositional structure that gives a tune shape.
