# Chapter 12 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 1: Harmonic background

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch12.md`._

Chapter 12 introduces the harmonic foundations of professional fingerstyle jazz guitar by reframing the instrument as a miniature orchestra of independent voices rather than block chords, teaching that jazz harmony rests on seventh chords (minimum four notes stacked 1-3-5-7) organized within major and minor scales. The core concepts are the diatonic seventh chord taxonomy (two major sevenths, one dominant seventh, three minor sevenths, one half-diminished in a major key), open-position voicings that redistribute chord tones across octaves for playability, and the cycle of fifths as the most basic harmonic motion in jazz, typically featuring V7-I or V7-V7 patterns. Carter's characteristic move throughout the chapter is his bidirectional practice methodology -- extract specific examples from general exercises and generalize specific ideas through the cycle of fifths and harmonized scales -- which trains the player to recognize harmonic patterns across keys and song contexts. The load-bearing terminology introduced includes seventh chords, diatonic harmony, open-position voicings, and the cycle of fifths.
