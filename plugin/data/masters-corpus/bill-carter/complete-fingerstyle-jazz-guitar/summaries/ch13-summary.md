# Chapter 13 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 2: Self accompanying

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch13.md`._

Chapter 13 teaches self-accompaniment techniques for fingerstyle jazz, progressing from simple to sophisticated approaches: doubled bass notes, root/fifth and root/seventh alternation, walking bass with scale-step passing tones, chromatic approach notes to chord roots, bass-note leading for rhythmic variety, and swing-era moving tenths inspired by pianists like Art Tatum and Fats Waller. The chapter emphasizes economy of means -- showing that two accompanying notes suffice for a full sound -- and establishes the II-V-I progression with its specific voicing recipe (minor seventh as root+seventh, dominant seventh as root+third) as the foundational harmonic movement in jazz. Carter's characteristic move is connecting guitar idioms to jazz history and other instruments, binding techniques to their cultural origins; key terminology introduced includes mixed harmonic movement (combining diatonic, chromatic, and cycle-of-fifths motion), diminished seventh as the "hamburger helper" passing chord, and fourths/tritones voicing as a rootless alternative that delegates root coverage to the bassist.
