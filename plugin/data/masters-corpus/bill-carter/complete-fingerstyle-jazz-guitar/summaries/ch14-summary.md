# Chapter 14 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 3: Capable accompanist accoutrements

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch14.md`._

Chapter 14 teaches the fundamentals of accompanying other musicians in jazz contexts, emphasizing that good accompaniment is transparent support that enables others' best work. The chapter introduces the concept of comping -- accompanying and/or complementing through sparse, responsive playing -- and establishes the cardinal comping density rule: play more in the soloist's rests and less when they play busily. Carter's characteristic approach appears in his insistence on the psycho-social dimension of accompanying (treating bandmates as family and making their goals yours) and his challenge to guitarists' chord-centric thinking, proposing that accompaniment can unfold as single lines inspired by other instruments. The chapter covers practical separation strategies for ensemble playing (ranging from upper-neck voicing for electric duets to alternate tunings for guitar pairs), the bass-first chord conception principle (conceiving the bass line before adding harmonic comping), and the limitations of guitar-simulated walking bass -- introducing the load-bearing terms comping, bass-plus-chord, and the distinction between self-accompaniment and backing others.
