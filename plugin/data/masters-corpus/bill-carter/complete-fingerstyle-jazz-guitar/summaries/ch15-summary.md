# Chapter 15 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 4: Expressive devices of jazz

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch15.md`._

This chapter teaches the idiomatic language of jazz through seven core expressive techniques: the foundational quarter-note rhythmic pulse and eighth-note melodic pulse, the syncopated swing feel (written as straight eighths but played with shuffle), off-beat accenting as a phrasing trademark, ghosted notes produced through light plucking or slurs like hammer-ons and pull-offs to create rhythmic understatement, slides and glissandos for melodic expression rooted in blues tradition, and rhythmic displacement through anticipation and delay of written notes. The master's characteristic moves visible throughout are the emphasis on listening to live and recorded jazz as the primary learning method and the consistent framing of jazz vocabulary as the street language of the idiom -- techniques that distinguish jazz from other musical traditions. The load-bearing terminology introduced includes swing feel, off-beats, ghosted notes, glissando, and rhythmic displacement, all techniques that must be felt and heard in context rather than simply read in notation.
