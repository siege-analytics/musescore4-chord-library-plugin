# Chapter 16 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 5: Pedaling the cycle of fifths

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch16.md`._

This chapter teaches jazz musicians how to practice and perform through the cycle of fifths -- a foundational technique for gaining fluency across all twelve keys and developing smooth voice leading. The master's characteristic approach is evident in the detailed voice-leading analysis, particularly the V7-I tritone pull, where Carter shows how independent moving voices (half-step motion in the tritone, common tones between chords, alternating root positions) create harmonic flow far more musical than playing static chord shapes. The chapter introduces essential terminology and concepts: the cycle notation convention (writing entirely in the key of C with accidentals), the practical movement rule (up a fourth instead of down a fifth to stay in position), the two-beat harmonic rhythm tied to root-first patterns, the tenth-interval spread for bass-and-melody balance, and the core principle that harmony should be conceived as several moving voices rather than a glob of dots on a chord box grid. Carter demonstrates this philosophy through analyzed phrases moving through the cycle in a downward direction, showing how tritone resolution, voice leading by half-step and whole-step intervals, and strategic chord voicings (not always root position) create the smooth, interesting harmonic movement that distinguishes professional fingerstyle jazz guitar playing.
