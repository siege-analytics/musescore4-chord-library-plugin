# Chapter 17 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 6: Intros, endings, turnarounds, tags & modulations

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch17.md`._

Chapter 17 teaches the essential structures for jazz guitar intros, endings, turnarounds, and modulations, all grounded in the I-V7 relationship and the cycle of fifths. Intros are demystified as ordinary improvisations in two- or four-bar frameworks that must end on V7 to resolve into the tune's opening chord, while endings reverse this logic or use IV-I; the pedal tone and vamp (a repeating intro cueing singers) are standard devices for establishing tempo and feel. Turnarounds, occurring in the final two measures before each chorus repeat, function as two-measure intros with the same I-to-V7 movement, while tags extend the final chorus and require listening for duration cues. The chapter introduces practical tricks -- notably that omitting the final V7 converts any intro into an ending, doubling the guitarist's repertoire -- and three modulation techniques: the fastest is borrowing V7 from the target key for two beats, while more complex moves use back-cycling through the cycle of fifths or the II-V7-I progression from the new key. Carter's characteristic move throughout is reducing each device to its essential principle: tritone half-step movement replaces root movement in modern jazz, the cycle distills to five steps down or four up, and turnarounds become interchangeable with intros once the underlying structure is recognized.
