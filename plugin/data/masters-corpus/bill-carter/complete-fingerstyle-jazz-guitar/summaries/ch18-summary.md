# Chapter 18 — Professional Fingerstyle Jazz Guitar, Part 7: Fun Jazz

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch18.md`._

Chapter 18 teaches jazz fingerstyle techniques grounded in harmonic movement and melodic priority: chromatic modulation by half step, half-step chord approaches, and the within-the-scale cycle that approximates the cycle of fifths while allowing deceptive cadences as modulation pivots. The chapter's characteristic move is Carter's philosophy of restraint -- imply more with less through two- or three-note voicings that defer to melody -- and his refusal to over-analyze, presenting seven Fun Jazz pieces as improvisations over standard changes rather than pedagogical exercises. Key load-bearing terms introduced include the walkdown (descending bass line with common-tone chord stack), the Joe Pass device (continual eighth-note flow to compensate for sparse bass), 3 on 4 polyrhythm, and the principle of self-accompanied arrangement (converting single-line melody into fingerstyle texture). Throughout, Carter emphasizes ear-based judgment balanced by ensemble taste, naming ballads as the form offering maximum freedom and demonstrating genre-crossing techniques like stride-to-bebop fusion.
