## VI7 dominant substitution rule

> Sweet Georgia Brown starts on the VI7. |t wouldn’t seem like such a
> surprising way to start if it was a minor 7 chord (meaning it would be
> the relative minor). However, the vi minor is converted into a VI domi-
> nant.

**Significance (p. 94).** This passage defines the core harmonic rule at work in the song -- that diatonic minor chords can be raised to dominant 7ths -- which recurs throughout the chapter's analysis.

## Converting minor chords to dominant 7ths

## Descending chromatic dominant approach

> Don't let this section throw you either. Understand we are just dealing
> with a series of chromatically descending dominant 7“ chords that are
> targeting the VI7 chord. From there the VI7 logically concludes to a ii-
> V-| to the parent tonic.

**Significance (p. 97).** This is the rule for understanding and demystifying the chromatic tail section of the song -- recognising it as a directed approach to the VI7 rather than arbitrary chromaticism.

## Mixolydian scale definition

> The easiest way to think of a Mixolydian scale is a major scale with a
> b7. Or you can think of it as starting and ending a major scale on its
> 5* tone.
>
> Formula: 1-2-3-4-5-6-b7

**Significance (p. 99).** This is the chapter's central scale definition, providing two complementary mental models plus a formula that the student will use directly for improvisation over dominant 7 chords.

## Scales as pitch collections

> best way to think about the use of scales is “pitch collections.” They
> aren’t notes that are meant to be played in order, nor are they to be
> used solely to improvise. They are simply pitch collections which map
> out the note choices you can use to make melodies over a particular
> chord.

**Significance (p. 99).** This reframes the pedagogical purpose of scales from mechanical running to melodic resource, which is a fundamental conceptual shift for improvisation students.

## Licks at micro level vs. macro composition

## Transposing licks through all 12 keys

> | always suggest taking things through all 12 keys. It’s a powerful
> practice that not only helps you internalize musical information, but it
> also enables you to become stronger in keys you may be weaker at.

**Significance (p. 101).** This is the method's explicit rule for how to practice any short musical phrase, making key-transposition a non-negotiable part of the lick-learning workflow.
