# Chapter 1 — Read This First / Before We Start: The Pro Guide to Learning Jazz Standards

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch01.md`._

This chapter establishes the foundational methodology for learning jazz standards: ear-first training rather than sheet-music dependence, grounded in how bebop musicians actually learned in the 1940s by listening and reacting in real time. Greenan introduces the LIST process -- Listen, Internalize, Sing, Transfer -- as the procedural framework governing all subsequent study, with each step building toward fluent harmonic response: put your instrument away during listening (using multiple recordings including vocal versions to internalize melody), sing the melody alone and with recordings as proof of internalization, then transfer to your instrument (melody first, then chords by ear, using bass-note identification as the key technique). The characteristic move visible here is Greenan's concrete, rule-based approach: rather than abstract principles, he provides specific behavioral directives (learn lyrics, use Sinatra as a melodic reference, avoid Holiday and Davis for melody study, listen to bass notes to identify chord roots) and identifies the hard stage explicitly (chord learning by ear). The chapter introduces load-bearing terminology including the LIST acronym itself, chromatic approach (CA), and chromatic passing (CP), which structure interpretation of all musical examples to follow.
