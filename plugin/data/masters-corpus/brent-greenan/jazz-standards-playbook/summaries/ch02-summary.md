# Chapter 2 — Jazz Standard Study #1: Autumn Leaves

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 introduces the harmonic architecture and foundational improvisation technique for analyzing jazz standards, using Autumn Leaves as the first case study. The student learns the Major and Minor Diatonic Series of 7th Chords -- reference charts that map chord quality to each scale degree -- establishing that the V chord is always dominant in minor keys and that the minor chart represents common choices rather than strict harmonization. The chapter then focuses on the ii-V-I progression, identified as jazz's most essential harmonic pattern, and teaches guide tones (the 3rds and 7ths of chords) as the primary tool for improvisation, grounded in the rule that these intervals define chord quality while the 7th resolves stepwise to the 3rd of the next chord, a principle called voice leading. Key terminology introduced includes AABA song form, relative keys (major and minor keys sharing the same key signature), and the practical rule for transposing to concert keys -- the common keys jazz musicians actually play in. The method prescribes isolated practice of guide tones through the full song form as the core drill for internalizing this technique across all standards.
