# Chapter 5 — Jazz Standard Study #4: It Could Happen to You

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch05.md`._

This chapter teaches harmonic analysis through the lens of target chords -- chords that other chords resolve toward -- and establishes the fundamental rule that harmony must always be understood relationally rather than in isolation, whether through the key-center thinking introduced in earlier chapters or through understanding how target chords function within overall harmonic movement. Greenan introduces the dual-analysis framework where a ii-V of ii can simultaneously be read as iiib5-VI7 within the parent key, and explains the minor iv chord substitution as a quick pivot into parallel minor tonality, offering concrete voicing alternatives (Abmin6, Abmin7-Db7, or Db7). The chapter's signature move is establishing improvisation as "composition sped up" and building an entire improvisational practice around contrafact composition -- writing new melodies over existing changes -- with explicit guidelines: keep melodies simple and singable, outline the changes, use recurring themes with variation, and leave space to breathe. Key new terminology includes target chord, contrafact, and bebop contrafact style, the latter defined as a busier, solo-like variant of the compositional form.
