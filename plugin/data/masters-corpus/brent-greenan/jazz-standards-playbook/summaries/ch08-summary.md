# Chapter 8 — Jazz Standard Study #7: On Green Dolphin Street

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch08.md`._

This chapter applies the keep-it-simple philosophy to a detailed harmonic analysis of "On Green Dolphin Street," teaching how to navigate complex jazz progressions by identifying the defining notes that change between consecutive chords -- specifically the 3rds and 7ths -- rather than attempting to fill harmonic space with excessive notes. Greenan introduces essential vocabulary including slash chords and pedal tones, explains the bIImaj7 chord as both a tritone substitution variant and a chromatic passing chord, and highlights recurring compositional techniques like key center shifts a minor 3rd up and half-step bass line movement connecting descending chord progressions. The chapter's characteristic move is breaking down seemingly complex harmony into minimal, essential changes: when Cmaj7 shifts to Cmin7, only the b3 and b7 move, and when improvising over such progressions, adjusting just those intervals while keeping other notes stable creates coherent melodic lines. The load-bearing concepts are motif, chromatic passing tone, diatonic sequence, secondary dominants, and the practice technique of playing fewer notes to reveal harmonic structure.
