# Chapter 9 — Jazz Standard Study #8: Have You Met Miss Jones

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch09.md`._

Chapter 9 teaches jazz musicians how to navigate the bridge of "Have You Met Miss Jones" by understanding diminished 7th chords as rootless altered dominants (the F#dim7 as a rootless D7b9), which function both as harmonic substitutions and as chromatic passing chords connecting key centers. The chapter reveals that the bridge's seemingly complex chord changes are actually a series of ii-V-Is moving in descending thirds -- a structural principle shared with Coltrane Changes -- and provides two practical voice-leading strategies for crossing key-center seams: connecting adjacent phrases by half or whole steps, or using rhythmic space and rests to separate key centers smoothly. Key terminology introduced includes the rootless altered dominant, the whole-half diminished scale (W-H-W-H-W-H-W), and the recognition that chord tones of the VI7b9 are valid note choices over the substituted diminished passing chord. Greenan's characteristic move throughout is to scaffold theory (interval formulas, harmonic function) with concrete musical examples and licks, culminating in the principle that melodic feel and ear-based navigation should guide improvisation more than mechanical scale application.
