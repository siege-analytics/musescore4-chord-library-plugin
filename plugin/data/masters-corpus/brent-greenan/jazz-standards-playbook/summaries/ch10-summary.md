# Chapter 10 — Jazz Standard Study #9: All the Things You Are

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch10.md`._

Chapter 10 studies "All the Things You Are" as a masterwork of cycling 4ths in jazz, teaching students to recognize its AABA form, 36-bar structure, and transposition across five key centers as a model for harmonic architecture. The chapter introduces two cornerstone improvisation concepts: the rule that a ii chord can almost always precede any V chord (enabling ii-V-I substitutions), and the recognition that diminished chords serve dual functions -- either as chromatic connectors or as substitutes -- distinct from their role in prior standards. Greenan's characteristic move here is the pattern-recognition pedagogy: identifying that bars 9-16 exactly replicate bars 1-8 transposed to a new key, and teaching students to use the V chord as a "secret weapon" for modulation when navigating between key centers. The chapter consolidates six improvisation tools (guide tones, melody incorporation, chord tones, contrafacts, motifs, and transcribed licks) and establishes composed solos as deliberate practice rather than performance, positioning composition as a method for internalizing jazz language rather than merely producing output.
