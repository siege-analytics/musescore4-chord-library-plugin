# Prescriptive Lessons: Brent Vaartstra -- The Jazz Standards Playbook

## maj7 (4 lessons)

Vaartstra treats maj7 primarily as the tonic arrival point of ii-V-I progressions and as the target of backdoor dominant approaches. He emphasizes guide-tone isolation as the foundational practice before melodic improvisation, and explicitly licenses upper extensions (9, 11, 13) over any maj7 chord.

- **tonic-arrival-ii-V-I**: For maj7 as tonic resolution, Vaartstra prescribes isolating and playing the guide tones (3rd and 7th) in half or quarter notes through the entire song form before attempting melodic improvisation. "It's a great practice to play them isolated with either half notes or quarter notes through the entire form of a song." Chapter 2, p. 38.
- **tonic-I-chord**: When navigating I-i minor shifts (e.g., Cmaj7 to Cmin7), Vaartstra prescribes adjusting only the b3 and b7 -- the defining quality intervals -- while keeping all other notes constant. Chapter 8, p. 122.
- **tonic-with-upper-extensions**: Vaartstra explicitly licenses targeting upper extensions (9th, 11th, 13th) over any maj7 chord in improvisation. "Most chords in jazz are labeled as 7 chords. But this doesn't mean you can't add or target extensions and alterations of these chords." Chapter 8, p. 132.
- **backdoor-dominant-target**: Vaartstra prescribes recognizing maj7 chords as targets of the backdoor dominant device: the bVII7 approaches the I maj7 by whole step rather than the conventional V7. Chapter 11, p. 177.

## dom7 (6 lessons)

Vaartstra treats dom7 as the most functionally versatile chord quality. He teaches voice-leading discipline (7th resolves to 3rd of the next chord), secondary dominant recognition and expansion, tritone substitution, and the backdoor dominant device. The Mixolydian mode is the primary pitch collection for improvisation.

- **V7-in-ii-V-I**: For dominant 7 chords in ii-V-I progressions, Vaartstra prescribes voice-leading the 7th of the dom7 to the 3rd of the next chord by stepwise motion. "Notice how the 7th of each chord resolves to the 3rd of the next chord... That's voice leading." Chapter 2, p. 37.
- **secondary-dominant-tonicization**: Vaartstra prescribes treating any dom7 that precedes a non-tonic diatonic chord as a secondary dominant, and expanding it with a ii chord in front to form a ii-V. "Where there is a V chord, you can almost always add a ii in front of it." Chapter 5, p. 78; Chapter 10, p. 158.
- **diatonic-minor-converted-to-dominant**: Vaartstra prescribes freely converting diatonic minor chords to dominant 7ths as a core jazz harmonic practice. "It is entirely normal and acceptable to turn minor chords in the diatonic series into dominant 7th chords." Chapter 6, p. 95; Chapter 2, p. 33.
- **mixolydian-pitch-collection**: For improvising over dom7, Vaartstra prescribes the Mixolydian scale as the primary pitch collection: "a major scale with a b7. Formula: 1-2-3-4-5-6-b7." Students use it as a pitch collection, not a linear drill. Chapter 6, p. 98-99.
- **tritone-substitute-target**: Vaartstra prescribes recognizing that any dominant 7 chord may be replaced by the dominant 7 chord whose root lies a tritone away, also readable as a chromatic passing chord between ii and I. Chapter 8, p. 122.
- **backdoor-bVII7-substitute**: Vaartstra prescribes the backdoor dominant as a V7 substitute: the bVII7 approaches the tonic by whole step and may be preceded by its own ii chord. "The Bbmin7-Eb7 is really acting as a backdoor ii-V into the Fmaj7." Chapter 11, p. 177-178.

## min7 (6 lessons)

Vaartstra covers min7 in multiple functional contexts: as the ii chord in ii-V-I (with nearest-chord-tone voice leading), as the vi chord initiating cycles of 4ths, as a Dorian modal tonic, and as the iv minor substitute. One proscriptive lesson bans major 3rds/7ths over the iv minor chord.

- **ii-chord-in-ii-V-I**: For min7 in its ii-chord function, Vaartstra prescribes entering the arpeggio on whichever chord tone is nearest to the last note played in the prior chord. "Whatever the last note played in a particular bar was, I move to the nearest chord tone of the next chord." Chapter 4, p. 65.
- **vi-chord-cycling-4ths-opener**: Vaartstra prescribes treating a song-opening vi min7 as the initiator of a diatonic cycle of 4ths rather than as an isolated tonic substitute. Chapter 10, p. 158.
- **dorian-modal-tonic**: For min7 chords functioning as modal tonics (e.g., D Dorian in So What), Vaartstra prescribes abandoning the guide-tone framework entirely and using the Dorian mode as the pitch collection. "There is no need to identify the guide tones... the best way to improvise over modal harmony is by using modes." Chapter 7, p. 103-108.
- **minor-iv-substitute**: Vaartstra prescribes the minor iv chord as a substitute for the plain V7 before the I chord, expandable to a full backdoor ii-V or compressible to a single bVII7. Chapter 5, p. 80.
- **iv-minor-avoid-major-intervals** [PROSCRIPTIVE]: Vaartstra proscribes using major 3rds or major 7ths when improvising over the iv minor chord. "Don't be playing major thirds or major 7ths!" Chapter 4, p. 63.
- **cycle-of-4ths-minor-ii-V-closing**: Vaartstra prescribes distinguishing a closing cycle-of-4ths built from minor ii-Vs from the more common dominant ii-V cycle. Chapter 11, p. 179.

## min7b5 (3 lessons)

Vaartstra treats min7b5 as the ii chord in minor-key ii-V-i and hybrid ii-V-I progressions. He teaches a dual reading (ii7b5 of secondary key vs iiib5 of parent key) and emphasizes interpreting min7b5 through its resolution target rather than in isolation.

- **minor-ii-in-minor-ii-V-i**: Vaartstra prescribes treating min7b5 as the ii chord in minor-key ii-V-i progressions and in the hybrid ii-V-I construction (minor ii-V resolving to major I). "Example: Dmin7(b5)-G7(b9)-Cmaj7." Chapter 11, p. 179; Chapter 4, p. 64.
- **iiib5-dual-reading**: Vaartstra prescribes a dual reading of min7b5: as ii7b5 of the secondary key or as iiib5 within the parent tonal center. "The Gmin7(b5) can also be thought of as the iiib5." Chapter 5, p. 79.
- **improv-resolve-to-target**: Vaartstra prescribes interpreting min7b5 in context of its resolution target rather than in isolation. "If we look at them as resolving to an Fmin7, then we have something functional to understand." Chapter 5, p. 79.

## dom7b9 (2 lessons)

Vaartstra treats dom7b9 as the V chord in hybrid ii-V-I progressions borrowed from the parallel minor, and as analytically equivalent to a dim7 built on its 3rd.

- **V7b9-in-hybrid-ii-V-I**: Vaartstra prescribes using dom7b9 as the V chord in the hybrid ii-V-I, borrowed from the parallel minor key while resolving to the major I. Chapter 11, p. 179.
- **rootless-dim7-substitute**: Vaartstra prescribes treating a dom7b9 as analytically equivalent to a diminished 7 on its 3rd: "F#dim7: F#-A-C-Eb / D7(b9): D-F#-A-C-Eb... You can think of the F#dim7 as a rootless D7(b9)." Chapter 9, p. 141.

## dim7 (3 lessons)

Vaartstra teaches dim7 primarily as a passing chord with dual function: rootless dominant b9 substitute and chromatic bass connector. He provides the Whole-Half diminished scale as the theoretical note resource and distinguishes the biii diminished as a pure chromatic connector.

- **passing-chromatic-dim7**: Vaartstra prescribes understanding a passing dim7 in two ways: as a rootless dominant b9 substitute and as a chromatic passing chord connecting adjacent diatonic chords by bass half-step. Chapter 9, p. 142.
- **improv-treat-as-dominant-b9**: For improvisation over passing dim7 chords, Vaartstra prescribes targeting the chord tones of the implied dominant b9. The Whole-Half diminished scale (W-H-W-H-W-H-W-H) is the theoretical resource. Chapter 9, p. 145.
- **biii-passing-connector**: Vaartstra prescribes reading a biii diminished 7 in a cycle-of-4ths context as a purely chromatic connector rather than a dominant substitute. "Its primary job is to connect the iii chord to the ii chord chromatically." Chapter 10, p. 161.

## min-maj7 (1 lesson)

Vaartstra addresses the i minor substitution on a major-key I chord as a deliberate quality shift requiring only b3 and b7 adjustment.

- **i-minor-substitution-on-I-chord**: Vaartstra prescribes recognizing the i minor chord substitution on a major-key I chord (e.g., Cmin7 replacing Cmaj7) as a deliberate quality shift and adjusting only the b3 and b7 in melodic lines. Chapter 8, p. 122.

## sus4 (1 lesson)

Vaartstra addresses suspended/quartal quality implicitly through the So What modal voicing, prescribing Dorian mode over stacked-4ths voicings with no guide-tone framework.

- **modal-so-what-voicing**: Vaartstra prescribes using D Dorian as the pitch collection over the modal chord structure of So What -- a stacked-4ths voicing implying suspended quality. Guide tones are explicitly not required for modal voicings. Chapter 7, p. 103-114.
