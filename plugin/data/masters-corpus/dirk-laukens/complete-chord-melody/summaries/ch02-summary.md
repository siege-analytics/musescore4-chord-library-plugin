# Chapter 2 — Listening Exercises - Great Chord Melody Renditions

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 establishes listening exercises as a non-negotiable component of jazz guitar study, requiring students to actively engage with recorded chord melody renditions rather than skipping this foundational ear-training step. The chapter introduces two core performance contexts that structure the course's later instruction: the solo guitar chord melody approach, where a single guitarist plays unaccompanied, and the trio chord melody approach, where the guitarist performs the melody and chords alongside accompanying instruments such as bass and drums. Through practical examples of complete performances that demonstrate both approaches in sequence, the chapter establishes the terminology and conceptual distinction between these two settings, preparing students to understand how chord melody application differs depending on whether they are playing alone or within an ensemble context. The master's pedagogical move here is foundational rather than technical — framing listening not as optional supplementary work but as essential study discipline, and creating clear categorical language (solo vs. trio) that students will reference throughout their development.
