# Chapter 4 — MODULE 2 - CHORD VOCABULARY

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch04.md`._

Chapter 4 teaches chord vocabulary for jazz guitar through a structured approach anchored in the ii-V-I progression, which is presented as containing one chord from each harmonic family (minor ii, dominant V, major I). The foundational technical principles include: chords are constructed by stacking thirds; chord symbols encode interval formulas (e.g., Maj7 = 1-3-5-7); extensions like 9, 11, and 13 refer to second-octave intervals; and voicings can omit notes (especially the 5th, root, 11th, or 9th) to remain playable on guitar. The method's characteristic moves are visible in its practical fretboard strategy — using the CAGED system to anchor chord collections across the neck, organizing separate libraries for melodies on both the 1st and 2nd strings, and generating multiple voicings from a single shape by adding or adapting notes. A critical operational rule runs throughout: melody intervals must always relate to the current chord in the progression, not the overall key — a distinction the chapter presents as the 'golden rule' that governs voicing selection in chord melody arranging. The chapter also introduces slash-chord notation and establishes that chord identity depends partly on harmonic context, not pitch content alone.
