# Chapter 7 — MODULE 5 - MORE ADVANCED ARRANGING TECHNIQUES

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch07.md`._

Chapter 7 teaches advanced arranging techniques that expand beyond basic substitutions to include back cycling (adding chords via the circle of fourths, though with caution in band settings), common tone substitution (matching chord substitutions by shared melody notes), and strategic lick swaps — replacing repeated melodic figures with jazz licks that match the underlying harmonic progression, with the caveat that complex substitutions should appear later in the form for listener comprehension. The chapter emphasizes Joe Pass's characteristic use of rubato for dramatic effect and introduces the critical fifty-fifty principle: arranging is only half the work, with the other half being the performance itself. Practical mastery requires methodical bar-by-bar practice (1-2 bars per session with careful fingering verification) and progressive metronome training (beats 1-3, then 2-4) to avoid the solo guitar rhythm pitfall of lagging behind the beat, which creates a 'sleepy' feel and undermines the arrangement's effectiveness.
