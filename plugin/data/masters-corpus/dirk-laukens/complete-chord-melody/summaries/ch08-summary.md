# Chapter 8 — APPENDIX 1 - CHORD DICTIONARIES

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch08.md`._

Chapter 8 is a reference appendix establishing the foundational fingerstyle vocabulary and biomechanics for jazz guitar, introducing the Spanish-derived finger-naming convention (p, i, m, a, c) that labels all subsequent exercises, alongside four concrete wrist-placement rules for injury prevention and tone control. The master defines the two primary plucking strokes — rest stroke for initial security and free stroke as the preferred jazz technique — and specifies the thumb's functional role as a bass-note player rather than a melodic voice, with motion originating from the lowest thumb joint in a rowing-paddle motion. Walking finger alternation (i-m or i-a combinations) and string crossing are presented as core technical skills with a candid personal recommendation favoring i-a for speed, tempered by an acknowledgment that individual hand anatomy varies; the Moonwalker exercise exemplifies the master's pedagogical efficiency by consolidating two separate skills (walking fingers and string crossing) into a single practice task. The chapter introduces essential notation ('sim' for continuation) and anchors all practice protocols in explicit, measurable criteria — five-minute daily sessions until motion feels smooth, then mastery before progression — revealing a systematic approach to skill sequencing that balances efficiency with individualized learning.
