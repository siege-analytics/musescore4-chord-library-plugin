# Chapter 9 — APPENDIX 2 - FINGERSTYLE TRAINING

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch09.md`._

Chapter 9 (Appendix 2) teaches fingerstyle plucking technique through systematic hand-position rules and multi-string coordination exercises. The core pedagogical moves are establishing preferred finger pairs (p and i, or i and m) for adjacent strings, enforcing simultaneous note onset to avoid ragged arpeggiation, and introducing string-skip fingering for playing 6th intervals. The master's characteristic pragmatism appears throughout: limiting voicings to four notes maximum, rarely using the weak little finger, legitimizing the thumb double-pluck as a deliberate simplification rather than a flaw, and prescribing mirror self-monitoring to catch hand-position errors. Key load-bearing terminology includes 'string skip,' 'thumb independence,' and the specific finger combinations (pim for three notes, ia for skips). The chapter's progressive structure moves from isolated single-chord pattern practice toward full chord progressions, with a standing mandate to vary finger combinations on repeats to expand picking vocabulary and develop mechanical fluency.
