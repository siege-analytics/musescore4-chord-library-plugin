# Prescriptive lessons -- heussenstamm-silbergleit x goldmine-100-jazz-lessons

## maj7

Heussenstamm treats maj7 exclusively as the I-chord arrival point in major-key ii-V-I, with the 7th-to-3rd voice-leading rule governing how lines land on it. The Lydian scale is the prescriptive alternative to plain major over maj7#11, but with a sharp constraint: avoid sustaining the tonic and 5th, and use B minor pentatonic as the practical shortcut. The half-step-above outside device (Dbmaj7 -> Cmaj7) is justified through tritone-substitution logic, not random chromaticism.

- [ch.2 p.9] Maj7 is the I chord in major ii-V-I; C major scale applies; the chord extends to maj9 for arpeggio exercises; the 7th-to-3rd voice-leading rule governs melodic arrival.
- [ch.6 p.56-57] Lydian generates maj7#11 via stacked thirds; B minor pentatonic is the practical soloing shortcut over Cmaj7#11, avoiding dissonant tonic and 5th collisions.
- [ch.6 p.57] [PROSCRIPTIVE] Sustaining the tonic and 5th of C Lydian over Cmaj7#11 produces dissonance -- omit them and use B minor pentatonic instead.
- [ch.7 p.86] Approaching Cmaj7 from Dbmaj7 (one fret above) is an outside device; Dbmaj7 functions as a tritone sub of G7, so the half-step slide carries V-I harmonic weight.

## min7

Heussenstamm builds the min7 teaching in three layers: chord-tone arpeggios first, Dorian as the scalar foundation second, and upper-extension access via chord-within-chord substitution third. Shell voicings strip min7 to root/3rd/7th then drop the root for two-string middle-string comping. The sus4 equivalence (G7sus4 = Dm7 over G) extends min7 ideas into dominant territory as an improv resource.

- [ch.2 p.9,14] Min7 is the ii chord in major-key ii-V-I; chord-tone-only arpeggio improvisation through the 9th (e.g., Cm9) is the first prescribed melodic approach.
- [ch.2 p.9,14] D Dorian is the prescribed scale for ii min7; arpeggio built from scale degrees 1-3-5-7 yields root/3rd/5th/b7; this is the scalar foundation before chord-tone exercises.
- [ch.2 p.10] In a minor ii-V-i, the ii chord is min7b5 (half-diminished), not plain min7 -- Locrian is its scale.
- [ch.5 p.48] Shell voicing prescription: reduce min7 to root/3rd/7th, then drop the root for compact two-string middle-string shapes alongside a walking bassist.
- [ch.6 p.55] Dorian (not natural minor) is the correct scale for deriving full extensions -- its scalar 6th becomes the chordal 13th, extending Dm7 to Dm13.
- [ch.6 p.55] Chord-within-chord improv: solo over Dm7 using Am9 arpeggio tones (A-C-E-G-B), which map exactly to Dm13's upper extensions from the 5th upward.
- [ch.6 p.69] Dm7 or Dm9 ideas fit directly over G7sus4 because the sus4 chord is structurally equivalent to a minor seventh built on its 5th -- accessing G9sus4 and G13sus4 extensions naturally.

## dom7

This is the book's most heavily prescribed quality. Heussenstamm layers scale choices in a strict priority order: Mixolydian as default, Lydian dominant to avoid the 4th-vs.-3rd clash, bebop dominant for Parker vocabulary, and the half-step/whole-step diminished for a separate coloristic register -- all paired with explicit restraint caveats on the last two. Tritone substitution is treated as a structural transformation rather than a voicing trick, governing shell shapes, turnarounds, and melodic descent simultaneously.

- [ch.2 p.9] G Mixolydian is the default dom7 scale; Dorian, Mixolydian, and major are modal siblings sharing the same tones -- context determines which label applies.
- [ch.4 p.30-31] Decision rule: bare dom7 symbol (e.g., "F7") requires choosing natural or altered. V-i to minor favors altered; V-I to major defaults to natural unless the natural-to-altered device is desired.
- [ch.4 p.32-33] Two-stage scalar move over a resolving dominant: open with Mixolydian, switch to super Locrian immediately before resolution to inject urgency via the distinctive altered tones.
- [ch.3 p.17] Tritone substitution: replace any dom7 with one rooted a tritone away -- b5 pivot point where both chords share the same tones enharmonically -- converting circle-of-fifths motion to chromatic bass descent.
- [ch.3 p.17] Over a tritone-substituted dominant, prescribe descending scalar tones 5-3-2-1 per chord, producing an overall half-step descent through the progression.
- [ch.3 p.18] Tritone substitution extends to whole ii-V pairs: substitute an entire ii-V unit, optionally playing the original pair first and inserting the sub pair before resolution.
- [ch.3 p.18] Tritone-sub turnarounds produce parallel chromatic movement of dominant-13 voicings (e.g., Db13 replacing G7#9) -- logically derived, not random sliding.
- [ch.5 p.46-47] Straight-4 Freddie Green comping: minimal voicing size, precise release timing between chords (not blurred), low volume -- "felt more than heard."
- [ch.5 p.48-49] Dom7 shell voicing: root/3rd/7th on two middle strings, root dropped; tritone-sub dom7 shares the exact same shape with 3rd and 7th inverted.
- [ch.8 p.93] Lydian dominant (Mixolydian + raised 4th) over unaltered dom7 eliminates the natural-4th clash with the chordal 3rd while accommodating #11.
- [ch.10 p.134] Two contrasting colors over a single dom7: minor pentatonic for blues color, Mixolydian for jazz color -- switching mid-passage without a chord change is a prescribed technique.
- [ch.10 p.142] Bebop dominant scale (Mixolydian + natural 7th passing tone) is Charlie Parker's tool; master it, do not overuse it.
- [ch.10 p.143 / ch.11 p.152] Half-step/whole-step diminished scale applies over dom7 (same generative scale); three-fret equivalence means one shape covers four keys. Mastery is mandatory; overuse is proscribed.
- [ch.10 p.143] [PROSCRIPTIVE] "Don't overuse this scale" -- the restraint rule is paired explicitly with the mastery imperative.
- [ch.11 p.145] Four tension tones (b5, #5, b9, #9) are the complete altered-dominant toolkit -- all other tones already exist in the native dominant scale; mastering these four is the core altered-dominant task.
- [ch.11 p.145] Bb Mixolydian as starting point for dom7 improvisation; introduces the lead-in tone -- a chromatic half-step approach note below the root.

## dom7alt

Heussenstamm treats altered dominants as functionally distinct from plain dom7, with super Locrian as the single master scale unifying all four altered tones. The strongest prescriptive rule in the book is categorical: V-i motion to a minor chord strongly favors an altered dominant. Melodic minor is the scalar parent that connects altered dominant, Lydian dominant, and min-maj7 into one system.

- [ch.2 p.10 / ch.4 p.31] V-i in minor strongly favors an altered dominant (at minimum b9 or #9); super Locrian is the corresponding scale -- this is the book's strongest single altered-dominant rule.
- [ch.1 p.6 / ch.11 p.160] Super Locrian (seventh mode of melodic minor, built a half step above the altered root) is the master altered scale capturing all four tension tones plus root/3rd/b7; G super Locrian = Ab melodic minor.
- [ch.3 p.21] Minor blues dominant pairing: bVI is natural dominant (e.g., Bb9/Bb13), V is altered dominant (e.g., D7#9/D7b5) -- presented as "the normal pattern."
- [ch.6 p.62] Bb melodic minor as scalar unifier: starting from A it yields A altered/super Locrian; starting from Bb it yields Eb Lydian dominant -- one parent scale covers the full altered dominant territory.

## dom7b9

Heussenstamm treats dom7b9 and dom7#9 as interchangeable voicings whose exchange generates inner-voice melodic motion in comping. The 7th-to-3rd guide-tone resolution is identified as the fundamental voice-leading move between dominant and resolution, applicable to both natural and altered dominant contexts.

- [ch.8 p.90] Dom7#9 and dom7b9 are interchangeable; swapping between Bb7#9b5 and Bb7b9#5 produces a half-step melodic line on the high E string -- inner-voice motion as a comping device.
- [ch.3 p.22] Guide-tone rule: the 7th of one chord resolves to the 3rd of the next (e.g., G as 7th of Am7b5 leads to F# as 3rd of D7b9); these tones are the prescribed melodic targets at chord changes.

## min7b5

Heussenstamm gives min7b5 a clear, narrow role: the ii chord in minor ii-V-i, with Locrian as its scale. A secondary teaching maps the multi-functional nature of minor melodic shapes across chord types, with min7b5 as one of the simultaneous target chords.

- [ch.2 p.10] Min7b5 (half-diminished) is the ii chord in minor ii-V-i; Locrian is prescribed -- the same note set as natural minor from the tonic.
- [ch.6 p.62] A minor melodic shape starting on the 9th of a minor chord simultaneously functions as the #9th of an altered dominant, the 13th of a natural dominant, and the 11th of a min7b5 -- prescribed as a multi-chord versatility awareness tool.

## sus4

Heussenstamm defines the jazz sus4 as 7sus4, not plain sus4, and the single most important behavioral rule is: do not resolve it to the major 3rd. The min7-on-5th equivalence is the structural insight driving both voicing and improv choices. Mixolydian applies for soloing but with an inverted caution -- the 4th is welcome, while the 3rd is the danger note.

- [ch.6 p.66] 7sus4 is the jazz form of the suspended chord; in jazz it typically remains unresolved -- the pop/classical instinct to resolve to the major 3rd must be suppressed.
- [ch.6 p.66] [PROSCRIPTIVE] Resolving 7sus4 to the major 3rd undermines the idiomatic jazz sonority -- leave it suspended.
- [ch.6 p.66,69] G7sus4 = Dm7 over G structurally; Dm7 and Dm9 improv ideas fit hand-in-glove, naturally accessing G9sus4 and G13sus4 extensions.
- [ch.6 p.68] Mixolydian for soloing over 7sus4, but with an inverted caution: the 4th is welcome (it is the chord tone), while the scalar 3rd requires caution.
- [ch.8 p.90] Inner-voice sus4 comping device: move the chordal 3rd up a half step to the 4th and back, creating a momentary 7sus4 sound within a held chord voicing.

## dim7

Heussenstamm gives diminished chords two prescriptions: the three-fret equivalence rule (one shape, four keys) as a fretboard efficiency principle, and harmonic minor as the improvisation scale via the vii-dim-within-harmonic-minor derivation.

- [ch.11 p.154] Three-fret equivalence: the same diminished voicing repeats every minor 3rd, covering four keys simultaneously (e.g., B/D/F/G# dim are the same four notes in inversion) -- learn one shape, get four keys.
- [ch.11 p.154] Harmonic minor is prescribed over diminished chords; the justification is derivational -- the diminished chord occurs as the seventh degree of harmonic minor (e.g., A harmonic minor over G dim).

## min-maj7

Heussenstamm treats min-maj7 as a theoretical outcome of harmonized melodic minor rather than a standalone comping target -- its role is to identify the parent scale and anchor the broader altered dominant system.

- [ch.11 p.158] Min-maj7 is the tonic chord of the melodic minor scale (harmonized A melodic minor: i = Am(maj7)); formula = major scale with b3 (1-2-b3-4-5-6-7); its identification anchors the melodic minor harmonization system that generates super Locrian and Lydian dominant from the same parent.
