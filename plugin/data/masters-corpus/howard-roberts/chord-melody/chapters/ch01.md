## Rule: choosing strings for sustain and tone

> As a rule, the musical quality can be enhanced by choosing a fingering or a set of strings in which the greater string length is maintained, thus permitting the Strings to "sing" and sustain longer.

**Significance (p. 4).** Prescribes a concrete rule for string selection: prefer the string set that yields greater vibrating length.

## Rule: avoiding unnecessary hand/finger movements

> Mechanical efficiency is primarily a matter of avoiding unnecessary or awkward hand or finger movements.

**Significance (p. 4).** Defines mechanical efficiency as the guiding principle for minimizing wasted motion.

## Principle: do not fear radical jumps or large intervals

> in developing a sense of mechanical efficiency. do not also develop a fear of radical jumps or large intervals on the fingerboard. for these techniques are frequently required. For example, radical interval jumps may supply a nice contrast to smooth scale-wise line movement.

**Significance (p. 4).** Counters over-reliance on efficiency by establishing that large position shifts are legitimate techniques.

## Notation convention: string numbers and fingering numbers

> String numbers indicate the lower note of the chord and are in circles under the staff. Fingering numbers are placed to the left of the notes. The thumb is frequently used throughout the studies and is indicated (T).

**Significance (p. 4).** Defines the notational system used throughout the method for communicating string choice, fingering, and thumb use.
