# Chapter 1 — Fingerings

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch01.md`._

Chapter 1 introduces the two-part evaluative framework — musical quality and mechanical efficiency — that governs every fingering decision throughout the method. On the musical side, Roberts prescribes a concrete string-selection rule: favor the fingering that keeps the greatest vibrating string length, allowing notes to sing and sustain rather than cutting them short by moving to a higher string. Mechanical efficiency, in turn, is defined as the disciplined elimination of unnecessary or awkward hand and finger movements. Characteristically, Roberts immediately tempers that efficiency principle with a counterweight: players should not cultivate a fear of radical position jumps or large interval leaps, since these are legitimate and frequently expressive techniques that provide contrast to smooth scalar movement. The chapter closes by establishing the notational conventions — string numbers in circles below the staff, fingering numbers left of the notes, and T for the thumb — that the reader will need to decode every exercise that follows. Roberts signature move here is the paired-principle structure: he never states one criterion without immediately qualifying or balancing it with another, preventing the student from collapsing a nuanced judgment into a single rule.
