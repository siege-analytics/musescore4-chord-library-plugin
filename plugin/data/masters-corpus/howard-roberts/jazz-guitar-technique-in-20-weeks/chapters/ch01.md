## String gauge and accuracy

> Extremely nar-
> row gauge strings do not create enough resistance or "return" to
> their center position quickly enough to permit the right hand to de-
> velope predictable accuracy, or to strengthen the left hand. So, use
> a medium-heavy set of strings; nothing smaller than an .012 first
> string, etc.

**Significance (p. 3).** Establishes the method's core equipment rule linking string gauge to development of both hands.

## Action height rationale

> The strings should be raised to a medium-high action.
> There are three reasons for this: (1) to eliminate buzzes, (2) to help.
> strengthen the left hand, and (3) to develop accuracy for the right
> hand.

**Significance (p. 3).** Ties medium-high action to three functional training outcomes.

## Neck and fret condition

> The neck should be inspected to assure that it is straight. The frets
>
> should be dressed down, eliminating high and low spots to avoid buzzes.

**Significance (p. 3).** Baseline hardware prerequisite for accuracy goals.

## Note clarity standard

> After adjusting your guitar, make sure that each note rings loud and
> clear; just like a grand piano, over the entire range of the fingerboard.

**Significance (p. 3).** Verification criterion for proper instrument setup.

## Pick selection

> Your pick should be of medium size, and medium to heavy in thickness. |
> Avoid very large or odd shaped picks. Standard celluloid picks are
>
> well suited to this purpose.

**Significance (p. 4).** Concrete pick spec with explicit avoidance rule.

## Essential practice equipment

> Also essential to the studies in this book will be: (1) a metronome, (2)
> a reel-to-reel or cassette tape recorder, and (3) an alarm clock or timer.

**Significance (p. 4).** Specifies non-guitar tools for timed, recorded, self-monitored practice.

## Speed as byproduct of accuracy

> Speed comes as a by-product of
> accuracy. Without accuracy, speed is unattainable.

**Significance (p. 3).** Foundational pedagogical principle of the method.
