## Program structure overview

> The Program is made up of a series of project lessons, each lasting
>
> 50 minutes per day, running six consecutive days per week, with
>
> one day off.

**Significance (p. 14).** Defines the foundational daily and weekly practice regimen that the entire 20-week program rests on.

## Tempo goal

> The objective is to gradually increase the tempo over
>
> the course of 20 weeks to reach a metronome setting of approximately
> = 126 (maintaining precision and accuracy at all times).

**Significance (p. 14).** States the program's measurable endpoint and the critical qualifier that speed must never come at the expense of precision.

## Duple time focus weeks 1-6

> The First Six Weeks, Project Lessons 1-A, 1-B, 2-A, 2-B, 3-A, 3-B,
> focus on the use of eighth notes, as representative of duple time,
> sixteenth notes, thirty-second notes, etc.

**Significance (p. 14).** Identifies the rhythmic scope of the first phase, anchoring duple subdivisions to eighth notes as the primary vehicle.

## Transposition stimulates ideas

> Project Lesson 1-B uses the same chord progression transposed to
> another key, thus requiring a change of fingering patterns, licks,
>
> etc. This also tends to stimulate fresh approaches and ideas.

**Significance (p. 14).** Explains the pedagogical rationale for key transposition: it forces new fingering solutions and creative thinking, not merely mechanical repetition.

## Triple time focus weeks 8-13

> Weeks Eight Through Thirteen, Project Lessons 4-A, 4-B, 5-A, 5-B,
>
> 6-A, 6-B focus on triple time via eighth note triplets as representative
>
> of their relative divisions and sub-divisions, i.e., quarter note triplets,
> sixteenth note triplets, etc.

**Significance (p. 14).** Marks the pivot from duple to triple time, establishing triplet feel as the organizing principle for the program's second phase.

## Weeks 15-17 tempo solidification

> Review the projecat lessons in any order to establish your present
> maximum tempo as a permanent reflex.

**Significance (p. 15).** Introduces the concept of tempo as a motor reflex rather than a conscious effort, which is the target neurological outcome of the review weeks.

## Legato techniques boost speed

> Review the project lessons as before using (a) hammer-on's
> (b)pull-off's (c) glissandi (slides). The introduction of these
> techniques should cause your technique 'and speed to increase
> dramatically in that via these devices several notes can be pro-
> duced with one picking stroke.

**Significance (p. 15).** Prescribes the specific legato devices for the final phase and explains the mechanical reason they accelerate speed: multiple notes per pick stroke.

## Appendix as line models
