## metronome as progress tracker

> Do not set your metronome to a desired speed and try to rise to meet it. Rather,
> use the metronome only to track your progress. Keep a daily record of your
> tempos in the boxes provided. (NOTE: Your tempo may vary slower or faster
> from day to day. This is to be expected.)

**Significance (p. 19).** Reframes the metronome's pedagogical role from a target to a diagnostic tool, a conceptual distinction that directly shapes how students approach daily practice.

## practice regularity imperative

> Do each project lesson for six consecutive days, with one day off. Avoid
> skipping a day, for whatever reason. The effect is hazardous to progress.
> Regularity is essential. Follow the steps faithfully as given.

**Significance (p. 19).** States the method's non-negotiable structure for skill consolidation and explicitly warns that irregular practice is harmful, not merely suboptimal.

## key center analysis flexibility

> The key centers bracketed in the chord progressions represent only one analysis
> of the progression. Other views may be applied as well. For example, you will
> encounter key center brackets in which a given chord could be viewed as a III
> or V1 chord of one key, but indicated as a I! chord of another. In these situations,
> one's individual preference can prevail. It's just a matter of where you want the
> change of tonality to occur. Also, there are almost unlimited scale and chord
> substitution possibilities. Feel free to use any harmonic devices at your disposal.
> If they're right, they will sound right and if they're wrong, you will know it
> immediately.

**Significance (p. 19).** Teaches the student that harmonic analysis is interpretive rather than prescriptive and introduces the ear as the final arbiter of substitution choices.

## tempo goals vs. accuracy

> Care should be taken not to sacrifice accuracy
> and precision in order to meet the tempo goals.. These will always come In time.
> "The tempo objective plan is Symmetrical, but the learning curve is not.

**Significance (p. 19).** Articulates a core pedagogical principle that accuracy precedes speed and that the learning curve is inherently asymmetric, countering the student's natural impulse to rush toward tempo targets.

## alternate picking stroke equality

> Use Alternate Picking only. No two strokes in the same direction, i.e. strive
> to make up strokes sound as strong as down strokes.

**Significance (p. 19).** Defines the technical goal of alternate picking not merely as direction alternation but as dynamic equality between up and down strokes, which is the actual mechanical challenge students must internalize.
