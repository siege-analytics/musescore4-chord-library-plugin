# Chapter 2 — PICKING

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

Chapter 2 teaches the foundational equipment and physical mechanics required before beginning serious practice. The chapter prescribes specific gear — medium-heavy strings (.012 or larger), medium-high action, medium to heavy picks, and three essential practice tools (metronome, recorder, timer) — each chosen to develop right-hand accuracy and left-hand strength. It identifies the right hand as the primary limiting factor for improvisers and establishes **flexibility** as the governing principle, cautioning against rigid anchor systems (elbow, wrist, pick-guard anchors) that inhibit movement and create a speed ceiling under tension. Left-hand technique requires perpendicular fretting angles (like piano hammers), precise thumb positioning on the neck center, and economical finger lift to balance noise control with efficiency. The chapter's core technical law states that perfect **left-right hand synchronization** (no flams) is non-negotiable, diagnosed through slow play across the full fingerboard, and supported by a flat left-wrist posture to avoid strain.
