# Chapter 8 — HOW TO DO PROJECT LESSONS FOR WEEKS ONE THROUGH SIX

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch08.md`._

Chapter 8 teaches foundational practices for executing six-week project lessons through disciplined, methodical study. The key pedagogical moves include treating the **metronome as a progress tracker** rather than a speed target, maintaining **practice regularity** (six consecutive days with one day off — skipping is explicitly warned against), and prioritizing **accuracy over tempo**, recognizing that the learning curve is asymmetric and speed will follow precision naturally. Students learn **harmonic flexibility**, understanding that key-center analysis and chord substitution are interpretive choices guided by ear rather than prescriptive rules. The chapter also emphasizes **alternate picking stroke equality**, where the mechanical goal is dynamic parity between up and down strokes, not merely directional alternation. Together, these moves establish the method's structural discipline: consistent practice rhythm, ear-driven harmonic judgment, and the counterintuitive patience to slow down for accuracy.
