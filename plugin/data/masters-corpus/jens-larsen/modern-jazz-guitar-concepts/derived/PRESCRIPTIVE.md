# Prescriptive lessons -- jens-larsen x modern-jazz-guitar-concepts

## maj7

Larsen treats maj7 as a shared-tone substitution problem: the 3rd-rooted upper-structure arpeggio (e.g., Am7 over Fmaj7) is the primary tool because it maximizes note overlap. The minor pentatonic from the 3rd is the main scalar option, reframed as an extended arpeggio to keep the harmonic rationale explicit rather than habit-driven.

- [ch.1 p.14] Am7 over Fmaj7: three notes in common justify the 3rd-rooted arpeggio as the richest upper-structure substitution for tonic maj7.
- [ch.1 p.18] E Minor Pentatonic over Cmaj7, reframed as Dm7add11 (D F G A C / 1 b3 4 5 b7): the pentatonic is a disguised extended arpeggio, not a modal choice.
- [ch.4 p.57] Minor pentatonic from the 3rd over tonic major explicitly endorsed as a "great device," yielding maj7, 9, 3, 5, and 13 coloring.

## min7

Larsen treats min7 primarily as a II-chord function, organizing his prescriptions around two poles: arpeggio selection (3rd and 5th rooted options with different voice-leading implications) and chromatic approach (enclosure to the 3rd, Coltrane groupings). Tonic min7 gets separate treatment via Ebmaj7 inversions and quintal arpeggios for an open, modern sound.

- [ch.1 p.24] Coltrane 1-b3-4-5 grouping (minor variant) prescribed to break scalic lines and create interest over fast changes on min7.
- [ch.2 p.35] 5th-rooted arpeggio over II chord works but requires adding the chord's 3rd back in to clearly define the sound; omitting it risks ambiguity.
- [ch.2 p.36] 3rd-rooted arpeggio over min7 named as a "useful device to produce characteristic bebop lines."
- [ch.3 p.47] Two-note enclosure (diatonic above, chromatic below) applied to root and 3rd of Gm7; formula is portable to any chord tone.
- [ch.3 p.49] 5th-rooted arpeggio over II chord targets the 3rd of the following V chord -- a voice-leading device that implies a sus sound without leaving the key.
- [ch.3 p.50] Chromatic descent from a whole step above to the 3rd (C->Bb over Dm7) is a named common bebop device for II chord approach.
- [ch.3 p.52] Omitting the 3rd of Gm7 opens the sound toward G7sus4; contextual tolerance -- the surrounding harmonic frame justifies the omission.
- [ch.6 p.77] Ebmaj7 arpeggio over Cm7 beginning on the root but skipping down to G before ascending; attributed to Benson/Green as a characteristic tonic-minor phrase shape.
- [ch.6 p.78] Quintal arpeggio from Eb over Cm7 spells Cm11; preceded by diatonic enclosure of the Eb -- the open structure is the point, not extension density.

## dom7

Larsen's dominant treatment is organized around a clear hierarchy: m7b5-on-the-3rd is the single most important substitution, followed by 5th- and 7th-rooted options with increasing caution. Diminished-scale upper-structure triads (three of the four -- F excluded) form the main coloristic layer, while rhythmic devices (triplets, implied passing chords) add bebop momentum.

- [ch.1 p.14-15] Upper-structure arpeggio hierarchy: 3rd-rooted is most versatile; 5th- and 7th-rooted include fewer chord tones and require more care. The hierarchy governs all subsequent arpeggio choices.
- [ch.1 p.15] Bm7b5 over G7: the m7b5-on-the-3rd avoids the root, adds the 9th, and is so effective that "many musicians always use it instead of the original arpeggio." Named as the most common arpeggio use in jazz.
- [ch.1 p.23] [PROSCRIPTIVE] C over G7 = G7sus4 -- use deliberately or avoid by default. The 11th is not a free color; it changes the chord's identity.
- [ch.4 p.56] Triplets on 7th arpeggios named as a "great bebop trick" applicable in modern contexts; rhythmic variation implies unwritten passing chords.
- [ch.4 p.58] Implying unwritten passing chords (Gm7 arpeggio over Bb7alt approach) creates forward motion toward the chord change; the arpeggio shape, not the written harmony, drives the line.
- [ch.5 p.66-67] 13b9 dominant color expressed as a major upper-structure triad over the dom7 chord (D major triad over F7); guitarists think in triads, not scale degrees.
- [ch.5 p.70] Ab+D major triad pair over F7 conveys #9, b9, and 13; voice-leading transition between the two triads (Ab->A, Eb->D) is explicit.
- [ch.6 p.74] [PROSCRIPTIVE] F major triad (root-position) excluded from diminished-scale dom7 lines: "doesn't add any colour." Effective palette = Ab, B, D triads.
- [ch.6 p.75] First-inversion F major + B major triads over F7 highlight #11 and b9 extensions specifically; inversion choice matters for extension emphasis.
- [ch.6 p.82] Harmonic minor material over the V chord (Ddim arpeggio over Bb7) signals tonic resolution; the diminished arpeggio functions as a pull device, not a color device.

## dom7alt

Larsen treats altered dominants as a guide-tone filtering problem: of all arpeggios available from the altered scale (= 7th mode of melodic minor = tritone sub's melodic minor), only those containing both the 3rd and 7th of the original chord are primary candidates. G#m7b5 is named the single best arpeggio. Triad pairs (B+Ab in first inversion) maximize extension density for coloristic lines.

- [ch.4 p.53-54] Altered scale = 7th mode of B Melodic Minor over Bb7alt, reframed via the tritone sub (E7); this makes all B Melodic Minor arpeggios available as altered dominant material.
- [ch.4 p.54] Guide-tone filter: only arpeggios containing both the 3rd (D) and 7th (Ab) of Bb7 are primary. Two qualify: E7 and G#m7b5.
- [ch.4 p.54] G#m7b5 is "probably the best candidate" for Bb7alt -- maps as b7, b9, 3, b13. The m7b5-on-the-3rd rule from Chapter 1 is directly extended to the altered dominant context.
- [ch.4 p.61] Four-note chromatic enclosure over the preceding tonic (Fm7) delays arrival at the 3rd until beat 3 before continuing with Abmaj7; enclosure is used as a suspension device, not just an approach.
- [ch.6 p.77] B+Ab major triads (both first inversion) over F7alt add b9, #9, and #11 simultaneously; the inversion is load-bearing for the extension content.
- [ch.6 p.82] F7alt line admits two simultaneous valid readings: Ebm7b5 over the whole bar, or Gbm triad + Ebm7b5 inversion. Multiple analyses are endorsed; internalization of the system, not a single reading, is the goal.

## dom7b9

Larsen derives dom7b9 material directly from the harmonic minor scale of the target key, filtering arpeggios that convey b9/b13 color. The b9 and #9 can coexist in the same line, with the #9 functioning as a chromatic passing note to the 3rd.

- [ch.3 p.44] Recommended arpeggio list for C7b9/b13 from F Harmonic Minor: Cmaj, Edim, Gdim, Abaug, Bbdim, C7, Edim, Abmaj7#5. Selection criterion is harmonic minor compatibility + b9/b13 coloring.
- [ch.3 p.47] #9 (Eb) added alongside b9 over C7; the #9 functions as a chromatic passing note leading to the 3rd (E). Combining both altered ninths in one line is a named "common trick."
- [ch.3 p.50] Bbm7b5 is non-diatonic to F Harmonic Minor but constructible from its notes over C7; it imposes b7, b9, 3, and #5 -- "they sound great." Scale-derivability, not strict diatonicism, is the criterion.

## min7b5

Larsen treats min7b5 as a load-bearing upper-structure substitute: the m7b5-on-the-3rd rule (established for plain dom7 in Chapter 1) is the mechanism that makes G#m7b5 the primary altered dominant arpeggio. In solo analysis, min7b5 arpeggios also admit dual readings as part of Larsen's general endorsement of multiple simultaneous analyses.

- [ch.4 p.54] G#m7b5 over Bb7alt is the primary prescription: extends the Chapter 1 dom7 rule (m7b5 from the 3rd) directly into the altered dominant context, yielding b7, b9, 3, b13.
- [ch.6 p.82] Ebm7b5 over F7alt as either a whole-bar reading or the second half of a Gbm triad + Ebm7b5 split; both readings are valid. The arpeggio's ambiguity is a feature, not a problem.

## dim7

Larsen treats the diminished scale's dim7 structure primarily as a triad-extraction system rather than as scalar material. Minor-third symmetry is the explicit learning-load reducer: one triad shape, transposed three times, yields the full palette. Melodically, a phrase-repeat-with-alteration technique adapts dom7 material to a passing dim7 with minimal new material.

- [ch.5 p.63] The half-whole diminished scale viewed as two interlocking dim7 chords; the primary use is extracting its four embedded major triads (F, Ab, B, D) as melodic material rather than running the scale.
- [ch.5 p.65] Minor-third symmetry reduces learning to one triad shape: learn the triads on the first two scale degrees, then transpose in minor 3rds to complete the set.
- [ch.6 p.82] Over a passing Edim chord, restate the preceding Eb7 phrase with minimal alteration (F->E); phrase repetition with single-note adjustment is named as "a very common melodic trick."

## aug7

Larsen prescribes the augmented triad over dominant as a bop resolution device, noting its survival into modern styles. The aug7 arpeggio (C7#5) is also available as a non-diatonic but scale-compatible option from F Harmonic Minor, expanding the harmonic minor arpeggio palette.

- [ch.3 p.52] C7#5 constructible from F Harmonic Minor notes over C7; non-diatonic arpeggio justified by scale-note compatibility, consistent with the book's harmonic-derivation logic.
- [ch.4 p.60] Augmented triad resolution over the dominant is a "common bop trait that has carried over into more modern styles"; deploy the augmented triad on the dominant, then resolve it.
