# Prescriptive lessons — joe-pass × guitar-chords

## maj7
Pass treats the major-ninth sonority as the definitive tonic landing point, not the plain major-seventh. In the major-sounds progression the entire harmonic machinery — dominant, tritone substitute, chromatic motion — is aimed at depositing the ear on a Cma9 voicing. Reviewers should confirm whether this preference for ma9 over ma7 is stated explicitly or inferred from the example.

- [ch.2 p.15] Cma9 is named the tonic resolution target of the major-sounds progression: G13b5 (or its tritone substitute Db7+9b5) resolves into Cma9, establishing the major-ninth voicing as the canonical resting point rather than a plain major-seventh.

## dom7
Pass's dominant vocabulary is built on two interlocking principles: qualifier order in the chord symbol mirrors the vertical stack of voices (bottom to top), and common tones on top must ring across the bar line. Turnaround and cycle motion (back to G13, then through cycle-of-fifths sevenths) are the structural engine of the seventh-sounds section. Both principles apply simultaneously whenever a dominant leads somewhere.

- [ch.2 p.20] Chord-symbol qualifier order equals voice order bottom-to-top: D7+9+5 means the raised 9th sits directly above the D7 shell and the raised 5th crowns the voicing — the symbol is a literal map of the fretboard stack.
- [ch.2 p.24] Sustain lead notes between chord changes: moving from C9 to F13, the G on top is held, making the common tone a melodic anchor rather than a passive by-product of fingering.
- [ch.2 p.15] G7 anchors the seventh-sounds section via a substitute turnaround back to G13 followed by cycle-of-fifths seventh movement — the dominant is always in motion toward the next tonal target, never static.

## dom7alt
Altered dominants inhabit two distinct roles for Pass: tritone substitutes that are fully interchangeable with their parent dominant, and complex upper-structure voicings whose alteration layers are read directly off the chord symbol. Both usages reinforce the bottom-to-top symbol convention established in the dom7 section.

- [ch.2 p.15] Db7+9b5 is explicitly interchangeable with G13b5, confirming tritone substitution as a first-class harmonic option rather than a coloristic aside — the two chords swap freely in the major-sounds progression.
- [ch.2 p.20] D7+9+5 demonstrates the voicing-layer convention for altered dominants: raised-9th layer above the shell, raised-5th layer on top — the symbol encodes the physical voicing order.

## dim7
Pass deploys the diminished seventh in two related functions: as a multi-resolution approach chord that can pivot to more than one target, and as a direct substitute for any dominant-seventh-flat-nine chord. The multiple-resolution property is stated prescriptively, not merely illustrated by one example.

- [ch.2 p.15] Diminished chords substitute for 7b9 movement: the basic pattern A7—Dm—G7—C demonstrates that a diminished voicing can stand in for A7b9, collapsing the dominant-with-flat-nine into a symmetrical shape.
- [ch.2 p.15] Any diminished chord in the sequence can resolve to multiple targets: Pass states explicitly that "the last chord in this sequence" can move "into any chord," prescribing the diminished seventh as an open-ended pivot rather than a single-destination approach.

## aug7
The augmented chord functions as sustained chromatic color that precedes and prepares cycle resolution, not as a chord of momentary surprise. Pass describes the first six chords of an example sequence as "basically G aug," with chromatic motion used to navigate toward the cycle — the augmented sound is the environment, not the event.

- [ch.2 p.15] Augmented chords sustain as a chromatic color field before cycle resolution: "the first six chords are basically G aug" and chromatic movement is the mechanism for arriving at the basic cycle, positioning aug as a prolonged pre-cycle zone.
- [ch.2 p.15] An augmented chord can also serve as the terminal resolution target of a half-diminished-to-dominant cadence: A7b9 moves to Dm7b5, which resolves "finally to G aug" — the aug voicing absorbs cadential energy rather than generating it.

## min7
Pass treats the minor-seventh chord as an active ii-chord whose motion toward the dominant is sharpened by chromatic approach rather than plain stepwise movement. The Dm7—Ab13—G13 example shows the minor-seventh handing off to a chromatic (tritone-area) dominant before the diatonic dominant arrives.

- [ch.2 p.15] Minor-seventh as ii-chord with chromatic approach to the dominant: "movement in Dm7, resolving into Ab13 to G13" — the minor-seventh does not resolve directly to G13 but passes through the chromatic Ab13, adding a half-step compression before the diatonic dominant.

## min7b5
The half-diminished chord occupies the penultimate slot in a three-chord cadential unit (dominant-seventh-flat-nine → half-diminished → augmented), functioning as a prepared dissonance that tightens the pull toward its resolution target.

- [ch.2 p.15] Dm7b5 appears between A7b9 and G aug: "A7b9 to Dm7b5 resolving finally to G aug" — the half-diminished is the middle term of the cadence, receiving the flat-nine tension and channeling it into the augmented landing.

## dom7b9
The dominant-seventh-flat-nine initiates a compact three-chord cadential pattern in which it serves as the trigger chord, the half-diminished as the pivot, and the augmented as the resolution. Pass presents this sequence as a unit, implying the b9 dominant is inseparable from what follows it.

- [ch.2 p.15] A7b9 opens the half-diminished-to-augmented cadence: "A7b9 to Dm7b5 resolving finally to G aug" — the flat-nine alteration creates the maximum tension that the cadence then systematically releases across two subsequent chords rather than one.
