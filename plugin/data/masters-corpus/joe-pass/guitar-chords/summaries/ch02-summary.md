# Chapter 2 — CHORD PASSAGES

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch02.md`._

This chapter teaches chord passages—applied harmonic movement using the six chord-sound categories from Chapter 1—organized around seven distinct functional contexts: major-sound resolution via tritone substitution (Db7 to Cma9), minor seventh to seventh movement with chromatic approach chords, seventh-chord substitute turnarounds and cycle progressions, diminished chords as interchangeable approach chords with multiple resolution paths, diminished chords substituting for 7b9 sonorities, augmented sounds resolved chromatically into cycle cadences, and half-diminished to dominant and augmented resolutions. The characteristic moves visible here are chromatic bass-line motion, common-tone sustain of lead notes across changes, and the foundational jazz ii–V and tritone-substitution patterns. The chapter introduces the proprietary notation rule that chord-symbol order equals voice order (e.g., D7+9+5 means D7 with the raised 9th next and the raised 5th on top), establishes "approach chord" as a load-bearing concept for diminished harmony, and anchors all passages to recognizable harmonic skeletons like I–VI–II–V and the cycle of fifths.
