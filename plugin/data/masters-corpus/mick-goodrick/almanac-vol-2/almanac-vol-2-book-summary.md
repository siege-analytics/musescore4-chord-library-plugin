# Book-level distillation — almanac-vol-2

_Aggregated from per-chapter summaries under `summaries/`._

## The M-Lode: A Complete System for Voice-Led Harmony on Guitar

This volume is the second half of a two-volume method whose central ambition is exhaustiveness: to identify, notate, and systematically practice every 3-part and 4-part chord voicing available within the three diatonic scales — C Major, C Melodic Minor, and C Harmonic Minor. Chapter 3 establishes the foundational premise immediately: chords are deliberately left unnamed to preserve their harmonic flexibility across functions, and the five chord families introduced here (7th chords, TBN I, TBN II, 4-part 4ths, and spread clusters) constitute the complete set of 4-part possibilities within those three scale systems. The shift from Volume I's triad-based architecture to structures built on 4th and 2nd intervals is the defining structural move of the volume, producing greater harmonic ambiguity and functional range.

## The M-Lode: Definition and Scope

Chapter 4 names and closes the system: the M-Lode (Mother Lode) is the totality of all material across both volumes — triads and triad-derived structures from Volume I combined with the 3-part and 4-part structures of Volume II — voice-led through six cycles in each of the three scales. The master's characteristic move is to prove theoretical exhaustiveness: these five 4-part chord families are not merely a selection but the only 4-part families possible within seven-note diatonic scales. Chapter 16 quantifies the result: 20 different 3-part chords (240 voicings), 30+ different 4-part chords (800+ voicings), and 1,000+ total voicings, all found through systematic voice-leading. The master's foundational principle, stated explicitly in Chapter 16, is: *all of the M-Lode is based on voice-leading.*

## Quartal Harmony and the Five Applications

Chapter 5 introduces 3-part 4ths (quartal harmony) as structurally opposed to tertial triads: 4th voicings prioritize 4ths and 5ths and exclude 3rds and 6ths. A critical technical hazard is the diminished 4th enharmonic problem — the interval B to E♭ sounds like a major 3rd and can collapse quartal voicings into triadic sonorities. The chapter's guitar-specific solution is the drop-D pedal technique, using the low D string as a harmonic anchor to explore 4th-voicing cycles in D Dorian, where no avoid notes obstruct free exploration. Chapter 6 then establishes the five M-Lode applications — melodies, arpeggios, counterpoint, 3-part chords, and 4-part chords — as the complete taxonomy of how any harmonic material in the system can be deployed, paired with the arpeggio alternation rule: ascend on one chord, descend on the next.

## Voicing Techniques: Drop Voicings, Octave Insertion, and Physical Tools

Chapters 8 through 11 develop the voicing toolkit. Finger-style guitarists are given a structural advantage throughout: only they can access partial and full barres, selectively pluck required strings, and execute the most demanding voicing types. Octave insertion — placing an octave between any two voices within a 3-part or 4-part chord — is introduced in Chapter 8 and formalized in Chapter 9 as a generalizable principle applicable to both triadic and quartal voicings. Chapter 10 extends this into advanced hybrids: Drop 4, the Double Drop 2/Drop 4 (octave inserted between the middle two voices of a Drop 2), and Drop 3 and 4 as finger-style-only techniques. Chapter 11 introduces an index card practice tool — eight half-inch strips cut from a 4×6 card — for voice isolation and reduction work, exemplifying the method's consistent pairing of theoretical concepts with low-tech, tactile study aids.

## Generative Techniques: Passing Tones, Combinatorics, and Call-and-Response

Chapters 7, 12, and 13 teach methods for generating musical content from the voicing system. Chapter 7 prescribes exhaustive voice-combination practice: all 6 possible 2-voice combinations and all 4 possible 3-voice combinations within any 4-part structure, building systematic command of the M-Lode's internal relationships. Chapter 12 introduces Joe's 2 Plus 2 Thing — dividing a 4-part chord into two alternating 2-note groupings functioning as question-answer call-and-response pairs, treating chord construction as dialogue rather than static vertical structure. Chapter 13 establishes the passing tone rule as a single generative principle scaling from solo melody through full 4-part voicing: whenever any voice moves by a third, fill that interval with a passing tone; when two voices simultaneously move in thirds, both may receive passing tones.

## 3-Part Chord Families Beyond Triads and 4ths

Chapter 15 names three additional 3-part chord families within the M-Lode: 3-part Spread Clusters, 7th Chord (no 5th), and 7th Chord (no 3rd). All are derived mechanically — drop one voice from any 4-part chord, yielding four possible configurations. These voicings are characterized as among the most guitar-friendly in the system while retaining the characteristic voice-leading sound of the M-Lode. Prescriptive arpeggio guidance accompanies them: begin with 4-way close options, prefer C Harmonic Minor for its sonic quality, exploit all 24 possible arpeggio sequences per 4-part voicing, and apply up/down alternation across successive chords.

## The Notation Chapters: Dual Voice-Leading Grids Across Nine Scale/Family Combinations

Chapters 19 through 27 constitute the bulk of the book's content: systematic notation of every chord family through every cycle in every scale, organized as nine large chapters (three chord families × three scales). The master's signature analytical tool — the paired Intervallic/Functional Voice-Leading grid — governs every voicing entry. The intervallic grid states the interval of each voice's motion (2nd, 3rd, 4th, or common tone); the functional grid states the scale-degree destination of each voice. Common tones are marked *c.t.* and carry their own distinct analytical category. The M.S.R.P. (memorized note-sequence) drill loop appears at the 3-part 4ths level (Chapter 19) to enforce memorization. Chapters 19–21 complete 3-part 4ths in all three scales; Chapters 22–24 complete 4-part 4ths; Chapters 25–27 complete Spread Clusters — ending in C Harmonic Minor, which the method identifies as the most sonically rewarding and the most chromatically dense voice-leading environment in the entire system.
