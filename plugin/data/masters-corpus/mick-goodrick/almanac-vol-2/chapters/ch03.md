## Volume II chord naming rationale

> the reason that all the chords in Volume II are left nameless is that the names one
> might use get complicated. [Cmaj9th(noSth), for example.] Also, these names could easily tend
> to limit how the chords could be used.

**Significance (p. 7).** Explains the deliberate methodological choice to omit chord names in Volume II.

## 4ths and 2nds chord ambiguity

> Since the chords in Volume II revolve around structures of
> 4th intervals and 2nd intervals, they are more ambiguous sounding than the structures in Vol-
> ume I. Also, in many cases they are more flexible in terms of multiple functions.

**Significance (p. 7).** Defines the sonic and functional character of Volume II's harmonic vocabulary.

## Volume II retroactive application

> Volume II are retroactive. That is to say that they apply to both books.

## Volume II content overview

**Significance (p. 8).** Enumerates the structural content and three scale systems organizing the book.

## Volume I triad-based architecture

> If you look at all of the material in Volume I, you would see that
> it’s all triad-based. The triads are triads. The 7th chords are a triad plus another note. TBN Lis a

**Significance (p. 8).** Provides the unifying structural definition of Volume I's chord vocabulary.
