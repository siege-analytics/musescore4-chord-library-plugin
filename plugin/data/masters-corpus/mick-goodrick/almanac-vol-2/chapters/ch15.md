## 3-part chord families defined

> So far, within the fabric of the M-Lode, we have identified and used two 3-part chord
> families: Triads and 3-part 4th voicings. [If you remember, we described these as “supple-
> ments” for the 4-part chords.]

**Significance (p. 19).** Taxonomy grounding before introducing new families.

## Three additional 3-part families

> There are, in fact, 3 other members of the 3-part chord families. They would be the
> following: 3-part Spread Clusters, 7th Chord [no 5th] and 7th Chord [no 3rd]. These 3-part
> chords have not been IDENTIFIED as such, but they are there, within the M-Lode...(which is
> to say that they have been FOUND). All of them can be discovered by exploring the 3-part
> options of the 4-part chords. [If you remember, it just amounts to leaving out one of the
> voices...] There are 4 possible configurations.

**Significance (p. 19).** Names three additional 3-part chord types with derivation method.

## Guitar-friendly 3-part voicings

> These possibilities turn out to be some of the most “guitar-friendly” options for us
> guitarists. Almost all of them are much easier to play, from a guitarists’ perspective. And they
> still have that compelling “MUSIC-REPLICA” sound and effect, which is the result of the
> consistent [and, I believe, excellent] voice-leading.

**Significance (p. 19).** Pedagogical rationale for prioritizing these voicings.

## Arpeggio start: 4-way close

> I would suggest that you immediately look at all of the 4-way close options within the
> M-Lode. This is the easiest area to explore that gives the best initial [and playable] results.

**Significance (p. 20).** Prescriptive rule for where to begin arpeggio exploration.

## Harmonic Minor preference

> I would further encourage you to experiment with those options using the C
> Harmonic Minor Scale material. [Let’s face it; much of the time, IT JUST SOUNDS BETTER IN
> HARMONIC MINOR...]

**Significance (p. 20).** Aesthetic preference rule.

## 24 arpeggio sequences

> Keep in mind the following : Any [and ALL] 4-part voicings can be arpeggiated in 24
> different sequences...[simple fact, but HUGE implications]

**Significance (p. 20).** Combinatorial rule establishing arpeggio permutation scope.

## Up/down alternation

> You could start out with arpeggiating up one chord and then down the next. [Or the
> other way around...it all depends...you will have to experiment.]

**Significance (p. 20).** Directional alternation as beginner strategy.
