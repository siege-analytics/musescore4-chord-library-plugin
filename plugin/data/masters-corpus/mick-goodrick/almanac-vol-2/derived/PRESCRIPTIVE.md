# Prescriptive lessons — mick-goodrick × almanac-vol-2

## quartal

Goodrick's Volume II treats all chords structurally — not as named harmonic objects but as quartal (4th-based), tertial, or spread-cluster voicings traversed by voice-leading through six cycles in three diatonic scales (C Major, C Melodic Minor, C Harmonic Minor). His prescriptions operate at the voicing-family level: which intervals belong, which are excluded, which physical techniques unlock each voicing type, and how to traverse positions while preserving voice-leading economy. Specific chord qualities (maj7, min7, dom7) are addressed only implicitly via their diatonic position within a scale; the named target of every lesson is the quartal voicing or spread-cluster family.

- [ch.5 p.9] Harmonic family: quartal harmony is the structural opposite of tertial. Primary intervals are 4ths and 5ths; 3rds and 6ths sit as color/secondary intervals — the inverse of triads.
- [ch.5 p.10] Color-tone exclusion: the interval inventory of a 3-part 4th chord is "Two 2nds, Four 4ths, Two 5ths, One 7th"; 3rds and 6ths are explicitly missing.
- [ch.5 p.9] Proscriptive — enharmonic collapse: a quartal voicing containing B–E♭ (a diminished 4th that sounds as a major 3rd) collapses into a triadic sonority and "doesn't work very well in a 4th voicings context."
- [ch.5 p.10] Drop-D pedal exploration: tune the low E down a whole step and use the open D as a pedal while cycling through 4th-voicings in D Dorian; no avoid notes makes this the safest free-exploration context.
- [ch.5 p.10; ch.6 p.11] Melodic generation via arpeggiation: arpeggiate quartal voicings to discover melodies. The five M-Lode applications (melodies/arpeggios/counterpoint/3-part/4-part) all apply to quartal material.
- [ch.6 p.11; ch.15 p.20] Arpeggio direction alternation: ascend through one chord, descend through the next, alternating throughout a sequence.
- [ch.19 p.27] Voice-leading grid application: every voicing is analyzed in paired Intervallic/Functional grids — e.g. 3-part 4ths Cycle 6 Close shows "5→2nd / 2→2nd / 1→2nd" intervallic and "5→1 / 2→5 / 1→2" functional.
- [ch.19 p.27; ch.23 p.159] Common-tone preservation: c.t. is a distinct analytical category — voices that don't move are tracked explicitly. Cycle 7 Drop 2 in melodic minor shows three c.t. voices while only one moves by a 4th.
- [ch.8 p.14; ch.10 p.15] Fingerstyle application requirement: Drop 3 and 4 is fingerstyle-only; Double Drop 2/Drop 4 favors fingerstyle for the octave between the middle two voices.
- [ch.13 p.17–18] Passing-tone third fill: when any voice moves by a 3rd between adjacent positions, fill with a passing tone; applies to solo melody, counterpoint, 3-part, and 4-part voicings.
- [ch.16 p.22] Proscriptive — spread cluster physical caution: "BE VERY CAREFUL!!!" — many spread clusters are physically demanding; approach with preparation.
- [ch.15 p.20; ch.16 p.21] Density floor at 4-way close: begin practice with 4-way close voicings; seek open-string playable versions wherever possible.
- [ch.15 p.20] Harmonic Minor scale preference: when in doubt, prefer C Harmonic Minor — "IT JUST SOUNDS BETTER IN HARMONIC MINOR."
- [ch.8 p.14; ch.10 p.15] Octave insertion voicing expansion: place an octave between any two voices to derive new voicing variants (e.g. Double Drop 2/Drop 4, Drop 3 and 4). Applies to triadic and quartal voicings alike.
- [ch.7 p.12; ch.12 p.16] Voice-combination practice: play all 6 two-voice and all 4 three-voice subsets of every 4-part chord; Joe's 2 Plus 2 Thing divides the 4-voice chord into two alternating 2-note call-and-response groupings.
