**Significance (p. 161).** Establishes the key conceptual distinction between specific chromatic voice-leading (used in the Grip-Slipping exercises) and the generic diatonic voice-leading of the prior volumes.

## Grip-Slipping defined

> "Grip-Slipping" is a term I use to describe a series of exercises that I sometimes give to
> my guitar students to help them learn all the inversions of different chords. Without giving away
> too many "secrets," I've included six examples incorporating seventh chords in a six tonic system
> (whole tone scale). The voicing type is Drop 3. (You might try Drop 2 and Drop 2 & 4 as well...)

**Significance (p. 161).** Defines the Grip-Slipping technique and its pedagogical purpose—learning all chord inversions—while specifying the voicing type and suggesting variations.

## Melody-harmony equality rule

> I think it's clear that both writers are saying that melody has the edge over harmony. I would
> disagree, but only slightly. I feel that excellent voice-leading treats both melody and harmony as
> equals.

**Significance (p. 188).** States the book's governing aesthetic rule: voice-leading excellence requires treating melody and harmony as equals, distinguishing this method from classical theory.

## Spread Clusters as missing link

> I went back to my notes and DID THE MATH! Much to my surprise and delight, I realized
> that I had completely overlooked SPREAD CLUSTERS! I did some more musical math and real-
> ized that this was the missing link! It not only completed the set of 4-part chords, but it helped me
> to realize that this WORK was not only finite, but do-able!

**Significance (p. 189).** Identifies Spread Clusters as the chord family that completes the entire taxonomy of diatonic 4-part chords, making it the conceptual keystone of the system.
