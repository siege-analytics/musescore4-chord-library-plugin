# Prescriptive lessons — mick-goodrick × almanac-vol-3

## triad

Goodrick treats triads as a distinct M-Lode family defined by their interval content — 3rds and 6ths with some 4ths and 5ths, explicitly excluding 2nds and 7ths — which separates them categorically from 4th voicings. The derivation logic is central: each 4-part 7th chord yields two triadic subtypes by voice omission, and each resulting 3-part type nests inside exactly four different 4-part families, making triads a reharmonization hub. For practical application, Goodrick favors 3-part voicings on guitar for their playability and the melodic space they open between voices.

- [ch.4 p.8] Goodrick places triads in the M-Lode as 3-part structures whose interval content consists primarily of 3rds and 6ths with some 4ths and 5ths, but containing no 2nds or 7ths.
- [ch.4 p.8] Interval omission is permitted within the triad family — omission of interval types occurs only within triad, 3-part 4th, and spread cluster families.
- [ch.5 p.9] Dropping one voice from a 4-part 7th chord produces two possible triadic 3-part types. Each resulting type appears across four different 4-part chord families.
- [ch.5 p.9] Substitute any of the four parent families when harmonizing a given 3-part sonority.
- [ch.10 p.43] Split 4-part voicings into two-voice duets (top two / bottom two) and add passing tones; "NICE in Harmonic minor!"
- [ch.10 p.56] Apply all six generic A-B-C voice-leading patterns to triads, not only the three used in Volumes I and II.
- [ch.11 p.71] Favor 3-part chords over 4-part for guitarists: easier playability, more melodic embellishment space.
- [ch.11 p.71] Derive twenty foundational 3-part chord types from Major, Melodic Minor, and Harmonic Minor transposed to C; transpose independently to remaining keys.
- [ch.12 p.161] Learn all inversions via Grip-Slipping: Drop 3 voicings within a whole-tone (six-tonic) system; Drop 2 and Drop 2&4 as alternates.

## sus4

Goodrick treats stacked-fourth chords as a distinct M-Lode family and prescribes sus4 naming over more cumbersome alternatives. The interval profile is the inverse of the triad: primarily 4ths and 5ths with some 2nds and 7ths, no 3rds or 6ths. Like triads, the sus4 family permits internal interval omission.

- [ch.2 p.4] Use sus4 notation: "G [sus4]" = G Major triad with 4 suspended for 3.
- [ch.2 p.4] Prefer sus4 notation for all stacked-fourth chords (3- and 4-part); reject cumbersome alternatives.
- [ch.4 p.8] Interval omission permitted within 3-part 4th voicings.
- [ch.4 p.8] Interval content: primarily 4ths and 5ths with some 2nds and 7ths; no 3rds or 6ths.

## maj7

Goodrick uses CMaj7 as the primary pedagogical entry for the derivation system, structure number 1 in the 35-structure taxonomy. The full menu of voicing transformations is applied to maj7-type chords across all four inversions.

- [ch.5 p.9] CMaj7 = primary worked example for deriving 3-part chords from a 4-part 7th chord. Drop 3rd → 7th-no-3rd; drop 5th → 7th-no-5th.
- [ch.5 p.9 / ch.11] Apply 4-way close, Drop 2, Drop 3, Drop 2&3, Drop 2&4, Double Drop 2 Drop 3 across root and 1st/2nd/3rd inversions.
