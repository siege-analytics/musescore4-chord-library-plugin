# Chapter 4 — 3-Part Chords: Found and Identified!

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch04.md`._

Triads and 3-part 4th chords represent fundamentally different harmonic structures distinguished by their interval content: triads consist primarily of 3rds and 6ths with some 4ths and 5ths but exclude 2nds and 7ths, while 3-part 4th voicings reverse this pattern by emphasizing 4ths and 5ths with some 2nds and 7ths but containing no 3rds or 6ths. This chapter establishes that available intervals are distributed relatively evenly across five different chord types in both 3- and 4-part voicings, with interval omissions occurring only within specific 3-part chord families (triads, 3-part 4ths, and spread clusters). The four chord families demonstrating the most balanced interval distribution—7th no-3rd, 7th no-5th, TBN I, and TBN II—appear consistently across both 3- and 4-part chords, revealing a structural principle that links voicing families through their interval-distribution patterns.
