# Chapter 5 — 3-Part Chords Derived From a 7th Chord

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch05.md`._

This chapter teaches the method of deriving 3-part chords by systematically omitting one voice from a 7th chord, producing two distinct triadic types: the 7th chord without its 3rd and the 7th chord without its 5th. The foundational insight is that each 3-part chord type appears across four different 4-part chord families, a symmetry principle with direct applications to reharmonization and harmonic substitution. The chapter introduces the core taxonomic framework showing exactly which triads emerge from voice reduction, using five concrete examples (CMaj7 and variants) positioned as numbers 1, 8, 15, 22, and 29 within the book's larger 35-structure system. This is primarily an instructional chapter focused on systematic harmonic derivation, establishing the mechanical rules for triadic generation that students can extend independently across the remaining 30 structures.
