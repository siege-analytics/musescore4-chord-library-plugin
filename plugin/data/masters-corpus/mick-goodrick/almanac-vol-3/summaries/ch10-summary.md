# Chapter 10 — Stuff To Do With 3-Part Chords

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch10.md`._

Chapter 10 teaches systematic voice-leading techniques for three-part and four-part chords within diatonic scales, establishing C Major as the default presentation with the expectation that students transpose to melodic and harmonic minor variants. The chapter introduces a mathematical framework for analyzing voice-leading through generic formulas using abstract A-B-C note labels, revealing that prior volumes employed only three of six possible voice-leading patterns and establishing space for new alternates in four-part chords. Key analytical concepts distinguish intervallic motion (interval sizes between voices) from functional voice-leading (chord-tone assignments and their cyclical roles), anchored by the M.S.R.P. sequence and reinforced through practical annotations that acknowledge when complex voicings sound effective despite defying simple analysis. The instructional material progresses from foundational triad patterns through increasingly sophisticated spread-cluster and duet textures, culminating in guidance for adding passing tones and recognizing stylistic applications such as the characteristic resonance of voice-leading patterns in harmonic minor contexts.
