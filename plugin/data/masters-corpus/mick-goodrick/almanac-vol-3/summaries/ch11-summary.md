# Chapter 11 — Twenty 3-Part Chords You Should Know

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch11.md`._

All of the following 3-part chords are derived from the Major scale, the Melodic Minor and the Harmonic Minor scales, but they are transposed to the root of C. As guitarists, 3-part chords are easier to play than 4-part chords and afford more space between the voices for melodic embellishment, making them practical for both playability and composition. The chapter introduces twenty foundational 3-part chord types, though the title is somewhat misleading—when accounting for close and spread inversions, there are actually one hundred twenty distinct voicings to master. The author explicitly identifies this chapter as the most important in the volume, underscoring that mastery of these twenty chord structures forms the central goal of the entire book. The load-bearing terminology includes the three minor scale families (Major, Melodic Minor, Harmonic Minor) as the theoretical sources, the concepts of close and spread inversions that multiply the voicings, and the principle of melodic embellishment space as a compositional advantage.
