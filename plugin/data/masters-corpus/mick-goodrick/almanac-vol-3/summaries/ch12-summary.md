# Chapter 12 — 840 Chords You Should Know (Part 1)

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch12.md`._

Chapter 12 introduces the Harmonic Major and Hungarian Minor scales as extensions of diatonic material, along with the conceptual framework distinguishing Specific Chromatic Voice-Leading (SCVL) from Generic Diatonic Voice-Leading (GDVL) that underpins the volume's advanced work. The Grip-Slipping technique is defined as a systematic method for learning chord inversions using Drop 3 voicings and variations within a whole-tone system. A central aesthetic principle establishes that excellence in voice-leading treats melody and harmony as equal partners rather than privileging one over the other, departing from classical theory conventions. Spread Clusters emerge as the missing structural link that completes the taxonomy of 4-part diatonic chords, making the entire system finite and comprehensive. The chapter presents all material across five scales—C Major, C Melodic Minor, C Harmonic Minor, C Harmonic Major, and C Hungarian Minor—establishing a framework explicitly designed to extend across the entire three-volume method.
