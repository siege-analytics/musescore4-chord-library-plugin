# Mick Goodrick — *Almanac of Guitar Voice Leading, Vol. 4*
## Prescriptive & Proscriptive Usage Notes

## `sus4`

### Preferred Label Over `add2` / `C2`

Use `sus4` notation exclusively. Shorter notations such as `C2` or `add-2` are explicitly proscribed on grounds of musician legibility. Goodrick concedes brevity ("I will grant you that C2 is shorter") but nonetheless prescribes `G [sus4]` as the correct form. This applies to all voicings built on fourth-based structures, whether 3-part or 4-part.

> Ch. 2, p. 4 · Ch. 3, pp. 4–5

### Inversion-Label Consistency

The `sus4` naming convention must be maintained across all inversions without exception. No inversion reverts to `C2` or `add-2` notation. Goodrick states this directly: "Of course, this applies to all of the inversions as well."

> Ch. 2, p. 4

## `7th (no 3rd)`

### M-Lode Taxonomy Member

`7th (no 3rd)` is prescribed as one of three new 3-part chord types that expand the M-Lode into the complete taxonomy underpinning Volume III. It must be recognized and cataloged as a distinct 3-part type — it may not be collapsed into another category or treated as a reduced form of a fuller chord.

> Ch. 3, p. 7

## `7th (no 5th)`

### M-Lode Taxonomy Member

`7th (no 5th)` is prescribed as a distinct 3-part chord type within the expanded M-Lode. Its derivation is explicit: it arises from the voice-omission principle taught in Chapter 4 — removing exactly one voice (the 5th) from a 4-part 7th chord.

> Ch. 3, p. 7 · Ch. 4, p. 9

## `3-part Spread Cluster`

### M-Lode Taxonomy Member

The 3-part Spread Cluster is prescribed as the third and final new chord type completing the expanded M-Lode. It must be cataloged as a distinct 3-part chord family, coordinate with — not subordinate to — `7th (no 3rd)` and `7th (no 5th)`.

> Ch. 3, p. 7

## `3-part` (General)

### Playability Priority Over 4-Part

3-part chords take foundational priority over 4-part chords in sequencing chord literacy work. The prescription is ergonomic: "As guitarists, we can see that 3-part chords are easier to play than 4-part chords." The 840-chord catalog (Chapters 6-9) institutionalizes this by introducing all 3-part material before 4-part material.

> Ch. 7, p. 71

### Passing-Tone & Melodic-Embellishment Exercises Anchored in C Major

Passing-tone and melodic-embellishment work on 3-part voicings must begin in C Major (no accidentals). Goodrick states: "All of the material that follows in this next section will be presented in C Major (no accidentals)." The student is then expected to transpose independently to all other 7-note scales.

> Ch. 5, p. 15

## `4-part`

### Density Ceiling & Voice-Omission Source

4-part chords constitute the density ceiling of the system: no chord construction exceeds four voices. All 3-part chord derivation flows downward from 4-part sources by removing exactly one voice at a time. Each 3-part type appears exactly four times in the derivation table — once per available omission position — revealing the symmetric structure.

> Ch. 4, p. 9
