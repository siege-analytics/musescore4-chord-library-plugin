# Prescriptive lessons — mickey-baker × complete-course-in-jazz-guitar

## maj7
Baker treats maj7 as the baseline tonic color in modern jazz comping — plain major triads are "corny" and maj7 is the minimum acceptable landing point. The chord carries a dual identity (Gma7 = Bmi7 via the two-names principle), and that same relative relationship transfers directly from chord voicings into improvised runs.

- [lesson 3 p.9] maj7 belongs to a three-member interchangeable tonic family: Cma7, Cma6, and Cma9 are all "on the major mode" and any one substitutes freely for a plain major triad.
- [lesson 7 p.19] Two-names principle: Gma6 = Emi7 and Ama6 = Fmi7 are identical shapes under different harmonic identities. Learn both names in all keys.
- [lesson 7 p.26] Cma7 is the prescribed tonic arrival in Cycle No. 1 — "Cma7 Ebmi7" rather than plain C, establishing maj7 as the canonical tonic destination.
- [lesson 10 p.34] Relative-run substitution: "Emi7 and Gma6 are the same (relatives), so it can be used as a major run." Bmi7 run substitutes for Gma7.
- [lesson 10 p.41] Gma7 and Bmi7 are "such close relatives that one can be used for the other at any time" — two-names license extends into solo contexts.

## min7
Baker's most-used functional move is the dominant-to-relative-minor substitution: replace any dom7 with the minor-7 chord a fifth above it. min7 operates as ii-chord, dominant stand-in, and tonic surrogate (via two-names equivalence with maj6).

- [lesson 3 p.9] min7 in the minor-mode interchangeable family: "Dmi6, Dmi7 and Dmi9 are adaptable, because they are all on the same mode."
- [lesson 4 p.12] Core rule: "if you have a dominant 7 chord you can substitute its relative minor which is the 5th of the chord." G7 → Dmi7; D7 → Ami7.
- [lesson 4 p.12 / lesson 5 p.15] min7 as dominant surrogate: "most of the time when we have a seventh chord, we substitute minor chords in their place."
- [lesson 7 p.19] Two-names: Gma6 = Emi7. Learn both names in all keys.
- [lesson 7 p.26] Cycle No. 1: "Replace C7 with Gmi7-Gmi6; Replace G7 with Dmi7-G13b9." min7-to-min6 descent is the structural feature.
- [lesson 10 p.34] Blues bars 9–10: "Ami7 run for one bar and a Gmi7 run for one bar" over D7 and C7.
- [lesson 10 p.34] Voice-leading chain into major: "Ami7 run to a Cmi7 run which will blend right into a G major run."

## dom7
Baker regards unaltered dom7 as an entry point, not destination: "with dominant chords there is no limit to what can be done," and plain dom7 signals insufficient harmonic engagement.

- [lesson 3 p.9] Substitution menu: G13, G9, G7#5+9, G13#5+9, G7b5, G11, G13b9 "and so on."
- [lesson 3 p.9] Color-tone requirement: plain triads sound "corny" — unaltered dom7 heading to tonic is also insufficient.
- [lesson 3 p.9] Progression-dependency: "you just can't throw these chords in any place, they have to be part of some kind of progression."
- [lesson 4 p.12 / lesson 11 p.42] Relative-minor substitution: "the fifth of any dominant chord can be substituted in place of the dominant itself."
- [lesson 5 p.15] Dominant is "the key to all of the other lessons" and "the best possible way to connect one chord to another."

## dom7alt
Always-alter-the-V before major: G7#5+9 and G13b5+9 are the two canonical altered forms; the prescription extends from comping into solo work. Root-omitted tritone equivalence (G7#5 = Db9b5) gives every altered dominant a built-in dual identity.

- [lesson 7 p.19] Root-omitted tritone equivalence: G7#5 and Db9b5 share the same fingering — tritone substitution structurally embedded in the voicing.
- [lesson 10 p.34] "When connecting a dominant chord to a major the dominant is in most of the time altered — G7#5+9 to Cma or G13b5+9 to Cma."
- [lesson 10 p.34] "This same thing applies to solo work" — altered-dominant rule applies universally regardless of texture.

## dom7b9
G13b9 (not plain dom7b9) is the canonical altered V in Cycle No. 1, paired with preceding Dmi7. The b9 alteration is structurally built into the ii-V recipe.

- [lesson 3 p.9] G13b9 in the extended dominant menu — drop-in replacement for any plain G7.
- [lesson 7 p.26] Prescribed resolution target in Cycle No. 1: "Replace G7 with Dmi7-G13b9."

## dom13
G13 is Baker's least-altered dominant extension and most common starting point beyond plain dom7 — leads the extended menu, appears as Dmi7 cell endpoint, and supports tritone-equivalence in root-omitted shapes.

- [lesson 3 p.9] G13 heads the extended dominant substitution menu.
- [lesson 4 p.12] "I may use Dmi7 to Dmi6, or Dmi7 to G13" — both unaltered G13 and altered G13b9 are available endpoints.
- [lesson 7 p.26] G13b5b9 appears as a root-omitted form; tritone-equivalence applies to 13-chord shapes.

## dom9
G9 — second slot in the extended dominant menu. Unaltered ninth extension available as drop-in for any plain dom7; no further specification in source.

- [lesson 3 p.9] "G13, G9, G7#5+9…" — context-appropriate substitute for any plain dom7.

## dom11
G11 in the menu but no dedicated prescription beyond membership.

- [lesson 3 p.9] Listed in the extended dominant menu: "G13, G9, G7#5+9, G13#5+9, G7b5, G11, G13b9 and so on."

## dom7b5
Two tracks: standard extended dominant substitute (menu) and tritone substitution form (root-omitted equivalence G7#5 = Db9b5). Chapter 7 adds chromatic inner-voice passing function.

- [lesson 3 p.9 / lesson 7 p.19] G7b5 in extended menu; Db9b5 = G7#5 root-omitted — direct substitute and tritone vehicle.
- [lesson 7 p.26] C7b5 as chromatic passing dominant: "Gmi7 C7b5" resolving to Fmi7 — distinct from tritone-substitution role.

## aug7
Negative prescription: aug7 belongs to "Standard" vocabulary Baker's modern reharmonization explicitly replaces.

- [lesson 7 p.26] G+7 in Standard progression replaced by Dmi7–Dmi6 cell in the New version.

## dim7
dim7 as one of four foundational solo building blocks (scale, minor, dominant, diminished runs) drilled chromatically with identical fingering. No dedicated harmonic placement rule.

- [lesson 9] Diminished run in Chapter 9 exercise vocabulary (Lessons 27–31), transposed chromatically up and down the neck.

## maj6
Member of major-mode interchangeable family AND structural anchor of two-names principle: Gma6 = Emi7, Ama6 = Fmi7. Same equivalence licenses minor-7 runs over major tonic.

- [lesson 3 p.9] Cma6 one of three interchangeable major-mode substitutes with Cma7 and Cma9.
- [lesson 7 p.19] Two-names principle: Gma6 = Emi7, Ama6 = Fmi7.
- [lesson 10 p.34] "Emi7 and Gma6 are the same (relatives), so it can be used as a major run."

## maj9
Most coloristically rich major-mode family member.

- [lesson 3 p.9] One of three primary major-mode substitutes alongside Cma7 and Cma6.

## min9
Member of minor-mode interchangeable family.

- [lesson 3 p.9] "Dmi6, Dmi7 and Dmi9 are adaptable, because they are all on the same mode."

## min6
Resolution step within the relative-minor substitution cell — min7-to-min6 descent provides inner-voice stepwise motion.

- [lesson 3 p.9 / lesson 4 p.12] Member of minor-mode family. "I may use Dmi7 to Dmi6" establishes min7-to-min6 descent.
- [lesson 7 p.26] Cycle No. 1: second chord of each relative-minor cell ("Gmi7-Gmi6"). Seventh stepping down to sixth gives the cell its inner-voice resolution.
