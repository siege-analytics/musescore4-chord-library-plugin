# Prescriptive lessons -- nelson-faria x brazilian-guitar-book

## dom7

Dom7 is Faria's most-worked chord quality, appearing across all five styles with distinct roles in each. In baiao it functions as a modal tonic (mixolydian) or lydian-b7 color rather than a dominant-function chord, while in samba, frevo, and bossa nova it is the harmonic workhorse that receives the most rhythmic texture variation. Bass construction throughout defaults to root/fifth alternation with the fifth below root, and nearly every dom7 treatment is subordinate to a rhythmic prescription -- the harmony itself is simple; the groove is the lesson.

- [ch1 p25] Bass alternates root and fifth under dom7 (and all chords); fifth-below-root is the preferred voicing when the 6th string is already the root. This is the foundation bass rule applied globally.
- [ch1 p26] Variation #1 switches between low and high registers to simulate the agogo's two-cowbell timbre -- dom7 voicings are chosen for register contrast, not harmonic completeness.
- [ch1 p30] Thumb carries an independent syncopated bass line; index finger handles block-style bass strokes. The two fingering modes produce different groove textures on the same dom7 chord.
- [ch1 p30] Variation #3 prescribes left-hand fingering 3-1-4-2 top-to-bottom as the correct solution for its dom7 voicing -- fingering is not left to the player's discretion.
- [ch1 p33] Variation #6 syncopates every beat, placing chords off-beat against an on-beat bass, creating overt counterpoint within a single dom7 vamp.
- [ch1 p33] Variation #7 is the Partido alto sub-style; dom7 voicings are kept minimal to let the rhythmic pocket dominate.
- [ch1 p39] Variation #9 runs a four-measure cycle mixing syncopated and non-syncopated bars to produce batucada style -- harmonic content stays on dom7 throughout.
- [ch1 p44] Variation #15 is reserved for the singer's pause or end of section; it is a contextual fill, not a continuous texture. Applying it continuously is a deployment error.
- [ch1 p47] Variation #17 addresses a Db9 (dom9) wide stretch with three alternative left-hand solutions: thumb-over, 4-1-1-3-2 fingering, and split-finger barre. The note gives the player a decision tree, not a single answer.
- [ch2 p68] Bossa nova Variation #4 carries the same contextual restriction as samba Variation #15 -- fill only, optional 16th-note displacement available.
- [ch2 p69] Harmony change arrives 16th note before the syncopated accent; dom7 and its neighbors must be fretted slightly early to land on the rhythmic grid.
- [ch2 p67] Variation #3 uses a short-note sub-style: staccato articulation and registral reduction thin the dom7 voicing to its upper structure.
- [ch4 p102, p104] Frevo bass moves in even quarter notes (jazz walking-bass character); syncopations are confined to the upper voices. Dom7 appears in simple turnaround harmonies.
- [ch4 p104] Anchor bass to 6th string on dom7 where possible to prevent unnecessary string crossing mid-pattern.
- [ch4 p108] Variation #4 is upbeat-only texture; downbeats are muted with the left hand as an alternative technique on dom7 voicings.
- [ch4 p110] Variation #5 is designated for marcha rancho sub-style in 3/4 meter -- the same dom7 harmony is recontextualized by metric shift.
- [ch5 p121] Baiao core pattern: dom7 as modal tonic (mixolydian), bass is a syncopated pedal while upper voices fall on the beat -- the rhythmic polarity is reversed relative to samba.
- [ch5 p122, p124] Dom7 with raised 4th (lydian b7) is the primary baiao color; cowbell texture and open-string berimbau device are the characteristic voicing/texture moves.
- [ch5 p124] Variation #2: open G string sustains as a berimbau drone against the lydian-b7 dom7 chord -- an idiomatic open-string coloristic device.
- [ch5 p125] Variation #4 applies broken-chord texture specifically to dom7 in baiao; texture type is prescribed, not improvised.
- [ch5 p126] Variation #5 introduces bass syncopation on dom7 to import a salsa flavor into baiao -- cross-stylistic borrowing via rhythmic displacement of the bass note.

## maj7

Maj7 receives minimal explicit treatment; the book's single maj7 lesson is a voicing-density prescription in the bossa nova context rather than a harmonic or functional teaching. Faria treats maj7 as a color chord whose interest is in its upper-structure stack, not its root-position weight. The lesson implies that right-hand finger assignment determines chord density and that maj7 voicings are an appropriate site for five-voice upper-structure writing.

- [ch2 p66] Five-voice maj7 chord voicings use right-hand fingers 1-2-3 plus additional fingers for dense upper-structure grabs -- finger allocation is specified, and the voicing is expected to be full-register rather than stripped down.

## dim7

Faria treats dim7 as a passing color in choro exclusively, always inverted, and its root-position form is explicitly prohibited as a stylistic error. The two dim7 entries are a matched prescriptive/proscriptive pair: the positive lesson (use inversions) and its negative mirror (never root position) are cross-referenced on the same page, signaling that Faria considered root-position dim7 a common enough mistake to flag twice.

- [ch3 p86] Diminished chords in choro use inverted voicings as the default; the inversion is not an option but the style-correct form for passing-harmony function.
- [ch3 p86] PROSCRIPTIVE: Root-position dim7 voicing in choro is a stylistic error. Cross-referenced with the inversion lesson above -- the prohibition and the prescription are inseparable.
