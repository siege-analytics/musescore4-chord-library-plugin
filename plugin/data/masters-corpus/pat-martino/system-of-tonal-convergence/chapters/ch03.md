## Convergence system overview

> In my convergence system I use 24 scales on various tonalities and of various colors (Blues, diminish, augmented, pentatonic, angular, descending, spanish, modal & dorian Jazz minor), to ‘illustrate the many possibilities of use in composing, improvising solos & arranging.

**Significance (p. 4).** This is the foundational definition of the book's entire system -- naming the 24 scale types and their categorical colors that will govern every subsequent chapter.

## Scale-chord raw material principle

> Each scale may be used as raw material for convergent ideas within a piece of music as well as many chordal sounds extracted from them,

**Significance (p. 4).** This establishes the core pedagogical rule that scales and chords are treated as interchangeable raw material, not separate domains.

## Scale combination & color rotation

> These scales & their chords may be combined (several of them together) in several rotas varying their order and making more interesting musical usuage of color (A,—»; +, 0, 7th).

**Significance (p. 4).** This defines the combinatorial technique -- rotating scale-chord groupings by color type -- that is the system's characteristic compositional move.

## Goal: expanding ear to chromatic resources

> The important factor is the expanding of hearing tolerance increasing the musicians ear awareness to the entire tonal resources of the chromatic scale.

**Significance (p. 4).** This states the overarching pedagogical goal of the method: training the ear to accept the full chromatic spectrum, not just diatonic resources.

## Scales in turnbacks, cadences, cycles

> The usuage of these scales in turnbacks, cadences and cycles (which comprise all music) in Jazz yields a wider source of ideas for the improvisor-composer-arranger.

**Significance (p. 4).** This gives the functional context for applying the system -- turnbacks, cadences, and cycles -- telling the student exactly where to deploy the scales.

## Tonal gravity law

> A tonal gravity law is created once you start to play music (in a key). The key of the music exerts a gravity pull on all other chords no matter what they may be.

**Significance (p. 5).** This defines the concept of tonal gravity that underpins why convergence toward a key center is musically inevitable and theoretically grounded.

## Convergence system purpose

> It is this "significant end" to which I have compiled this convergence system. To end musically a major cadence or lesser tonal cadence with variety of scale color & their constituent chords, variety of plane for an unforgettable conscious end, wherever that end may lie.

**Significance (p. 5).** This is the explicit functional definition of the convergence system's purpose -- providing scale/chord variety for cadential resolution points.

## V chord convergence example (Bb7)

> Bb7 is a standard V convergent chord to EP minor & major tonalities as well as many other chords and their parent scales in this convergent system.

**Significance (p. 6).** This is the book's first concrete harmonic example, illustrating how a single dominant chord serves as a convergence point for multiple parent scales -- the paradigm case of the system.

## 7th chord as most flexible chord type

> The 7th chord having the all important tritone host the largest and most flexible of all the chord types. (7th bs, 7b 9, 7th #5, 7th #9, 7th 9s, ren bg bs ect).

**Significance (p. 8).** This defines why the dominant 7th chord is the pivot of the entire system -- its tritone makes it maximally convergent and generates the widest range of alteration options.

## Multiple scale colors on one chord

> This, from listening to John Coltrane, Yusef Lateef and others caused me to try using many different scale "color" types on one chord (7th).

**Significance (p. 8).** This articulates the system's central improvisational technique: applying multiple distinct scale colors to a single chord quality, not just one scale per chord.

## II-V as system's origin point

> It was at this final juncture (II-V) where my convergence system was conceived, noticing all the chord possibilities on the final 5th chord in the cadence.

**Significance (p. 8).** This precisely locates where the convergence system is to be applied -- on the V chord of the II-V cadence -- giving students the exact structural target.

## Scale color spectrum analogy

> Major, minor, augmented, dominant & diminish became like the green, blue, black, red & yellow of the color spectrum showing a manifestation and parallelism of two art forms.

**Significance (p. 8).** This defines the book's core conceptual framework -- mapping scale/chord quality to color -- which is the organizing metaphor for how students are trained to categorize tonal material.
