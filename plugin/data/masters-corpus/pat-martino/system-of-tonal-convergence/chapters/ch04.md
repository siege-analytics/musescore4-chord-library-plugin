## Convergence system definition

> This convergence system shows a fixed bombardment of
> any type chord that we want to give "tonal significance" to then
> bombard it with 24 scales, a mixture of them, their ideas and
> constituent chords.

**Significance (p. 17).** This is the foundational definition of the entire method -- the systematic application of 24 scales to any target chord as a means of approaching tonal resolution.

## Convergence system origin / V-chord technique

> I conceived this convergence system by using a one chord tag
> at the very end of "Stella Bye Starlite." The first chord (numbered)
> of each of the following group of 43 chords & scales yield a different
> type V chord (function) and its parent scale, yielding several con-
> vergent scales & their chords for tonal convergence.

**Significance (p. 21).** Martino explains exactly how the system was conceived -- each of the 43 chords/scales functions as a unique kind of V chord, giving the player dozens of approach routes to a tonal destination.

## Ear preparation before convergence

> Before playing each scale in this
> system, you should brainwash your ear with the
> tonality (F major in this case) with several cadences
> into the key. (Ex. G-7-C7 FA) (G£-C 139 FA)
> I- VI- II- V-T ect.

**Significance (p. 32).** This is a prescriptive practice rule -- the player must first internalize the destination tonality through cadential repetition before attempting convergent scale approaches.

## Aural projection rule

> When converging on a tonality project yourself sound wise to
> it already having your attention (sound attention) on it and
>
> on the chord or scale you are using.

**Significance (p. 34).** This gives the mental/aural technique that accompanies the physical scale work -- the improviser must simultaneously hear the destination while executing the approach scale.

## Scale #5 -- Diminished scale (W-H)
