# Prescriptive lessons -- pat-martino x system-of-tonal-convergence

## maj7

The maj7 chord (FA7, FA79, FA679) is the system's sole structural destination: every scale, alteration, and tritone substitution in the book exists to make arrival at the major seventh sound inevitable. Martino treats the maj7 not as a resting point to be stumbled upon but as an actively prepared target -- the student must have internalized it through cadential saturation *before* any approach scale is deployed. The 360-degree architecture of Chapter 5 formalizes this: all 24 scale colors surround the maj7 center and can be aimed at it from any angle.

- [ch.7 p.58] The maj7 chord (FA7) is the canonical convergence landing: dominant-family chords Bb7b5, C7+5, C7b5, and Gb7b5 are all prescribed as approach chords resolving to FA7, establishing the maj7 as the universal endpoint against which tritone-related dominant variants are tested.
- [ch.5 p.33] Martino frames the maj7 tonal center as a target under 360-degree bombardment by all 24 scales and their constituent chords -- the "astrological charting" metaphor makes clear that the maj7 is acted upon, not merely arrived at by default.
- [ch.4 pp.32-34] A three-step preparation protocol governs every maj7 approach: (1) brainwash the ear with cadential repetition (G-7-C7-FA, I-VI-II-V-I), (2) execute the approach scale while aurally projecting the resolution, (3) extract all voicings latent in the scale as additional convergent devices.

## dom7

The dom7 is the system's primary raw material and structural pivot: its embedded tritone makes it maximally convergent, and the entire 24-scale apparatus exists to supply variety of approach to this single chord type. Martino's prescriptions for dom7 span deployment zone (II-V cadence, turnbacks, cycles), ear protocol (internalize destination first, project resolution simultaneously), and harmonic excavation (every scale yields multiple dom7-family character chords to be mined, not just one). The Circle of Gravities formalizes the system: three interlocking tritone pairs -- (C-F#), (G-Db), (E-Bb) -- govern all convergent dominant motion toward any tonal center.

- [ch.3 p.8] The dom7 is prescribed as the convergence system's most flexible pivot because its tritone hosts the largest alteration family: 7b5, 7b9, 7#5, 7#9, 7(9)s, and their extensions are all generated from this single chord type's tritone content.
- [ch.3 p.8] The V chord of the II-V cadence is the *exact structural moment* of convergence deployment: "It was at this final juncture (II-V) where my convergence system was conceived, noticing all the chord possibilities on the final 5th chord in the cadence." Every scale color is aimed here.
- [ch.3 p.8] Multiple distinct scale colors must be applied to a single dom7 -- not one scale per chord: "This, from listening to John Coltrane, Yusef Lateef and others caused me to try using many different scale 'color' types on one chord (7th)." The 24-scale system supplies this variety.
- [ch.4 p.32] Before deploying any approach scale against a dom7 target, Martino prescribes ear saturation through cadential repetition: "brainwash your ear with the tonality with several cadences into the key." Approach without internalized destination is not licensed.
- [ch.4 p.34] While executing an approach scale, the player must simultaneously project aural attention to the dom7 resolution: physical execution and arrival anticipation must co-occur.
- [ch.4 p.34] Every scale must be mined for all constituent chords as independent convergent devices: "Extract all possible chords from all scales for resolution purposes." Scales are not melodic entities only.
- [ch.10 p.82] The concise chromatic resolution rule for scalar convergence onto a dom7: "using chromatic tones to resolve the tritone (7th or 3rd)" -- approach scales must target those two defining tones of the dominant via chromatic motion.
- [ch.10 p.80] For any dominant resolution, 21 convergent approach chords are available simultaneously: the V7 and all its altered variants, the tritone-substitute E7 family with added tensions, the shared-tritone diminished D7, augmented D7+5, and diminished seventh Bo7. No single form is the default.
- [ch.10 p.83] The Circle of Gravities organizes all convergent dominant motion through three tritone pairs: (C-F#), (G-Db), (E-Bb). Knowing which family a dom7 belongs to determines its convergence destination.
- [ch.10 p.84] Proscription: scales containing a major third must NOT be applied against the root of a minor-chord convergence target -- they must target the fifth of the minor chord instead, preserving minor quality. Exception: fifth-targeting with a major-third scale is the one explicitly licensed move.
- [ch.3 p.4] Prescribed deployment zones are turnbacks, cadences, and cycles: "The usage of these scales in turnbacks, cadences and cycles (which comprise all music) in Jazz yields a wider source of ideas for the improvisor-composer-arranger."

## dom7b5

The dom7b5 is the system's primary character-chord form because the flatted fifth is the tritone itself made explicit in the voicing. Martino prescribes it as the canonical harmonic product of the whole-tone scale and as one of two co-equal poles of tritone substitution: C7b5 and its tritone substitute Gb7b5 are interchangeable approach chords because they share the same resolution path.

- [ch.7 p.58] Tritone substitution is prescribed at the dom7b5 level: Bb7b5 and Gb7b5 share the same tritone interval and resolve identically to FA7, making them fully substitutable as approach chords. The substitution is not a stylistic option but a structural equivalence.
- [ch.4 p.41] The whole-tone scale (Scale #10) prescribes C7b5 as its primary linking chord: "The linking chord is the C7b5 which resolves nicely into F major." Additional licensed products from the same scale are C+7 and C69#11.
- [ch.8 p.62] Scale #23 prescribes a cluster of tritone-resolved dom7b5 voicings -- Gb7b5, C7b5, C7b9, C7b9b5 -- as its convergence chords, demonstrating that the tritone substitute and original dominant are simultaneously present and co-equal as approach options.

## dom7b9

The dom7b9 functions in the system both as a scale-identity chord (Scale #11's defining sound) and as the characteristic approach chord for minor-key convergence. Its role extends across major and minor contexts because the b9 places a half-step above the root of the target, supplying the chromatic voice-leading that is the system's resolution engine.

- [ch.5 p.28] Scale #11 (Martino's personal scale) is defined by its C7b9 sound: "My own personal scale containing an E-Bb tritone & the note Bb9 of which gives the scale a C7b9 sound." The dom7b9 is this scale's sonic identity and convergence vehicle into F major.
- [ch.7 p.58] Scale #11 prescribes a tritone-related cluster -- C7b9, D7b5, Gb, Eb13 -- all converging on FA79. The C7b9 anchors the group; the others are interchangeable approach options sharing the same tritone resolution path.
- [ch.10 p.84] For F minor convergence, the governing tritone pair (G-Db) yields a "G7 or C7b9 relationship to the F minor chord," making C7b9 the characteristic dominant approach chord. The dom7b9's role extends intact from major-key into minor-key contexts.

## dom7alt

Altered dominants are the direct harmonic product of multiple scales in the system -- not a single specialized device. Martino prescribes generating altered tension variants on both the V chord and its tritone substitute simultaneously, treating both as co-equal convergers; the full altered palette (b9b5, b9, b5, and their tritone-substitute equivalents) is available for any dominant resolution.

- [ch.7 pp.57, 59] Scale #13 prescribes the full altered dominant family -- C7b9b5, C7b9, C7b5 -- as its native character chords resolving to FA679. Scale #6 (Jazz Melodic Minor) prescribes altered extensions E+A7 and Bb7+ as its characteristic convergence chords. Altered dominants are a multi-scale product.
- [ch.10 p.80] For a target of Eb, "E7b5, E13b5, E9+11, E9, E13 may be used as chord convergers in addition to Bb7." Altered tensions applied to the tritone-substitute dominant are fully licensed and co-equal with the primary V chord's alterations.

## dom7#5

The augmented dominant dom7#5 appears as a character chord across multiple scale contexts -- whole-tone (Scale #10) and Scale #24 -- and is prescribed as a licensed substitute alongside the tritone-substitute and diminished families when approaching any dominant resolution. Its convergence justification is the same tritone engine, but the raised fifth provides a distinctive augmented color distinct from the b5 voicings.

- [ch.4 p.41 / ch.10 p.80] C7+5 appears as a whole-tone scale character chord alongside C7b5, and D7+5 is prescribed as a valid convergent substitute in Bb7-to-Eb progressions: "D7+5 also works beautifully as well as Bo7." The dom7#5 is a licensed augmented-dominant substitute sitting alongside the tritone-substitute and diminished families.
- [ch.8 p.62] Scale #24 prescribes C7+5 as one of its primary character chords alongside D-triangle7 and Gb13, all resolving to FA79. The augmented dominant is Scale #24's characteristic dominant form.

## min7

Martino treats the min7 as cadential infrastructure rather than a convergence destination in its own right: within the II-V-I, the min7 (II chord) is the ear-preparation vehicle and the structural setup, while the convergence system's machinery is concentrated at the V chord. The Dorian mode's min7 product (G-7) is explicitly named as a character chord, but the system's attention moves through the min7 toward the dominant.

- [ch.3 p.8 / ch.4 p.32] The II-V cadence is the convergence system's structural origin point. The min7 II chord (G-7) participates as cadential setup; example progressions G-7-C7-FA and I-VI-II-V-I are the prescribed practice vehicles for ear saturation before approach scales are deployed.
- [ch.6 p.37] The Dorian mode is prescribed as a convergence scale that contains the min7 function: "This scale is simply a dorian mode of the F major scale. It contains C7, G-7." The G-7 (min7) is listed as one of the Dorian scale's extracted character chords, confirming the min7 is a licensed harmonic product of diatonic scale convergence.

## min-maj7

The minor-major seventh chord appears in the system as a character chord of Scale #24, functioning as a non-dominant convergence participant -- evidence that the system's harmonic palette extends beyond dominant-family chords. However, when used to converge onto a minor resolution target, the min-maj7 inherits the major-third restriction: it must be aimed at the fifth of the minor chord, not its root.

- [ch.8 p.62] Scale #24 prescribes D-triangle7 (min-maj7) as a co-equal convergence chord alongside C7+5 and Gb13, all resolving to FA79. The min-maj7 demonstrates that non-dominant chord types can participate directly in convergent motion.
- [ch.10 p.84] When Scale #24's character chords are deployed toward a minor resolution target, the major-third restriction applies: "Scale #3 has a major 3rd already in it so it has to be used as above to embellish the 5th of the F minor." The min-maj7 character chord inherits this fifth-targeting constraint when the destination is minor.

## dim7

The diminished seventh is the system's most versatile convergence surrogate: its symmetrical structure allows a single dim7 to serve multiple tonal destinations simultaneously (symmetrical indifference), and it justifies itself through tritone-sharing with the dominant rather than by convention. Martino prescribes entering the diminished scale at different internal notes to multiply its convergence utility across multiple key centers.

- [ch.10 p.80] The dim7 is prescribed as a tritone-sharing dominant substitute: "Also the diminish chord D7 has the same tritone as the Bb7 (D-Ab). It smoothly voice leads into the Eb7." The tritone, not convention, is the justification.
- [ch.8 p.64] The diminished scale is prescribed as a convergence source for both Fi7 (diminished/half-diminished) and FA7 (major) resolution targets simultaneously -- "DIMINISH Fi7 / FA7" -- illustrating symmetrical indifference in direct application.
- [ch.4 p.36] The diminished scale's symmetrical indifference permits starting on different internal notes to serve multiple tonal centers with the same scale form. The dim7 chord inherits this multi-key convergence utility directly.
- [ch.6 p.41] The diminished scale's terminal voice-leading lands the b9th of the 7th chord on C -- the fifth of F major, not its root: "The last note of the scale (the b9th of 7th chord) voice leads smoothly to the note C (the fifth of F major)." The fifth is the primary resolution point.

## aug7

The augmented major seventh (aug7 family, E+A7) appears in the system as a character chord of the Jazz Melodic Minor scale and is further analyzed through dual harmonic identity: a single pitch (Ab) simultaneously reads as the minor third of the target tonality and as the augmented fifth of the V chord, licensing chromatic approach that remains tonally grounded.

- [ch.7 p.57] Scale #6 (Jazz Melodic Minor) prescribes E+A7 as one of its character chords for convergence, appearing alongside Gb7b5. The aug7 family is a licensed harmonic product of the Jazz Melodic Minor scale within the convergence system.
- [ch.5 p.35] Dual harmonic identity governs the aug7 reading: the note Ab functions simultaneously as the minor third of F minor tonality and as the +5 of C7+5 -- "making it more tonally palatable" -- and also as the third of E7+5 produced on the same scale. One pitch carries two simultaneous tonal justifications.

## sus4

The suspended dominant form (dom7b9sus) enters the system as a character chord of Scale #14, generated from a pentatonic structure mapping onto V-chord degrees. It is a single-appearance prescription: a licensed but peripheral approach chord, included in the system's palette rather than featured as a primary convergence vehicle.

- [ch.7 p.59] Scale #14 prescribes C7b9sus as one of its derived character chords alongside DbA7b5 and Eb13. The suspended dominant with b9 is a licensed convergence approach chord generated from Scale #14's pentatonic structure; it is a co-equal member of that scale's chord palette, not a secondary option.

## min7b5

The half-diminished chord (min7b5) appears in the system as a character chord extracted from a diminished-family scale (Scale #12), demonstrating that the convergence system's approach-chord vocabulary extends to non-dominant, non-major chord qualities. Its convergence justification follows the same tritone logic as the dom7b5 forms that appear alongside it.

- [ch.7 p.58] Scale #12 (diminished with minor 3rd and raised 4th) prescribes G-9b5 as one of its character chords alongside Bb7b5 and Db7b5. The min7b5 (half-diminished) is a direct harmonic product of the diminished-family scale, expanding the definition of which chord qualities can function as tritone-related approach chords.
