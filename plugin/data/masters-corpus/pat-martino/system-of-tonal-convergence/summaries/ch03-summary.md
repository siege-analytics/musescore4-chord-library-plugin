# Chapter 3 — Conceiving this Convergence System (scales & their chords)

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch03.md`._

Chapter 3 introduces Martino's convergence system, a pedagogical framework centered on 24 scale types (Blues, diminished, augmented, pentatonic, and others) treated as interchangeable raw material with their extracted chords for composition, improvisation, and arrangement. The characteristic moves visible here are the combinatorial rotation of scale-chord groupings by color type and the application of multiple distinct scale colors to a single chord -- techniques originating from listening to Coltrane and Lateef. The system's foundational concept is tonal gravity, the inevitable pull toward a key center that makes convergence musically grounded, and its pedagogical goal is expanding the musician's ear to accept the full chromatic spectrum rather than just diatonic resources. Load-bearing terms introduced include: the 24 scale colors, the 7th chord as the system's most flexible pivot point due to its tritone, the II-V cadence as the structural target (specifically the V chord), and the organizing metaphor of scale/chord quality mapped to the color spectrum, guiding students to deploy these scales in turnbacks, cadences, and cycles.
