# Chapter 4 — Scale Justification Sheets (24)

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch04.md`._

Chapter 4 introduces the convergence system, Martino's core pedagogical method of applying 24 scales (and their extracted chords) to any target chord in order to generate multiple voice-leading paths to a tonal destination, where the player's technical work consists of (1) first internalizing the destination tonality through cadential repetition, (2) executing each approach scale while simultaneously hearing its resolution, and (3) mining each scale for all possible constituent chords as alternative convergent devices. The chapter presents the system's origin in Martino's analysis of 'Stella Bye Starlite,' where he discovered that treating each of 43 chords/scales as a unique V-chord function yielded dozens of convergent routes to tonal resolution. Martino demonstrates his characteristic analytical method by justifying individual scales -- tracing each note's tritone and voice-leading function into the target key -- and introduces load-bearing terminology including tonal significance (the destination chord's harmonic goal), symmetrical indifference (the property that allows diminished and other scales to serve multiple tonalities), and the linking chord (the characteristic chord each scale produces). The system's purpose is to furnish variety: not just scales themselves but innumerable permutations of inversions and chord extractions that expand the improviser's technical palette.
