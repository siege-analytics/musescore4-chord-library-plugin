# Chapter 5 — Scale of the System and some Constituent Chords and their Resolutions

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch05.md`._

This chapter systematizes 24 scales orbiting a single tonal center (F major) in a 360-degree architecture, each designed for convergence into tonality through distinct intervallic symmetries and harmonic functions. Martino's characteristic moves appear in the analytical rigor applied to every scale -- mapping melodic function by reading each note against harmonic degrees of the V chord and tonic -- and in his strategic use of tritone cores, diminished-whole-tone hybrids, and raised-fourth Lydian shapes as distinct tonal colors for approach. The chapter introduces load-bearing concepts including the 'no wrong notes' doctrine (which conditions chromaticism on key-center fixation, vantage point, interval tonics, resolution, and confident intent), dual-harmonic identity (reading a single pitch as both a scale degree and a chord extension), and the principle that any scale may be entered at any internal note and chained in any order for maximum flexibility. Scales #11-18 exemplify this system through pentatonic convergence, half-diminished/whole-tone symmetry, major-with-raised-4th (Lydian) mechanics, and melodic-minor tetrachord structures that merge minor and major color under one tonal roof.
