# Chapter 6 — Scale Examples of Convergence

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch06.md`._

Chapter 6 teaches scale-based convergence techniques for melodic improvisation, demonstrating how specific scales -- Jazz Melodic Minor, Dorian, Major #4/#5, Blues, and Diminished -- function as bridges into a target tonality (F major) through embedded tritone voice-leading. Martino's characteristic moves include identifying the tritone pair within each scale as the harmonic engine of resolution, recognizing structural overlaps (like the last tetrachord of Jazz Melodic Minor matching F major), and establishing substitution equivalences (such as the Major #4/#5 scale interchanging with G Jazz Melodic Minor). The chapter introduces load-bearing concepts: tritone as the core convergence mechanism, the idea of 'modal starting points within the target key' as valid vehicles for arrival, and the principle of transposing this entire system to all 12 chromatic keys while preserving the underlying voice-leading logic. The pedagogical stance -- 'increase consciousness' rather than dictate -- frames these scales as raw materials the musician shapes to suit musical intention.
