# Chapter 7 — Chord Examples of Convergence

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch07.md`._

This chapter teaches convergence of character chords across multiple scales, showing how tritone-related dominant and altered chords (C7b5, C7b9, Bb7b5, Gb7b5, and their variants) resolve to FA7 and its extensions (FA79, FA679) as a unified resolution target. The master's characteristic moves visible here are the systematic mapping of each scale to its native character-chord palette, the principle that multiple non-identical chords can substitute for one another when they share the same tritone interval and resolution target, and the demonstration that this convergence system works regardless of chord root (generalizing beyond C-centric examples). Key terminology introduced includes tritone substitution (interchangeability of tritone-related dominants), character chords (scale-derived altered and extended dominants), and the resolution formula itself -- FA7 and its extended voicings as the destination toward which all tritone-based approaches converge.
