# Chapter 8 — Scale-Chord mixed Convergence Examples

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch08.md`._

Chapter 8 presents mixed convergence examples showing how specific scales target particular chords through tritone substitution and altered dominant relationships, establishing the chord-scale associations that bind together Martino's scale taxonomy. The master's characteristic approach is visible in the precise mapping of scales to their harmonic destinations -- Scale #23 converges to tritone-resolved voicings like Gb7b5 and C7b9b5, while Scale #24 targets minor-major 7ths and augmented dominants -- demonstrating how scale choice follows from harmonic function rather than mode alone. Key terminology introduced includes the convergence concept itself (which formalizes when a given scale resolves to a target chord), the diminished scale's dual role in both Fi7 and FA7 contexts, and the Lydian-augmented variant (major with raised 4th and 5th) as a named scale type. The chapter also establishes a theoretical relationship between the Lydian scale (major with raised 4th) and the whole-tone scale, anchoring how scale families relate within the system. Together, these examples show how jazz melodic minor, diminished, and raised-scale variants serve as convergence sources for the altered and extended dominant chords that define modern jazz harmony.
