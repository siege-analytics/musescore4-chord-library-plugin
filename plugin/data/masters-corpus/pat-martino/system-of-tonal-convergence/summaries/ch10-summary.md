# Chapter 10 — Convergence on the Seventh Chord, Minor Chord

_Distillation; backed by `chapters/ch10.md`._

This chapter teaches the convergence system -- a method for approaching any target chord by substituting it with multiple alternative chords bound together by a shared tritone interval. Martino's characteristic move is to take a single geometric property (the tritone) and generate a palette of tension-laden substitutes from it, shown here through the worked example of converging on Eb, which yields 21 possible approach chords: the dominant-seventh Bb7, its tritone-substitute E7 variants, the diminished chord sharing the same tritone, and augmented dominants. The chapter introduces load-bearing terminology including convergence (approaching a main chord through other chords), tritone substitution (swapping Bb7 for E7 because they share the tritone), the Circle of Gravities (a framework where three interlocking tritone pairs govern all convergent motion toward a tonal center), and the chromatic resolution rule for scalar convergence -- targeting the tritone's defining tones (7th and 3rd) to resolve chromatically into a dominant chord. The system extends to minor keys through tritone relationships (like G-Db governing F minor) and usage restrictions, such as constraining scales with major thirds to embellish the fifth rather than the root of a minor chord.
